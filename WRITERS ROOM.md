# The Green Gallows: Final Writers' Room

Oct 14, 2026. The last pass before the pitch.

**Who was in the room.** Six chairs, each with one job:
- **The Showrunner:** does the season hold together, and does page one sell it?
- **The Editor:** what can go?
- **The Fan:** does it give me chills, and do I press "next episode"?
- **The Skeptic:** the exec who asks "why doesn't she just…"
- **The Historian:** 1889 London, and the pedant in the back row.
- **The Actor:** does every character sound like themselves and want something?

**Two cold reads.** Two readers who hadn't seen the drafts read the full bible:
- one checked every cross-reference against the scenes;
- one read as the harshest development executive in town.

Their findings were argued through in the room below.

---

## The arguments, and how they were settled

### 1. "Page one reads like a script consultant's notes."

**Skeptic:** The first thing a buyer reads is a dateline listing "clox, promise, tactics, cut and aura passes", then "Kept from your outline", "your colleague's" template and "the rule from the aura pass". Those are our process, not the show.

**Showrunner:** Agreed. The bible stays a bible, but the front door is a pitch.

**Verdict:**
- **The pitch now opens on a logline, the format, and why the show lands now.** The why-now: *a show about energy and who pays for it*.
- **The development history moves to the back,** with the sources, under *Development notes*.
- **Every "you", "colleague" and pass name is gone.**
- **The 14 italic *Therefore* / *But* markers inside scenes are gone.** They were scaffolding, and the scenes still run on "therefore" without the labels.

### 2. "Parliament can't sit in November 1889."

**Historian:** It was prorogued from August 1889 to February 1890. That breaks four things:
- the Charter hearing in a committee room (Ep 4);
- the vote (Ep 6);
- "The House sits at two" (Ep 10);
- reading the record into Hansard.

On top of that, a Lord Chancellor doesn't chair a Commons committee.

**Showrunner:** That's the one that makes us look careless. What's the fix that costs nothing?

**Historian:** Royal charters are Privy Council business, and the Lord Chancellor sits on the Council. The Crown's own record is the London Gazette, and it prints extraordinary editions.

**Verdict:**
- **The hearing and the vote move to a Privy Council chamber in Whitehall,** held in public because of the market deaths, with Vane presiding.
- **The Westminster ultimatum becomes "printed in the Gazette, under the Crown's own seal".**
- **The Record ends in a Gazette Extraordinary on every newsstand.**

It plays better, too. The Crown is forced to publish its own sin under its own seal.

### 3. "Who is Lady Harding, exactly?"

**Historian:** A second son's wife is Mrs Harding.

**Verdict:**
- **Eldgard is now the fifth baronet.** He dies before midnight, so by dawn Nathaniel is Sir Nathaniel and Tamara is Lady Harding by right. The Westminster scene says so in a clause: "Lady Harding since her father-in-law died at midnight."
- **Lambrick is styled correctly:** Hector, Lord Lambrick.

### 4. The Historian's other catches

All are fixed, at zero runtime cost:
- **"Daisy Bell" is from 1892.** The Pavilion now sings "Two Lovely Black Eyes" (1886), which is funnier under wolves anyway.
- **"Little Venice" is a 20th-century name.** It's now the frozen Regent's Canal at Paddington.
- **Newgate had no debtors' wing in 1889, and didn't keep lunatics for decades.** Marsh is now held off the books on a Crown warrant in a wing Newgate shut years ago. That makes the Crown's secret darker.
- **Seventeenth-century Lord Chancellors:** the Great Seal was in commission in 1689. It's now "every keeper of the Great Seal since", and Vane *countersigned* the annex.
- **The wedding law:**
  - a reception order needs a relative and two doctors, not a Chancery seal;
  - voiding the marriage would be a nullity suit, not a Chancery hearing;
  - Lambrick now stands *a breath too late*, after the priest's "if any man knows cause" has passed, so the priest has no legal reason to stop.
- **"Tyburn Road" has been Oxford Street on the maps since the 1700s.** One line under *Places* says London still calls the stretch by the gallows field by its old name. The name stays because it's the right name for this show.
- **"Marshals" is an American term.** They're the Crown's officers now.
- **"Christmas crystal market" on 9 November** is now a winter crystal market.
- **Three thousand Hargraves hanged on a hundred frames** is now thirty.

### 5. The Skeptic's logic holes

Each was fixed in a clause, unless noted:
- **The two 1489 cold opens contradicted each other.** Edric went down the stair an hour before the pilot showed him holding up the first light. Now it's the same night: Alistair makes the bargain, cuts the net, opens the wrist, and at dawn the boy carries *the first light* down alone.
- **"You said Parliament."** Alice was quoting a threat she never heard. Now Nathaniel tells her himself, can't finish it, and she understands his mother has already bought this too.
- **"I came for your ring."** How did he know about it? At 5.16, "Nobody looks at the ring *but Nathaniel*."
- **Nathaniel refitted every Guard rifle. Why didn't he read the discharge logs?** They're sealed into the cases, and only a captain may break the seal.
- **The Lost One knew Nixon had been told "fever".** She couldn't have. Now: "Your mother's name is in the song. Whatever they told you she died of, she died down there." The song now carries the names of the Hargraves who died waiting in the cellar, as well as those fed to the chain.
- **Who opened Jamie's coffin for the arrest?** Only Nathaniel and Eldgard knew it held slag. Now the Crown opened it at dawn.
- **Nathaniel ignores Nixon's whistle for hours.** He couldn't hear it: the White was roaring up through the broken floor between them and the stair until the storm moved up the hill.
- **The rule said "four rules, and nothing else".** But grief cracks the bridge, Morgana makes the snow, and the Frost Witch shuts the White. Now:
  - the line reads "Four rules run the season";
  - *The White* adds one sentence: where something has crossed, the wall stays thin, and grief on either side can crack it;
  - Rule 3 says the price is one of the house "by blood or by name". That makes Josephine's "I'm a Harding. I chose the name" literally true under the rules.
- **"The only other Harding on this hill"** was wrong with Nathaniel and Josephine both standing there. It's now "a Harding nobody else on this hill knows about".
- **How did Ajax know about the coach for two?** "A man pays double, I follow him home."
- **Other small fixes:**
  - "Jamie's key" is Byron's key;
  - the boys made their whistle "as boys", not "at nine", since Nixon is two years older;
  - Byron's ribbon is twenty years old in 1859, not fifty;
  - the core and the shaft are both four hundred feet down;
  - "He dies" now reads "Eldgard dies";
  - Josephine no longer has two lines in a row with Grise;
  - the Lost One's own memory isn't one the cold can burn;
  - "Verdance line" was never defined, and is now a gallowglass line.

### 6. The cross-reference read

**The Editor's case:** A bible that misquotes its own scenes reads as unfinished. The reader found 19 cases. The worst: Cole's "Today, sir" is cited three times but never said on screen. What Cole actually says is "Tell Mr. Harding, sir, or I will", and Nixon says "Cole says today." The rest were similar:
- misquoted lines ("She holds her wrist" for "That girl holds her wrist");
- a Season map tag that didn't match the episode;
- payoffs cited in the wrong scene;
- a Season 2 note that had Tamara holding Nixon's hand, when the Record says she doesn't;
- a Season 2 note that put the women's terms on a Westminster bench, when they shake on them at a depot gate.

**Verdict:** All fixed against the scene text. The counting game's ledger row now cites the market stall. The FROST KILLER bills that a constable takes down in the Record are now pasted up after the hearing in Ep 4, so the image has a plant.

### 7. What the room argued for and kept

These were challenged, and stay:
- **Alice nods knowing her sister is being taken (8.11).**
  - *The Fan:* it's too cruel, and we'll lose her.
  - *The Actor:* it's the most Alice thing in the season. Her sister chose, and Alice chooses who gets saved on the way, then says so to his face in 9.2.
  - **Kept.**
- **The convergence in the pilot (1.12).**
  - *The Skeptic:* four cutaways in a premiere might confuse.
  - *The Editor:* it's fifteen seconds and reads as atmosphere until Ep 4 turns it into a reveal.
  - **Kept.**
- ***Why this isn't Otty again,*** the note in Ep 10.
  - *The Skeptic* wanted it cut as defensive.
  - *The Showrunner:* it answers the first question a buyer asks about the ending. Better to answer it on the page than in the room.
  - **Kept.**
- **St Bride's and the Harding vault.**
  - *The Historian:* the Burial Acts closed City churchyards in the 1850s.
  - *The Showrunner:* an old family vault by licence is defensible, and the brass plate on the chancel wall is a season image.
  - **Kept, and it's on the cheat sheet below.**
- **Gin Lane** is Hogarth's name, not an 1889 street. It's a deliberate fantasy London name, like the Works at Tyburn. **Kept, and on the cheat sheet.**

### 8. The runtime check

Everything above paid for itself:
- **Ep 7** gained one Grise line and lost a redundant sentence in 7.14.
- **Ep 8** comes out about 25 seconds shorter.
- **Eps 9 and 10** are unchanged.
- **Eps 2 and 4** spent a few seconds of spare time.

## The cheat sheet: questions you'll get in the room

| They ask | You say |
| --- | --- |
| Why doesn't Eldgard walk down the stair himself? | That's the confession, and he says it in Ep 9: "I could have gone down that stair myself. I raised a boy for it instead." Every Keeper chose sons. That's the show |
| Why not just turn off the light? | Because four million people are warm. Nathaniel does turn it off, and becomes the most hated man in London. The season is the price of that choice |
| What are the magic's rules? | Four, each one sentence: the Tithe; Harding blood is the key; breaking it costs light, a night of the White, and one of the house by blood or by name, given, never taken; the cold costs her Miriam's memories. Nothing important is solved by a rule the audience hasn't seen |
| Is Morgana the villain? | She's the victim the city runs on. The villains are arithmetic: Eldgard's, Lambrick's and the Crown's |
| Why 1889? | Fifty years after Otty, the last Harding to pay. It's the gaslight era with the gas replaced by a woman's blood, and 1889 is the year of the Great Dock Strike, when London's workers made the city ask who pays for its comfort |
| Is this real history? | Real London, real institutions (Privy Council, Gazette, Bethlem, Newgate), fantasy industry. Gin Lane and Tyburn Road are deliberate old names in a city that kept them |
| What's the Season 2 engine? | A London with no light, run on Tamara's coal; a Harding child carried by a daughter of the White; Josephine in the White beside a son who can't answer. Everyone the chain took is kept, not dead: what do you owe the dead when you know exactly where they are? |
| Can we afford it? | Morgana is practical from the waist up, and her full height is saved for three episodes. The wolves exist only in strobe frames. Ep 4 and Ep 7's Pavilion are contained, and they pay for Newgate and Tyburn hill |
| How long are the episodes? | About 48 minutes standard, about 52 for the premiere, about 58 for the two tent-poles, and a two-part finale over one night. Runtimes are measured scene by scene, not guessed |
| Comps? | The dread of *Chernobyl*, the sisters of *Arcane*, the drawing rooms of *House of Cards*, with whimsy breathing between the violence |

**Status:** ready to pitch.
