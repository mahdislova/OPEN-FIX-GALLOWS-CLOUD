**Numbering.** I use the document's own numbering. **10.19** means Episode 10, scene 19. "Rec." is the Record montage (10.20).

**How the runtime was measured.** The document's own rule says each episode runs about 44 minutes. I used Episodes 1–4, the least-revised episodes, to set a rate of about 17.6 seconds per beat: one sentence of action is one beat, a line of quoted dialogue counts as half, and each montage row counts as two and a half. I then applied that rate to every scene. The result is the **top** of a range. The bottom, at 0.8× the top, allows for the estimator over-pricing long dialogue scenes and for writer's commentary that never reaches the screen. Commentary turned out to be only 3–7% of each episode, so the later episodes really do hold more material, not just more words.

**Verdict.** The season is about **20–25% too long, and the overload sits in its last four episodes.** Under the document's own 44-minute rule, Episodes 7–10 run 53–106 minutes. That isn't something trimming can fix, and the overload predates our passes: in the f7b373e draft, Episodes 7–10 already ran an estimated 66–94 minutes. The three passes added roughly 4 to 12 minutes to each of Episodes 5–10.

Two things are true at once:

1. **There's real clutter to cut**, about 22 minutes of it. Most of it is in Episode 10's coda, where Westminster alone carries 12 payoffs. The rest is in duplicated arguments and in Cole, who carries more threads than any other secondary character.
2. **Even after the cuts, Episodes 7–10 need more room than a network hour gives.** Getting them to 44 minutes would mean cutting spine. So, as this pass's rules require, I'm proposing a tier change instead (below), and holding everything else to it.

## Step 0: The Runtime Budget

### The one question

**Network hour or streaming?** The document's rule is "about 44 minutes" for every episode. That's a network hour, and it can't hold this back half without cutting spine. My preferred answer, for a show pitched on *Chernobyl* and *House of Cards*, is a streaming model with tiers:

| Tier | Target | Band | Episodes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Premiere | 52 | 46–58 | 1 |
| Standard | 48 | 44–54 | 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 |
| Tent-pole | 58 | 52–64 | 7, 8 |
| Finale, part one | 60 | 54–66 | 9 |
| Event finale | 78 | 68–88 | 10 |

Two tier moves from the document's current rule:
- **Episode 5 loses its tent-pole status.** It fits a standard slot comfortably, at 36–45 minutes.
- **Episode 7 gains tent-pole status.** It's the double reveal plus the wolves set piece, and it needs the room.

Episodes 9 and 10 become a two-part finale. Every minute of Episode 10 past 60 is a set piece (the dark city, Tyburn hill) or a goodbye (Josephine, dawn, the Record). None of it is plot.
