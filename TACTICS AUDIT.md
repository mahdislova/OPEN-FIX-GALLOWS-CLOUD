# The Green Gallows: Tactics Audit

Audited: `FULL NEW SEASON.md` after the clox and promise passes (Oct 14, 2026).

**Numbering.** I use the document's own numbering. **2.13** means Episode 2, scene 13. **CO** is a cold open.

**What this pass protects.** A pure Mamet pass would flatten the scenes that make this season what it is. So scenes that work as image, ritual or counterpoint are marked **HELD** rather than forced into a fight. The cold opens, the rehearsal wedding, the oath, the Record and the dark city are among them. A held scene is excused from "who wants what" only if:
- it turns the audience;
- it carries a second layer under the literal action;
- it's short enough that nobody asks what it's for.

The Godfather's baptism is the model: no negotiation in it, and it's still the climax. Twelve scenes pass those tests. None of them needs touching.

**Verdict.** This is an unusually well-engined season. Of 190 scenes, **170 turn on the page, 12 are held, and only 8 don't turn.** The outline was built on "every scene turns something," and it shows. The problems are narrow and specific:

1. **Paraphrase is hiding three fights:**
   - "a standoff in the dark ends with the truth, almost" (2.13);
   - "every line is polite and every line is a knife" (5.7);
   - "Lambrick argues… Eldgard is calm" (4.13).

   The first is the scene where the season's central romance actually begins, and nobody has written the fight.
2. **Two big negotiations fold too fast:**
   - Vane gives in to Tamara's coal threat on his first reply (10.19).
   - In the Snow Confession, Eldgard volunteers half of what should be dragged out of him (9.15).
3. **Seven lines say out loud what the subtext already said.** Most of them are the last sentence of an otherwise great line.

## 2. The Tactics Map

The ten most important two-handers, beat by beat. Each beat shows a tactic, then the counter, then who is winning.

**1.13: Nathaniel and Alice, the match.** He asks ("Do you hear that?"); she deflects with a joke ("Half the room is") → Alice. He demands a name; she prices it ("the only one your house pays for") → Alice. He asks for one free; she defers ("when the lights come back on") → Alice. She congratulates him and robs him → Alice. **The model scene of the season.** The loser never fights back, and that's the point: he's charmed. Don't touch it.

**2.13: Nathaniel and Alice, Jamie's bedroom.** As written: "a standoff in the dark ends with the truth, almost." **There are no beats on the page.** It's the first time these two choose each other, and it's the only scene of theirs nobody wrote. → #1.

**4.9: the crane.** Ajax laughs (bravado) → Ajax. He sells information for his life ("Your brother paid me"; "Ask the Guard who fired") → Ajax. Alice joins his side ("Pull him up. He's the only witness we have") → Ajax. Nathaniel, alone with his grief, lets go → Nathaniel, at a cost. **It works.** Every tactic is answered, and the winner pays.

**5.20: Eldgard and Nathaniel, after.** Eldgard deflects ("She tells every Harding whatever will bring him to the lock") → Eldgard. He shows his wound ("She told me my sister was still singing") → Eldgard. He pleads ("I won't lose two sons") → Eldgard. He recruits ("Help me find another way") → Eldgard wins: "yes." Nathaniel never fights back, and that's correct. He wants to believe. The question he filed at 5.16 is the fight he's saving.

**7.3: Alice and the Lost One.** Alice confronts her with the letter → Lost One admits it ("Yes"). Alice accuses ("You left me under that bed") → Lost One kneels and apologizes → Lost One. Alice tests her ("Say my name") → nothing comes → Alice wins the truth. Lost One confesses love → Alice expels her ("Get out"). Both switch tactic three times, and the turn is caused by a test. **The season's best two-hander.**

**7.7: the roof.** Nixon confesses → Nathaniel hits him → Nixon takes it. Nathaniel asks "Was Jamie alive when you left?" → "Yes" → Nathaniel walks. One move each, by design. The scene is a wound, not a fight, and it's short enough to get away with it. Keep.

**8.6: the vestry.** Tamara offers ("Say one word") → Nixon refuses ("I'm on Eldgard's books. Like the coal") → Nixon. Tamara reframes ("Then hand it to me properly") → Tamara wins, by turning his refusal into a ritual he can keep. Only one fix: Nixon's refusal explains itself one sentence too long (see §4).

**9.2: Nathaniel and Alice, the glasshouse.** He blames her ("while we were saying it they took her") → she defends ("She'd have been in Bethlem by supper") → even. He wounds ("On a bill") → she doesn't flinch → Nathaniel. She gives the whole truth → Alice. He reaches across the bench; she stands ("Midnight. The core.") → Alice. **It works.** Alice's speech is long but earned (see §4).

**9.14–15: the Snow Confession.** Nathaniel re-asks the dressing-room question ("Did you hold his hand?"), a good tactic, and gets "Over the lock" → Nathaniel. He presses with the slag → Nathaniel. Then **Nixon says only "Marian" and "I was,"** and Eldgard volunteers the rest. One side has no tactic. → #3.

**10.19: Westminster.** Vane denies ("The Crown doesn't keep that kind of record") → Nathaniel: "It's in your desk" → Vane is unmoved. Vane bribes (the captain's lantern) → Nixon leaves it on the desk. Tamara threatens the coal → **Vane folds on his next line.** The obstacle gives way at the first real push, so the season's last negotiation has no second half. → #2.
