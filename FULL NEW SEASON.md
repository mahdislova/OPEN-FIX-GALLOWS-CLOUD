# The Green Gallows — The Showrunner's Season

Oct 3, 2026 · @patrick · revised Oct 10, 2026 · clox, promise, tactics, cut and aura passes Oct 14, 2026

A new ten-episode season, built from scratch on the strongest bones of the original: the Harding legacy, Miriam and the Lost One, and the sister reveal.

## The pitch

London, November 1889, and the whole city glows green. Every lamp, tram and furnace burns gallowglass, a crystal that only House Harding knows how to grow, at the Harding Works on the old gallows field at Tyburn. The Hardings say it grows from iron in the ground. In fact it grows from a woman. For four hundred years they have kept a creature from the frozen world beyond, the White, chained under the Works, and they bleed her for light. Every fifty years, at the first snow, the chain that holds her is fed with a Harding life. The last was Eldgard's own older sister. The next is due this winter, and the son raised to pay it is about to die.

**Series engine.** London runs on a secret. Every faction wants to own the secret, the girl at its centre or the boy who will inherit it. Every week, one plan for the light runs into another.

**Season question.** Can Nathaniel Harding end the bargain that lights London without becoming the father who kept it? Under it runs the sisters' question: will Alice Bounds let the thing wearing her dead sister's face choose its own life?

**Theme.** Protection as control. Every adult in this show hurts someone to keep them safe. The young ones have to learn whether love can let go. Five more themes run underneath it: bought or given, who pays for the light, what gets written down, the spare and the heir, and where you belong. The season argues every one of them from several sides and never says which side is right.

**Tone.** The dread of *Chernobyl*, the sisters of *Arcane*, *House of Cards* in the drawing rooms. Whimsy breathes between the violence: a creature learning what an orange is, a recruit tangled in laundry lines, two engaged friends who plan to be very polite to each other for forty years.

**The clock.** Every episode opens on a card: 49 DAYS TO FIRST SNOW. In four hundred years the first snow over Tyburn has always come on the longest night, 21 December, so that's the day everyone counts to. At the end of Episode 8 it comes two weeks early, because the prisoner makes it fall, and the last two episodes play out over one night.

**Kept from your outline:** the Harding legacy and the 1489 massacre at the Gallows; Miriam's death and the Lost One; the sister reveal ("You're not her"); Josephine's protection turning into control; Eldgard's Snow Confession; Nixon's Hargrave blood; Tamara the operative; the Vykra; Ajax; Miriam's red mittens.

**Cut:** Edmund, Virgil, the Chapel, the Duke, the Prime Minister, Trevane. Each one did a job someone else now does, or existed only to deliver information. Morgana moves from a nun's body in the Chapel to the chain under the Works, so the villain's secret and the light's secret become the same secret.

**New:** Mother Grise, who runs the Vykra; Lord Chancellor Vane, for the Crown; and the Royal Lantern Guard as a full corps with its own gear, ranks and oath.

## The craft rules this season obeys

Ten rules: eight taken from writers you named, the ninth from the clox pass, the tenth from the aura pass, and each one checked against every episode below.

1. **Therefore and but, never "and then."** Each scene is caused by the last one or cuts across it. Parker and Stone's test: if the honest word between two beats is "and then," one of them goes. ([Perell](https://perell.com/note/but-therefore-rule/))
2. **Secrets with long fuses (Brent Weeks).** Weeks plots the character arcs and the big external climaxes, then layers in secrets he pays off books later. Here each major secret has three layers: what the public believes, what the audience learns, and what the character learns last, when it stings most. Everything serves his one rule: keep people turning pages. ([Reactor, Weeks AMA](https://reactormag.com/highlights-from-brent-weeks-r-fantasy-ama/))
3. **Plans collide.** Most set pieces happen because three or four characters have separate plans for the same night. Nobody's plan works as drawn. The explosion, the heist and the wolves in the streets all come out of choices, never coincidence.
4. **Sisters only trauma can split (Arcane).** Alex Yee: the sisters had to be "so emotionally entwined that only an event that traumatic" could drive a wedge between them. Alice and the Lost One get three episodes of closeness before the first wound and four before the break. ([SYFY, Alex Yee](https://www.syfy.com/syfy-wire/arcane-co-creator-alex-yee-unpacks-the-act-i-twists?amp))
5. **Power lives inside intimate relationships (Willimon).** "Politics itself doesn't interest me. It's power that interests me." Every marriage, friendship and parent-child pair here is also a power arrangement, shown through behaviour and hands more than speeches. ([BFI notes](https://script-consultant.co.uk/bfi-beau-willimon-interview-notes-pt1/))
6. **One vision per episode, on a streaming clock.** Each episode has one focus you could put on its poster (the ball, the morgue, the hunt, the hearing, the heist), and every scene turns something. Runtime sets the budget, and scene counts follow it. The premiere runs about 52 minutes (46–58). Standard episodes, 2 through 6, run about 48 (44–54). The two tent-poles, Ep 7 (the two reveals and the wolves) and Ep 8 (the wedding), run about 58 (52–64), because their set pieces need the room. The finale is a two-parter: Ep 9 about 60 (54–66), and Ep 10 an event finale of about 78 (68–88), where every minute past the hour is a set piece or a goodbye, never plot. Scenes that only carry information fold into scenes that carry feeling, and no scene pays off more than two threads, three at a climax.
7. **The grid.** Your colleague's Want / Obstacle / Solution / Twist table runs for the four or five characters who carry each episode. The others rest and come back with their own stories later.
8. **Whimsy earns the grim, and every ending itches.** Each episode alternates a light beat with a dark one, gives at least one feeling that goes unsaid, and gives one young character a chance to prove they belong. No act runs without a breath, and no stretch of grim runs much past ten minutes. The darkest stretch gets its breath placed right before the blow, so the laugh is still in the audience's throat when the blow lands. Every episode lets its audience go on a question they could say out loud at the cut to black: a reveal, a turn, an escalation, a quiet ache, the season's one cliffhanger (Ep 9), or, twice, a warm close with dread on the horizon (Eps 3 and 7). Never more than two endings of one kind in a row.
9. **Every lead holds one thing shut (the clox pass).** Each lead keeps a truth, a wound, a want or a debt locked. The plot squeezes it, and when it breaks it breaks in a particular room, in front of particular people, and something changes that can't change back. A feeling that never alters a decision, a tactic or a reveal is decoration, and gets cut or wired in. Nobody announces a feeling: a lock shows in what a character avoids, overdoes or protects. Arcane's test: the whole rupture between Vi and Powder lives in a slap and three words, "You're a jinx," not in a conversation about it. See *What each of them is holding shut*, below.
10. **Out of nowhere, never from nowhere (the aura pass).** Every lead gets one moment the fans will clip, and it's paid for: we heard about the power or the nerve before we see it, we know what using it costs, it costs exactly that, and someone whose view of them changes is watching. The cooler the moment, the fewer the words. The season's biggest turns run on six sequence engines (see *The sequence engines*, below), and every one of them puts the clue on screen before the blow lands, so a second viewing finds it waiting. A surprise without a plant is a cheat. A plant without a surprise is homework.

## The world and its four rules

Gallowglass is Morgana's frozen blood, and an iron chain under the Works holds her. Four rules run the season, and nothing else does. A viewer should be able to say each one in a sentence.

1. **The Tithe.** Every fifty years, on the night of the first snow, a Harding must walk down to the chain of their own free will before midnight, or the chain breaks.
2. **Harding blood is the key.** Only Harding blood can hold the chain, mend it or open it, and her cold can't touch it. That's how Eldgard mends the crack Jamie cut, why the Lost One can take Nathaniel's hand without freezing him, and why Nixon, a Hargrave, is Harding blood too.
3. **Breaking it has a price.** If the chain breaks, she goes free, the light goes out for good, the White comes through until dawn, and one of the house that chained her goes with her into the White. It has to be given. She won't take it.
4. **The Lost One's cold costs her Miriam.** The cold comes out of her when she feels too much or when she chooses to use it, and it kills by touch. Every time it comes out, it burns one of Miriam's memories. While it's in her, it holds Miriam's body exactly as it was on the bridge.

Everything else in the season is people, not rules.

**The three layers of the secret**

| Layer | Who believes it | What it says |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Public | London | Gallowglass grows from iron under Tyburn. In 1489 the Hargrave rebels were hanged there for treason. |
| Second | Glim addicts, old Guardsmen, the Vykra | The light hums with a woman crying. People call it the Weeping and say it's the hanged. |
| Truth | Eldgard, later Nathaniel | Morgana, a mother from the White, has been chained under the Works since 1489. The Hargraves were a Harding cadet branch, hanged by their own cousins for trying to free her. Her daughter ran into the snow. |

**What gallowglass does**

- Burned in lamps and furnaces, it gives a green-white light and steady heat. The Ring, London's lamp grid, carries it under every street.
- Ground to dust it becomes **glim**, the undercity's drug. Users hear the Weeping, which is the first clue, given in Episode 1. It is Morgana's lullaby to her daughter, and London has sung it for four hundred years as the Lantern Hymn, with new words nailed over the old tune. Her daughter can hear what nobody else can under it: the names of every life the house fed her.

**The Royal Lantern Guard.** Chartered in 1689 to protect the Ring, they are London's elite, armed by the Harding Works. Oath: *Light the dark. Keep the flame.* Ranks run Wick (recruit), Flame (trooper), Lantern-Captain.

- **Lantern gauntlet:** a forearm rig with a gallowglass core. It fires a **flash-burst**, a blinding strobe that drops a man and sends wolves of the White running.
- **Leap-boosters:** steam and crystal thrust packs for rooftop flight, thirty seconds per charge. When the charge runs out, you fall.
- **Tether-hook:** a grapple line for swings and quick climbs.
- **Quench-blade:** a glass sabre that drinks heat, built for fast takedowns. One touch numbs a limb; a long cut freezes the blood.
- **Lantern rifle:** fires crystal rounds that burst into light. Every discharge goes into a log, bound into the rifle's case and audited whenever the rifles go back to the Works. That log matters in Episodes 4 and 10.

**Undercity tech.** **Ghostlights** are bootleg gauntlets, unstable and loud. Alice builds hers from parts stolen out of Nathaniel's shop. **Ajax's Black Gauntlet** is Nathaniel's own abandoned prototype, stolen two years ago, which hits like a piston.

**The Vykra.** A guild of undercity women, spies and knives for hire, run by Mother Grise from a Shadwell laundry. Their mark is a knot stitched inside the glove. Their poison, **winter's kiss**, is distilled from gallowglass and kills with frost on the lips, so it looks like the Frost Killer's work.

**The White.** A world of snow, wind and wolves on the far side of the chain. Time stops there. Whatever this side sends into it is kept exactly as it went in, and doesn't come back.

**Places.** Harding House (Mayfair) and its glasshouse. The Harding Works over Tyburn, with the core deep below. Gin Lane tenements. Blackwood Bridge. The Shadwell laundry. Lambrick House and the Pimlico gasworks, where the Tyburn conduit (the old Works drain) runs under the city into the Thames. The Lantern Guard tower at Charing Cross. Parliament. Newgate.

**Why now.** 1839 plus fifty is 1889: a Tithe year, and the son raised to pay it dies seven weeks before it's due.

**Otty, in the season.** She is never seen. She appears five times: the brass on the chancel wall in Ep 2; Byron's hand going to his pocket when Nathaniel asks who she was, and Eldgard's "She told me my sister was still singing," both in Ep 5; the blue ribbon in Byron's fist in Ep 6; and Eldgard's confession in Ep 9. By then the audience knows what Eldgard's "I raised Jamie for it" is a copy of.

**The Orchard.** Under the Works, thirteen steps below the coal-hatch stair, is a gallery of bunks and iron rings where Hargraves were kept alive for four hundred years: fed well, kept healthy, and taught from the cradle that walking down the stair was holy. Every fifty years one of them did. They were the cattle in the contract. Hardings are taught never to say the word upstairs. The first name on its wall is Marian, who led the raid in 1489, and the last is Marian too, Nixon's mother: the Orchard named its girls after the first one who tried. The keepers counted the children in chalk tallies headed APPLES. By 1889 the herd is gone. Marsh's rhyme counts the seven who were left when he ran, and Nixon is the only Hargrave anyone ever brought out into the light.

**Tobias Marsh.** An Orchard man who ran in 1867 and was caught. The Crown holds him in Newgate as a madman, cell 1417, and nobody listens to what he sings. Jamie Harding visited him every Sunday for two years and learned the way down. The diagram in Jamie's boot-tree in Ep 2 shows the chain, and beside it the Orchard: a stair of thirteen steps going down from a hatch marked coal. That is why Jamie went to the Works with a plan in Ep 1, and why Nixon, tracing a dead boy's footsteps without knowing it, ends up in the Orchard in Ep 8.

**How Nixon learns it.**

1. Ep 3: his blood test glows green, and Eldgard pats his head: "Still good." Felix gives him the Newgate slip.
2. Ep 4: Marsh pinches his cheek, "Marian's chin!", and sings the rhyme. Nixon walks away not understanding it.
3. Ep 6: cornered in the gas main, the Lost One uses it on him to make him let go of her wrist: "They told you your mother had a fever. She didn't." It works.
4. Ep 7 and 8: Tamara gives him the file, which she has already read, and he carries it a week before he opens it on the morning of the wedding: "Reserve. Blood suitable." He learns he is the spare, not what the spare is for.
5. Ep 8: he follows the rhyme down thirteen steps, and finds the Orchard and his mother's name. This is the scene that turns him.
6. Ep 9: he frees Marsh in the Newgate break-out, and on the last roof, with Eldgard on his back and the charge dying, he says his mother's name and doesn't let go. Hours later Eldgard answers him in the Snow Confession, including what the spare was grown to do.
7. Ep 10: he offers himself with his eyes open. "They grew me for this the way they grew my mother."

### The wound under the sisters

When Alice and Miriam were small, their stepfather drank and kept a belt. Miriam, four years older, made Alice a promise in the dark under their bed: *nothing will ever touch you while I'm here.* Then the belt came out, and Miriam didn't move. She never once moved. What she did was teach Alice to count: the posts of the fence outside the window, one to eight, over and over, until it was over. "Eight posts and he's done." That's the counting game. The sisters have played it as a joke for fifteen years, and neither of them has ever said where it came from. The stepfather went into the river one winter, drunk, and nobody looked for him.

Alice keeps all of it **behind the eightfold fence**, her own phrase, the one she uses when anyone gets close: *that's behind the fence.* It's the root of her lie. She learned that love is holding on, because nobody held the line for her.

Miriam carried it differently: guilt, all of it, never said. When the Lost One comes through on the bridge and puts Miriam on like a coat, it gets the guilt with the memories. A creature that has never felt anything human now has fifteen years of a sister's shame, and no idea what to do with it.

**How the show handles it.** The abuse is never shown. It lives in sound (a buckle, a floorboard), in Alice's flinch, and in the counting. It's about what it did to two girls, not about the man.

**The irony at the heart of the sisters' story.** Miriam knew she owed Alice an apology and never gave it. The Lost One isn't Miriam, but she gives it, and that's how Alice knows her sister is dead.

### How the season stays producible

Morgana is a practical performer in prosthetics from the waist up, with CG for the ice and fire and the full nine feet saved for Eps 5, 8 and 10. The pilot shows only her hand. The wolves are shown the way the flash-bursts show everything: one strobe frame at a time, so we render frames, not sequences, and the audience's imagination does the rest. Ep 4 (a hearing room and a dock) and Ep 7's Pavilion (one interior, a hundred extras and a song) are the season's contained episodes, which pays for the Newgate break-out and Tyburn hill.

### The sequence engines

Six machines for making a turn feel as if it came out of nowhere when it was standing in the frame the whole time. Each one names what has to be on screen, and when. Where an engine was already running in the season before the aura pass, it's listed too, so nobody breaks it by accident.

## Episode 1 — "The Engagement"

**49 DAYS TO FIRST SNOW.** 2 November 1889. **Focus:** one night, two parties: the Harding ball above, the glim run below, until the light flickers. **Set piece:** the Works blackout. **Sting:** the man who shoots the saboteur is the hero's best friend, and the saboteur is the hero's brother.

*Logline:* On the night his engagement is announced, Nathaniel Harding's brother dies at the family Works, his best friend fires the shot nobody can ever know about, and across the river a girl freezes solid on a bridge, then opens her eyes in the morgue.

| Character | Want | Obstacle | Solution | Twist |

| Nathaniel | To show his father the leap-gauntlet and earn a place in the work | Eldgard sees only Jamie. Inside, Nathaniel believes he's the spare | Demonstrates it at the ball anyway | Humiliated in public, then his brother is dead by morning |
| Alice | To pass her first Vykra test and to find out who Miriam keeps sneaking off to | The contract is a Harding. Her sister is lying to her | Gets into the ball as a lamp-girl | Finds Miriam frozen on the bridge, then sees her wake up |

### Scenes

**Cold open**

1. **Tyburn, 1489. Night, no snow, but frost.** The Crown's gallows field, a hundred empty frames and one old thief turning on his rope. *Creak. Creak.* Below the ground, in a pit lined with iron, something vast breathes behind the lantern glare. We never see her face. We see a hand of blue ice the size of a cartwheel with an iron hook through it, and we hear her singing: a wordless lullaby, low and patient, a tune the audience will hear again without knowing where. ALISTAIR HARDING, wolf-crest ring on his hand, opens her wrist, and her blood runs green into a lantern. *Fwhumph.* The first gallowglass light. A boy of nine holds the lantern up for his father, solemn and proud to be useful. In the dark behind them a tiny girl made of frost slips out of the pit. Alistair sees her, and steps aside to let her pass. He has what he wanted. She runs into the white dark, and the wolves follow her. At the treeline a young woman in Harding colours, nineteen, a cousin, has seen all of it. She backs into the trees before anyone sees her face. The singing doesn't stop.

**Act One**

2. **Under Gin Lane, the Ring tunnels.** 1889. Through a street grating overhead, children skip rope on Gin Lane to the oldest rhyme in London: *"Green light, green light, who's in the cellar tonight?"* Nobody below listens. ALICE and MIRIAM BOUNDS run glim through the lamp-mains. Alice splices a live Verdance line to black out a rival dealer's den, and shows off doing it. The buyer, mid-deal, tilts his head: "Hear that? Crying. Always crying with this batch." Then he pulls his belt loose to tie off a leaking pipe, and the buckle rings on the iron. Alice is across the tunnel before she knows she's moved. Miriam sees it, and looks away. Then she steals an orange from him, tosses it to Alice and laughs, and the moment is gone. It's Alice's twentieth birthday. She waits all scene for Miriam to say so. **Act Two**

I thought we might be very polite to each other." "I can be polite." "I'll draw up terms." Nixon, on duty at the door, watches Tamara and looks away when she looks back.

**Act Three**

12. **London, the stroke of eleven, then the ballroom.** Every church clock in London begins to strike eleven, and four small things happen on it. At a grid box by the Works gate, a man with his face out of the light eats an apple and watches a cheap watch in his palm. On Blackwood Bridge, Miriam counts the strokes under her breath. On Eldgard's desk, Jamie's watch ticks beside an untouched drink. In the ballroom, the conductor lifts his baton for the eleven o'clock waltz. On the eleventh stroke the man at the grid box pulls a lever. Every chandelier gutters. Then London goes dark: street by street, the green dies across the city in a wave. In the black ballroom Nathaniel hears a woman crying inside the walls. Nobody else does. Eldgard is already gone.
13. **The ballroom, in the dark.** Two hundred guests shouting for lamps. One match flares, *fssst*: the lamp-girl, cupping it, the only light in the room. Nathaniel finds her by it. "Do you hear that?" The crying in the walls. She listens, honestly, and hears nothing but rich people frightened of the dark. "Hear what, sir?" "Someone crying." "In this house? Half the room is, sir. They've lost their light." He nearly laughs, and that surprises him more than the dark. "Who are you?" "The girl who lights your wicks." "That isn't a name." "It's the only one your house pays for." "Then tell me one for free." She looks at him over the flame as if she's pricing him. The match burns down to her fingers and she doesn't flinch; he notices that too. "Ask me again when the lights come back on." She blows it out. *Fff.* In the black, her voice is suddenly close to his ear: "Congratulations on your engagement, sir." Her hand is in his coat, and then it isn't. When the next match flares across the room, she's gone, and so is the folded schematic of his leap-gauntlet. He won't miss it until morning.

Nixon leads his squad down the gantries; Cole is with them, whispering, fumbling his new rig: "Sir, which end of the gauntlet—" Nixon: "The end facing away from you, Wick." Flash-bursts strobe, *fzzt, fzzt*, freezing the dark into snapshots: pipes, steam, a masked figure at the lock. "Step away!" Guard drill is two warnings. The figure turns, something glinting in his hand. In the next frame Cole sees the iron under the man's other hand glowing green, the way nothing glows but a lamp. "Sir, his hand's—" Nixon, twenty minutes after being told he's the son Eldgard actually wanted, doesn't give the second warning. He fires his lantern rifle. *KRAK.* The figure drops. On the gantry, Cole counted the warnings, and never finished his sentence.
15. **Blackwood Bridge, midnight.** Miriam waits in her red wool mittens. Jamie doesn't come. Frost creeps up the iron rail, then up her coat. She hears the Weeping. A small girl made of frost is standing beside her. Miriam, teeth chattering, asks her something we can't hear. The girl nods.
16. **The core.** Nixon pulls off the mask. It's Jamie, alive and bleeding. ELDGARD steps out of the dark: "Go, Captain. Wait at the gate. Speak to no one." Nixon goes. On the gantry above, forgotten, COLE watches the man on the floor lift his head and say something to his father. Eldgard kneels beside his son. *Cut away.* We don't see what he does.
17. **Blackwood Bridge.** Alice, still in her apron, finds Miriam frozen solid on the bridge, eyes open, inside a perfect ring of frost on a night with no frost anywhere else. Alice's scream. Grim and long.
18. **The Works gate.** Eldgard to Nixon: "Jamie fell. The saboteur escaped. You never saw his face. You'll be the man who kept this family's secret, and this family never forgets." Nixon nods. The leash goes on.
19. **Harding House, grey dawn, then the stables.** Eldgard tells Josephine and Nathaniel: an accident in the core. Josephine looks at her husband a beat too long. Nathaniel hears the weeping again, and this time it's in the floor. He makes it as far as the stables before he breaks. Nixon finds him there and holds him, his best friend's brother's blood still under his fingernails. *Therefore* every scene these two share from now on has a bomb under the table.

**Tag**

20. **City morgue.** INSPECTOR FELIX HAWKE pulls back the sheet. Alice identifies her sister. She touches Miriam's hand, and the frost melts under her fingers. *Drip. Drip.* The corpse's eyes move to Alice. "Ali." Cut to black.

## Episode 2 — "The Wake"

**47 DAYS TO FIRST SNOW.** **Focus:** what came back? **Set piece:** the morgue escape, which births the Frost Killer. **Sting:** Josephine hired the girl who is falling for her son.

*Logline:* Alice hides her sister, who sat up on a morgue slab and isn't quite right, and decides it's a miracle, while London buries Jamie Harding. Nathaniel finds a Lantern Guard cartridge at the scene of his brother's "accident," and he asks the one man he trusts to find out who fired it, the man who fired it.

| Character | Want | Obstacle | Solution | Twist |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Alice | To get Miriam well | The cold gets out of her whenever she feels too much, fastest through touch; she doesn't remember things; and the police say she was dead | Takes her to Old Wren, who calls it shock and amnesia. Alice believes her and sets rules | She's been asked to get close to Nathaniel, and now she wants to |
| The Lost One | To get warm and find the crying | She doesn't know how to be a person | Copies Miriam's words and habits, and lets Alice call her Mim | Touches a cat, and it dies |
| Nathaniel | To see his brother's body | Closed coffin, his father's orders | Searches the core level himself | Finds a Guard cartridge and hands the case to Nixon |
| Nixon | To bury the night | Cole saw the shot, and now Nathaniel wants answers | Silences Cole kindly | Assigned to investigate himself |
| Josephine | To keep Nathaniel out of the Works | Jamie is dead, and Nathaniel is the last son | Pays Grise in full and doubles the contract | The girl she hired is the lamp-girl her son can't stop looking at |

### Scenes

**Cold open**

1. **City morgue, minutes after Episode 1.** The thing in Miriam's body sits up and screams, a sound like glass splitting, *eeeeiiiKKK*. Two attendants freeze where they stand, mid-step. Felix's hand goes numb on his revolver. She runs barefoot into the night, and Alice runs after her.

**Act One**

2. **Gin Lane rooftops.** Alice follows a trail of frost from roof to roof. She finds her "sister" curled inside a lamp tower, hands cupped round the crystal, draining its light to get warm. Alice talks her down with their childhood counting game, "one post, two post," and the creature counts back in Miriam's voice, because the body knows it even if she doesn't. Before dawn Alice hammers on OLD WREN's door. Wren, who set Alice's arm when she was nine, listens to a heart that's barely there and holds a mirror under a breath that barely fogs it. "Ice does this. I've seen a drowned boy sit up after an hour by the fire. She's had a shock, love. The memories come back in their own time." She sees more than she says, and Alice's face begs her not to say it. Alice takes her sister home, and chooses to believe.
3. **Harding House, the parlour.** Jamie lies in a closed coffin. Nathaniel insists on seeing him; Eldgard refuses. Byron gently moves Nathaniel's hand off the lid, and Nathaniel notices that Byron's own hands are shaking. Then Josephine takes his face in her gloved hands, the only time this season she touches him without a reason: "You're what's left. You will stay out of that place." It isn't a request. The kid leather is cold.
4. **Lantern Guard tower.** Nixon files his first report as captain: a saboteur fled. Cole, cheerful: "I saw you hit him, sir. Flash went right through him." Nixon: "You saw a flash, Wick. Flashes lie." Cole decides to believe him. *But* that night he writes all of it in his diary anyway: the flash, the shoulder, the lock lit green under his hand, *no second warning*, and the man on the floor still talking when the Captain was sent away.
5. **Gin Lane tenement.** Whimsy. The Lost One meets bread, a kettle (terror), and a cat (delight). She calls Alice "Ali" exactly the way Miriam did. Then she picks up the cat and it freezes stiff in her arms. Her face: she doesn't understand death. Alice does, and decides it's the cold still in her from the bridge, the way Wren said. *Therefore* rules: gloves, no touching, until it passes. Miriam's red mittens go on her hands. Alice: "You'll be yourself again soon, Mim." The Lost One looks at her a long moment, a creature deciding whether to tell the truth to the only warm thing in the world. "Yes, Ali." The first lie, and the first kindness.

**Act Two**

6. **Lambrick coach.** Lambrick to Tamara: "The heir is dead. Your fiancé is the heir now. Marry him fast." Tamara: "Let me run the books instead." "You're a wedding, not a ledger." On the ride she reads his account book upside down and spots a line: *2 Nov, Works gate, 200 gns, A.F.*
7. **St Bride's, the funeral.** The Lantern Guard honour guard; Nixon carries the coffin, and it's wrong in his hands: heavier than a man, and dead-still on the steps, nothing settling. He tells himself that's what guilt weighs. Lord Chancellor VANE and the great houses attend. Nathaniel gives the eulogy and breaks halfway. When the church lamps flare he hears the Weeping under the hymn, the same tune a half-step down. Among the Harding brasses on the chancel wall is a girl's: OTTOLINE HARDING, 1820 TO 1839, DIED ABROAD. Nathaniel asks Byron who she was. "Your father's sister, sir." Byron says no more, and Eldgard never turns his head toward it. Tamara takes his hand: kindness, nothing more. Nixon watches.
8. **The graveside.** Alice is there on her contract, posing as a mourner from the Works, and for herself: Miriam loved this man's brother. Nathaniel recognises the lamp-girl. Two people grieving, one of them lying. He offers his handkerchief, the only thing he has done all day that nobody told him to do; she keeps it.
9. **Vykra laundry.** Josephine, veiled, meets Grise and pays in gold for the contract she placed weeks ago on her son, and doubles it now that Jamie is dead. "Keep him close. Keep him out of the Works. His father has one son left to spend, and the snow is coming." Grise doesn't ask what that means, and Josephine doesn't say. A reveal for the audience: Josephine is old Vykra, and Alice's contract came from Nathaniel's mother.
10. **Harding Works, core level.** Nathaniel forces the outer gate. The core door is sealed and Eldgard's men turn him back, but on the stairs he finds a spent Lantern Guard rifle cartridge. *Therefore* his brother's death wasn't an accident.
11. **Guard tower roof.** Nathaniel shows Nixon the cartridge: "This is one of yours." Nixon: "Half the corps was down there after." "Then find me the one who was down there before." Nixon takes the case. He's now investigating himself.

**Act Three**

12. **Tenement.** Alice comes home from the graveside to find a cat on the windowsill, built out of frost to replace the one that died in the Lost One's arms. It's beautiful, and it has too many legs. Alice laughs before she can stop herself, the first time since the bridge, and the Lost One, very pleased, adds another leg. Then she goes back to the window, draws a chain in the frost and whispers "Mother." Alice, gently, wiping it away: "Mum's gone, Mim. Ten years." The Lost One lets her think that's who she meant. Round her neck, under the mittens' cord, Alice finds Jamie's ring. The Lost One: "He was supposed to come." *Therefore* Alice needs to know why he didn't.
13. **Harding House, Jamie's bedroom, night.** Alice breaks in Vykra-quiet and finds Nathaniel already there, on his knees at the boot-tree. He has the poker up before she's off the sill. "I'll call the house." "Call them," she says. "Tell them what you're doing on your knees in a dead man's room at midnight." The poker comes down an inch. "Looking for why he died." "So am I." "Why would a lamp-girl—" "My sister loved your brother. She nearly died the same night he did." Nathaniel didn't know Jamie loved anyone. It's the first thing anyone has told him about his brother that his father didn't. Then the door handle turns. There's one place to go: Jamie's wardrobe, among his coats, chest to chest in the dark, his heart going against her shoulder. Through the slats, ELDGARD comes in alone with a candle, sits on his dead son's bed, and winds Jamie's pocket watch. *Tk-tk-tk-tk.* He doesn't say a word, which is worse than anything he could say. He puts the watch in his own waistcoat pocket, where it will stay. Nathaniel's breath catches; he is about to step out and go to his father. Alice puts her hand over his mouth. The candle goes, down the back stair toward the Works. She doesn't take her hand away fast enough, and neither of them mentions it. Crouched among Jamie's shoes, they find what they came for: a letter hidden in the boot-tree, an escape plan for two, and a diagram of the chain, and beside it a second stair of thirteen steps going down from a hatch marked coal. At the window, going, there's a beat where neither of them knows what to say. Then she gives him one true thing: "Bounds." He asks for the rest of it. "Girls on Gin Lane don't give the rest of it to a Harding." So she's Bounds to him, and it's the only name he uses for her all season, first as a joke and then as something he's afraid to change. Below the window, Byron, passing with a candle, watches her drop into the garden, says nothing and smiles.
14. **The core.** Eldgard alone with the chained light, which we still only see as green glow and a voice. "He was mine, and I spent him." The chain has frayed links. He counts them with the hand that wears the wolf-crest ring: "Forty-seven."
15. **Scotland Yard, then Gin Lane.** Felix has two frozen attendants, a missing corpse, papers that have already named the Frost Killer, and a Crown order on his desk telling him to drop the case, with Eldgard's influence all over it. He keeps the file and goes walking. On Gin Lane a witness saw a girl with frost hands on the Blackwood stair: "Bounds girl. Lives on Gin Lane with her sister." Felix knocks. Alice is out. The Lost One opens the door, frightened, and frost blooms. *Krrrk.* Felix's hand freezes to the doorframe, and she bolts.
16. **Tenement.** Alice comes home to a frozen door and Felix, his hand bandaged. "Your sister was dead. The surgeon said she was with child. Then she got up. Explain that to me." Alice: "Your surgeon was wrong. The cold fools them. They pull men out of the Thames in January who sit up on the slab." She believes every word, which is why Felix can't tell if she's lying. He doesn't arrest her. He files the word *child* away, and so does she. That night she asks Wren, once. Wren, who had her ear to that chest for an hour: "If there was a child, love, the cold took it. Let it go." Alice lets it go, and doesn't say the word again. It's Wren's second kind lie, and the bigger one.
17. **Tyburn Road.** The Lost One, drawn by the Weeping, walks to the Harding Works and lays a mittened palm on the gate. Every lamp on the street bends its flame toward her. Nixon, on night watch, sees it.

**Tag**

18. **The core.** The chain jerks. For the first time in four hundred years the prisoner lifts her head, and we see her face: blue ice, green fire in the eyes. MORGANA: "My daughter is here." Eldgard drops his lamp.

## Episode 3 — "Lanterns"

*Logline:* Eldgard sets his captain on the Frost Killer, and Nathaniel, finally given a real job, arms the hunt without knowing its target is the girl Alice is hiding. When Nixon gets the creature at the end of his rifle, he has to choose between his patron's order and a frozen boy.

**Cold open**

2. **Harding Works, the shop.** Eldgard gives Nathaniel his first real job: re-equip the Guard with leap-rigs and flash-gauntlets, and refit its lantern rifles, for the Frost Killer hunt. A piece of Miriam's regret, surfacing in a creature who doesn't know why her hands wanted to do this. Alice stares at it. "You've never once remembered." "I know." **Act Two**

6. **Vykra laundry.** Grise asks for Alice's report on the Harding son. Alice says he knows nothing. Grise: "Three days. Bring me something real, or the frost girl on Gin Lane becomes my business." *Therefore* Alice has to use Nathaniel.

She feels sick at how easy it was, and sicker at how much she liked watching. She leaves without returning the handkerchief.
8. **Scotland Yard.** Felix, frostbitten hand in a sling, corners Nixon. The frost girl is Miriam Bounds, who was dead. Nixon thinks he's mad, until Felix adds that the Harding boy didn't die in a fall: "There was a bullet hole in his coat, Captain. Somebody burned the coat." 10. **Christmas crystal market, dusk.** The roster said Gin Lane, tonight. So at dusk Alice wedges a stolen flash-cartridge on a candle fuse into a Gin Lane chimney pot, and while it flares and every leaper on the Guard roof launches toward it, she walks her sister the other way, through the busiest place in London. A chestnut-seller with a drum of coals gives the girl in the red mittens a hot chestnut "for the pretty mittens." She holds it in both hands, enormously pleased, and forgets to keep her head down. She sees a hundred crystal lanterns and, enchanted, reaches out. Every crystal in the market flares at once. On the Guard roof a lookout turns his glass away from Gin Lane.

**Act Three**

11. **The crystal market, set piece.** Nixon's leapers launch, *kshhh-THOOM, kshhh-THOOM*, over the stalls. Flash-bursts strobe the crowd. Alice shoves her sister under the chestnut stall, drops the flap, and whispers the only rule she knows for hiding: "Count the posts. Don't move till eight." Then she takes the roofs on her ghostlight to draw the leapers off, and it coughs and sputters. Below, in the dark under the boards, a body that was once a small girl under a bed counts, and can't breathe, and the Lost One doesn't know why. At six, she screams. Every crystal in the market shatters, glass rains on the crowd, and the chestnut-seller freezes mid-shout, his scoop still raised. Four dead. At first light Felix walks through the glass and counts them, one by one.
12. **Bell tower, set piece.** Cole corners her alone. She reaches for him, *please*, and he freezes from the hand up. Nixon lands and fires a flash; she drops, stunned, and the captain who doesn't miss has her in his sights. Cole is turning blue. Nixon can take the girl to Eldgard, or carry Cole to the furnace room before his heart stops. He lowers the rifle. "Hold on, Wick." He takes Cole. Alice, dragging the Lost One away, sees him choose.
13. **Harding House, morning.** The papers scream FOUR DEAD AT CRYSTAL MARKET. The servants say the Frost Killer was helped by a dark-haired girl with a ghostlight rig. Nathaniel remembers a hairpin and a gauntlet fixed in four seconds. Byron, pouring tea: "You're your mother's son, sir. You notice."
14. **Under the Works, an old lamp-tunnel.** Alice hides the Lost One where nobody would look: right under the Hardings. Four people are dead in the market. Alice, holding her with gloves on, says the thing you say: "You didn't mean it." It's the wrong sentence. It's what Miriam used to whisper under the bed about *him*, and it unlocks something in the body. Something runs down the Lost One's face and freezes. *Tink.* A bead of ice on the stone. Another. She catches one and stares at it. "What is this?" Alice, very quietly: "You're crying, Mim. You always cried. Under the bed, you cried for both of us." And the Lost One is suddenly full of a memory she has never lived: a buckle, a small girl's back, her own body not moving. "Ali. The belt. I should have—" Alice goes hard as iron. "We don't talk about him. That's behind the fence." The Lost One doesn't know what the fence is. She knows she has just been shut out of something that hurts, and that she wants in, to make it stop hurting. She remembers a woman in chains crying, too, and says nothing about that one. Alice holds her until the ice stops.
15. **Guy's Hospital.** Cole lives but loses two fingers. "Did I do good, sir?" "You did good." Eldgard is in the doorway. In the corridor: "You had her and you let her go." Then, softly: "I know what you did at the core, Captain. So do you."
16. **The tunnel mouth.** Nathaniel is waiting. He found it the way an engineer would: her ghostlight runs on his design and whines at his pitch, and he spent the night walking the lamp-tunnels under his own family's Works with a listening horn until he heard it. It's built from the schematic that left his coat in the dark at his engagement ball. Alice tells him the truth as she believes it: her sister nearly froze to death and came back wrong, and she's hiding her until she gets better. The Lost One steps out and, before Alice can stop her, takes Nathaniel's bare hand. Nothing happens. No frost. All three of them stare. The Lost One, still holding on, sniffs at his sleeve like an animal: "You smell like the man who keeps my mother." He doesn't understand it. Alice decides not to. He could take the Frost Killer to his father tonight: four dead in the market, his father's hunt, the first real job he has ever been given, and the one thing that would finally make him the useful son. **Tag**

18. **The tunnel mouth, then the Works yard.** Nathaniel turns to go. The Lost One, mittens back on: "Come tomorrow. Bring bread." 18. **Harding House, Josephine's dressing room.** Josephine lifts her veil, peels off gloves with a Vykra knot inside, and washes frost-dust from her fingertips in a porcelain bowl. The water skins over with ice. In the mirror, Byron is standing in the doorway.

## Episode 5 — "Lantern Night"

**33 DAYS TO FIRST SNOW.** The midseason turn. **Focus:** the heist into the core. **Set piece:** four separate plans on one festival night, ending with every lamp in London blazing white and bursting at once, and a crush on Westminster Bridge. (The city goes fully dark twice in this season: for an hour in the pilot, and for good in the finale. Lantern Night is the other thing light can do: it screams.) **Sting:** the light is a woman, the Tithe is Nathaniel, and the door is locked by his best friend.

*Logline:* On the night London celebrates its light, Nathaniel breaks into his own family's Works to see what Jamie died for. He finds a chained mother bleeding green, learns he's the next Harding meant to die for her, and hears the door lock behind him, on Nixon's order.

| Character | Want | Obstacle | Solution | Twist |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Nathaniel | The truth about the core | His father, his best friend, his own hope that his father is good | Byron's key, Tamara's code, Alice's hands | He's the next Tithe, and he chooses to stay with his father anyway |
| Alice | To get her sister what she needs, and to keep her | The Lost One has her own plan | Goes in beside her | Morgana: "You have my daughter's hand. Give it back." |
| The Lost One | To free her mother tonight | The chain burns her, and Alice holds her back | Freezes the whole core | Her mother's pain bursts every lamp in London, people die in the crush, and she doesn't stop wanting it |
| Nixon | To obey the man who made him | The order is to trap his best friend | Warns Nathaniel with their boyhood whistle, then seals the door | Nathaniel sees his face through the glass |
| Tamara | To prove her mind is worth something | Alice's contempt, and locks that reset mid-heist | Recodes the inner door by ear | Becomes Alice's equal, and her rival |

### Scenes

**Cold open**

1. **Tyburn, the same winter, 1489.** The Hargrave raid. Torches, young cousins of the Harding blood, and leading them the girl of nineteen from the treeline in the pilot, reaching the iron chain with a hammer. Alistair cuts her down at the lock. Somebody in the dark screams her name: "Marian!" By dawn the empty gallows field from the pilot is full: three thousand Hargraves on Harding timber, and their wives and children walked down thirteen steps into the dark. The heist we're about to watch has been tried before, and it failed.

**Act One**

2. **Harding Works, the Upper Gallery.** Eldgard brings Nathaniel into "the work" at last: a cathedral of glass vats where engineers "grow gallowglass from Tyburn iron." Nathaniel's joy is real and painful to watch. "At the solstice I'll show you the rest."
3. **Glasshouse.** Nathaniel: "There's nothing down there but vats." The Lost One: "He showed you a nice room. My mother isn't in a nice room." Nathaniel defends his father; Alice defends her sister. "He's my father, Bounds." "She's my sister, Harding." Their first fight, and it's about family.
4. **Harding House, the silver pantry.** Byron gives Nathaniel his old Guard key on a black ribbon. Jamie borrowed it in October, had it copied, and gave it back with a note Byron has kept in his pocket ever since: If I don't come up, give it to Nat. Byron guarded the core thirty years ago: "I heard her every night. I stopped hearing it when I stopped letting myself." Nathaniel, turning the key over: "The girl on the brass at St Bride's. Who was she to you?" Byron's hand goes to his tunic pocket and stays there. "Your father's sister, sir." The same words as the funeral; the hand is new. He lights himself back down the servants' stair with a tallow candle. He hasn't carried a green lamp since the core, and nobody in the house has ever asked why. He keeps quiet about Josephine's hands. Not yet.
5. **Vykra laundry.** Grise tests her new girl: a Works lock cipher, solved in four minutes. Grise slides it back across the counting table. "Four minutes. The inner codes change on Lantern Night. That's worth more than tea." Tamara: "What does it cost?" "Something of your father's." A long moment. "His coal-men cut the outer grid during the finale." Grise pours. Tamara is welcomed with tea and a knife. For the first time in her life she belongs somewhere because of her mind, and the first thing it cost her was her father.
6. **Guard tower.** Eldgard is folding a note in a laundress's hand when Nixon comes in: *Someone has bought your inner codes for Lantern Night.* He gives Nixon Lantern Night security for the Works, plus a private order. "My son will come tonight. Let him through. Seal the core behind him. The girl doesn't leave." Nixon: "And Nathaniel?" "He's my son, Captain. He'll be with me." When the door has closed, Cole comes in with the duty roster, sees the captain's face and reads it as pride. "Lantern Night at the Works, sir. You've never once disobeyed an order, have you?" Nixon: "No."

Alice and Tamara meet: the fiancée and the undercity girl, the new Vykra and the cut one. Tamara, setting the code on the table: "You must be the lamp-girl." Alice: "You must be the wedding." "I'm told I've a head for locks." "I'm told you've a father for them." "Then we've both been told things." Nathaniel, delighted: "You two are getting on." He's the only one in the room who thinks so.

**Act Two**

8. **Lantern Night, the Strand.** Fireworks over a blazing green city, crowds, the Lord Mayor. In the crowd, Lambrick, and beside him Ajax, very alive, with a new scar from brow to jaw: the river police cut him out of a barge's anchor chain, gauntlet and all, and Lambrick paid their fee. He's eating an apple. Behind them, a coal-mob. Their plan: cut the outer grid during the finale and let London see Harding light fail before the vote.
9. **Thames outfall, the heist begins.** The pipe is full of black water. The Lost One freezes it into a glass road and they walk inside the river. Jamie's key opens the first grate.
10. **The gantries.** Nixon, on the high walk, sees them pass. Orders say let them through. He gives the whistle they invented at nine years old, *turn back*. Nathaniel hears it, smiles because it's Nix, and keeps going.
11. **The Strand, finale.** Ajax's crew cuts the outer grid. The East End goes dark in the middle of the fireworks. Inside the Works, every lock resets, and the heisters are trapped between two doors with the Guard alarm ringing. Tamara goes white. She sold Grise that minute for the codes.
12. **The shaft.** Tamara recodes the inner door by ear, listening to the tumblers, while Alice holds her by the belt over a crystal shaft four hundred feet deep. Tamara, upside down, not looking down: "If you drop me, I'll haunt you." Alice: "You'd have to find me first." Neither of them says thank you. *Click.* It opens.
13. **The core.** They go down, and we see her whole for the first time. MORGANA: nine feet of ice and green fire, iron hooks through her shoulders and hips, her blood running green along channels into London. She is singing, and it's the Weeping: the Lantern Hymn's tune, with none of the words.

**Act Three**

14. **The chain.** "Little frost. You came." The Lost One grabs the chain, and for one second her whole face changes: every song Miriam ever knew comes back into her at once. Then it burns her mittens to smoke. Alice pulls her back. Morgana, to Alice: "You have my daughter's hand. Give it back." The Lost One, mittens still smoking, steps between Alice and her own mother, and doesn't know why her feet did it. Alice, behind her, holding tighter: "She's my sister. Whatever came back in her, my sister's under it." Morgana looks at her daughter standing in front of a human. Then she tilts her great head and looks at Alice with something terribly like pity. She doesn't correct her. The Lost One doesn't either. Then, to Nathaniel, almost gently: "You came down these stairs once, little Harding. And your brother is not dead. He is cold."
15. **The lock.** Nathaniel touches the chain, and it tightens. Morgana screams, and across London the lamps surge brighter. Down here, a hundred feet under the street, a few flakes of snow form in the air of the core and melt on the gantry rail. Nobody looks up. His blood feeds it, and for one frame, in the surge, he sees snow, and a man standing very still in it. Then it's gone, and he'll tell himself it was the dream. Jamie's glass blade is still stuck in the lock where he left it, and his diagram suddenly makes sense.
16. **The core.** Eldgard steps out of the dark; he's been waiting. He lays a folded note on the gantry rail, in a laundress's hand, and looks at Tamara. "Four minutes, Miss Lambrick. Mother Grise sells everything twice." They didn't break in. They were let in. The chain is still straining from Nathaniel's touch, and Morgana is still screaming. Eldgard lays his ringed hand flat on the lock. The iron settles under it with a long *tunnng*, the way a struck bell stops when you touch it, and the scream cuts off. He takes his hand away. Nobody looks at the ring. "Now you know what your brother knew." The Tithe: every fifty winters, at first snow, a Harding walks down to the chain of their own will, or it breaks. "And if it breaks, the light dies for good, the White comes through until dawn, and one of us goes into it with her. She won't drag us. She never has. We've always been handed over." "I raised Jamie for it. Jamie ran. Now it's you." Nathaniel: "Why not you?" Eldgard doesn't answer. The silence goes on a beat too long, and Nathaniel files it away.
17. **The core door.** It seals with a hiss, iron on glass. Behind the glass is Nixon, following orders, his lips moving on the oath he swore on the night he was made captain, *Light the dark. Keep the flame*, over and over, loud enough in his own head to drown out whatever Nathaniel is hearing. Nathaniel: "Nix!" Nixon doesn't look away, and that's worse.
18. **The core, escape.** The Lost One, desperate, turns to Alice and says, in Miriam's voice, "Hold on to something, Ali." Then she lets go of the cold. The core freezes around her mother, and Morgana screams, the way she screamed when Nathaniel touched the chain, only worse. Her blood flares through every channel at once. Across London, on Lantern Night, every lamp blazes white for one heartbeat and bursts. *Pop-pop-pop-pop*, street after street, a wave of shattering glass under the fireworks. The Lost One flinches as if she heard every one. Alice, gripping the gantry rail, is watching her sister's face in the white light, and for the first time in her life she is afraid of her. Then she runs her gauntlet into the red and blows the door, and lets go a half-second early, shaking out her fingers. Tamara drags the Lost One into the outfall. Alice reaches back through the blown door for Nathaniel, and he takes her hand. Then he lets go of it. "Get her out. I need to hear the rest." She leaves him there.
19. **Westminster Bridge.** Glass rains on the festival crowd from the bridge lamps, then only fireworks for light, then a panicking crowd and a crush. Cole's squad hold a barricade with flash-bursts; people still die against the railings. In the crush a small girl pushes a shard of broken lamp-glass into Cole's two-fingered hand, "for luck," and is gone. He keeps it. Lambrick, on a coal cart: "Remember who kept you warm!"
20. **The core, after.** Father and son, alone with the prisoner. Nathaniel: "She said Jamie isn't dead. She said he's cold." Eldgard doesn't look at Morgana when he answers. "She tells every Harding whatever will bring him to the lock. She told me my sister was still singing." He says it like a man pressing on an old scar. "Thirty-three days. I won't lose two sons. Help me find another way." Nathaniel can't tell if any of it is a lie. He says yes. He chooses his father, and he puts the word *cold* away where he keeps the weeping in the floor.

**Tag**

21. **Gin Lane rooftop, the black city.** Alice and the Lost One, soaked and shaking. The Lost One: "I'm going to free her. Even if all of them freeze." Alice takes her burned hand anyway. Below them, London is lit in patches, every third lamp a broken stump.

### Scenes

**Cold open**

**Act One**

2. **London, the cold.** Since Lantern Night half the lamps in London are broken glass, and the rest come and go. Coal carts with LAMBRICK on the side. Prices chalked up: doubled, then tripled. And it can't take her. She has to step out of the hooks herself. Everything down there runs on willing." Nathaniel has never in his life left a sum unchecked. 5. **Harding Works, the shop window, midnight.** Alice climbs the Works wall to tell the only person who would understand that her sister has forgotten her birthday. It's everything he has wanted his whole life, and she can see that it is. She doesn't knock. Her breath fogs the pane. She wipes it clean with her glove, the one with his handkerchief folded inside it, and goes back down the wall. A second later Nathaniel looks up, at a window with nobody in it.
6. **Lambrick House, the gun room.** Tamara, behind a curtain, hears her father and Ajax: charges along the Tyburn conduit where it meets his Pimlico gas main, set to blow on the night of the vote so Harding's Works look rotten to the Crown. Ajax: "And if your daughter gets in the way?" Lambrick, after a pause: "Then my daughter should have stayed home." Then, to the ledger, not to Ajax: "And if she comes home and talks, Dr Cutler has signed the papers. I only need a second doctor." Behind the curtain, Tamara learns her price twice in one breath.

**Act Two**

7. **A Covent Garden tea shop.** Alice swallows her pride and asks Tamara, the new Vykra with a coal baron's maps, for a way into the core. Tamara, to the girl with the teapot: "Something with no poison in it, please." Alice: "Here? Good luck." Tamara tells her about the charges. The only route in runs through Ajax's tunnels. Tamara: "Then go another night." Alice: "There isn't one. She's forgetting me." Two girls who dislike each other, starting to understand each other. The girl who pours their tea has a knot stitched inside her glove, and neither of them looks at her hands.
8. **The frozen canal at Little Venice.** They meet in secret under a bridge, snowless cold, their breath smoking between them. He tells her about the Heartglass: "We can save both of them." She tells him the Lost One is forgetting her. When she pulls off a glove to warm her hands, something falls out of it into the frost: his handkerchief, folded small, kept where a Vykra keeps the thing that says whose she is. He picks it up. "You kept it." "Bounds keeps things." Behind it the lining is slit, a neat cut where something was stitched once. He runs his thumb along it. "What was here?" She could tell him now: a knot, a contract, his mother's gold. She's going into Ajax's tunnels tonight and may not come up, and she wants him to have one clean thing. "Something I'm not any more." It's true, and it hides the truth, and she chooses it. For once in his life he doesn't wait to be reached for. He kisses her, and finds out she has been waiting since the ballroom. *Tik. Tik.* Ice ticking off the bridge above them. Then both of them use the kiss to hide the dangerous part: she's going in tonight, and he still hasn't finished the sum on what the Heartglass is for.
9. **Harding House, the linen room, then the study.** Byron confronts Josephine. "I saw your hands." "I did it for Nathaniel." "You always say that." She has a needle in her sleeve, and she doesn't use it. "Thirty years, Josie." He leaves. She sits on the linen and doesn't cry. Then a maid brings her a folded note in Grise's hand, bought an hour ago from a tea-girl in Covent Garden: *Tyburn conduit. Vote night. The girl.* Josephine takes it straight down to her husband's study. "How do you know?" "I'm your wife." Eldgard sends for Nixon. "You stood at my core door and let her walk out of it. Bring her back alive, or bring me your lantern." The Heartglass needs her. The woman who has just spared Byron's life has set the trap he will die in, and neither of them knows it.
10. **Harding Works, then the Harding House hall.** A cipher note from Tamara in their childhood code reaches Nathaniel: *The conduit. Charges. She's going in.* He runs, grabbing the leap-gauntlet as he goes. In the hall, Byron sees him tear out of the yard. He hasn't carried a green lamp since the core. He takes his old Lantern down from its glass case on the wall, still charged after thirty years, and when it lights he hears her again, as clearly as he did on night watch. He follows anyway.

**Act Three**

11. **The Tyburn conduit, set piece.** Alice and the Lost One go down. Ajax's men are lighting fuses in a side drain. Four tunnel children are sleeping in the warm pipe-nook beyond it. One wakes as the girl in red mittens goes past, and asks if she's the Frost Killer from the papers. The Lost One considers it seriously. "No. I'm a sister." The child goes back to sleep. Nixon's squad waits in the dark ahead, Cole among them. In the dark, Cole holds up his diary: "Tell Mr. Harding, sir, or I will." Nixon: "After the vote." Cole agrees, because he still believes in him.
12. **The ambush.** Flash-bursts. Nixon corners the Lost One and gets her by the wrist, and she grabs his bare wrist back. No frost. He doesn't freeze, the way Nathaniel didn't, and in the strobe and the shouting nobody sees it but the camera. She can smell Harding on him the way she smelled it on Nathaniel, and under it the cellar beneath her mother; and one of the names in the song she has heard in every lamp since the bridge belongs to the same blood. She's cornered, it's the only weapon she has, and she uses it: "They told you your mother had a fever. She didn't." He lets go as if burned, which is what she wanted. Alice hits him before he can get his hand back. She was at the core door on Lantern Night and saw this face behind the glass, and she fights him gauntlet to gauntlet, in a tunnel too narrow to swing in, the two leads' first real clash, to win. She's winning when Nathaniel comes down the main screaming "Gas!" Nixon is nearest. Before he has thought about it he has Nathaniel by the collar and is hauling him back toward the river stair, out of it, the way he'd haul any man: the core door again, by hand. Nathaniel sees the face from the glass, tears loose, and goes the other way, deeper into the main, toward Alice. Nixon's hand closes on nothing. Ajax, seeing the Guard, lights early. The Lost One's cold meets a flash-burst in the main.
13. **Pimlico, then Parliament.** The gasworks erupts. *WHOOOM.* Fire rolls along the river. A mile downriver the Charter vote is three names from done: Eldgard, Lambrick and Vane in their places, Nathaniel's chair empty, and Josephine looking at it when the windows blow in on the voting lords.
14. **The rubble.** Nathaniel was deep in the main when it went. In the smoke, a green flash, then another: Byron's Lantern, coming into the main for the boy, into the space where Nixon would have been, and finding him under the rubble. Byron lifts the beam off Nathaniel and takes its weight himself, and it crushes him. Dying, he says: "Your mother killed the inspector. She does it for you. Don't let her." A breath, and the thing he has known for thirty years: "I let her go at the gate, sir. I'd do it again." Then: "You were always enough." He dies with his hand on the boy's face. In his other fist is the blue ribbon.
15. **The river stair.** Nixon, his hand shaken off, has already turned back for Cole, and carries him out of the fire. Alice comes up with the Lost One, who uses the last of her cold to smother the flames around four trapped children. Then she looks at Alice politely, like a stranger. "Thank you, miss." She doesn't know her name. Alice tells herself it's the shock again, worse this time. It's the last time she'll manage to.
16. **Parliament, broken glass.** Vane, bleeding from the temple, announces that the Charter is suspended and the Works pass to a Crown board. Lambrick takes a seat on it. Eldgard has lost the Works.

**Tag**

17. **Harding House, the music room.** Nathaniel walks in covered in ash. Josephine is at the piano. "Byron's dead." She weeps, really. "He told me about Inspector Hawke." The music stops. A long beat. "Then you know I love you."

## Episode 7 — "The Girl Who Isn't"

**21 DAYS TO FIRST SNOW.** **Focus:** two reveals on one day: the sister and the best friend. **Set piece:** wolves of the White run through Whitechapel, and Nathaniel and Nixon fight side by side an hour after the friendship breaks. **Sting:** Alice chooses the creature anyway, and Tamara hands Nixon a folder with his name on it that he cannot make himself open.

*Logline:* Alice finds the letter Miriam never left her and learns that the thing she has been protecting is not her sister. Nixon finally tells Nathaniel who fired the shot. Then the chain thins enough for the White to come through, and the only way through the night is together.

| Character | Want | Obstacle | Solution | Twist |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Alice | The truth about who she's been protecting | The truth: Miriam was leaving her, and this isn't Miriam | Throws the Lost One out | Runs into a wolf attack for her, and chooses her: "I'm Alice. I'm your sister." |
| The Lost One | To be wanted for herself | She has lost Alice's name, Alice has thrown her out, and Miriam's love for Jamie keeps surfacing in her body | Goes to the place she first came through | Commands the wolves in front of the city, and becomes the Frost Witch |
| Nixon | To put the lie down | Nathaniel's love for him | Confesses at their boyhood spot | Is handed a folder with his name on it, and carries it a week unopened |
| Nathaniel | To know what his father did, and what he made | Every adult he loves is lying | Opens Jamie's coffin himself, then corners Eldgard with a fistful of slag | His brother's coffin holds no body, and Alice says out loud what the Heartglass he built is for, which he half knew |
| Tamara | To stop her father, and to stop being polite | A wedding in seven days | Takes the seized Harding papers and gives Nixon his file | Kisses Nixon a week before she marries his best friend |

### Scenes

**Cold open**

1. **Gin Lane, 2 November, morning.** Miriam's last day, from Miriam's side. She is radiant, sick in a basin, and hand on her belly. Alice is asleep on the other side of the bed. Miriam starts a letter: *Ali, about him, when we were small—* She stares at it a long time. She scores it out until the paper tears. She starts again, and this time writes it fast: three short lines we don't get to read. She means to leave it on Alice's pillow, loses her nerve, and sews it into her coat lining. Then she pulls on the red mittens.

**Act One**

2. **Gin Lane, a borrowed room.** Afternoon. Alice mends the Lost One's coat, burned in the gasworks, and her needle catches on something in the lining. Folded paper, Miriam's hand. She reads it twice. *I'm leaving with him. I'm sorry, Ali. Don't follow me.* Miriam was leaving her and never meant to say goodbye. Then the door bangs. The Lost One, home from the market with an armful of oranges she is enormously proud of, chattering about a man who sells hot chestnuts out of a drum. She doesn't use Alice's name; she hasn't since the gasworks. Alice puts the letter in her apron pocket, smiles, and peels her an orange. She says nothing. She says nothing all evening, and we watch her not say it.
3. **The same room. The peak of the sisters' story.** Late, one candle. Alice holds out the letter. "You were leaving." The Lost One, who lost Alice's name in the gasworks, still knows this face and still knows this hurt. "Yes." And the fence comes down, because Alice is too angry to hold it up: "You always left. You left me under that bed. Every time. You promised nothing would touch me, and then you counted fence posts while he—" She can't finish. Fifteen years, and she has finally said it out loud, to the only person who was there.

    The Lost One has been waiting four weeks to be let in. She has the guilt, and it's the last thing of Miriam's left in her, because the cold took the birthdays and the songs and the name and couldn't get its teeth into this. She kneels on the floor so she's lower than Alice, the way you'd kneel to a child under a bed. "I should have stood in front of you. Every time. I'm sorry. I was so frightened of him that I let you be frightened alone, and I've hated myself every day since." Then the thing Miriam never could have said: "If he came through that door now, I'd stand in front of you. I'd let him freeze."

    It is the apology Alice has wanted her whole life, word for word, and it's perfect. That's how she knows. Miriam couldn't have said it. Miriam *flinched*. Alice, barely a breath: "Say my name." The Lost One opens her mouth, and there's nothing there. A long silence. "You're not her."

    "No." Plainly: Morgana's daughter, who came through on the bridge, wearing Miriam's body and Miriam's memories like a coat. Alice has known since the core that something else came back in her sister. What she built her whole theory to avoid knowing is whether Miriam is still under it. "Is she in there? Under you?" "No. She was gone before I opened her eyes. I'm sorry. She was going to leave you, Ali. She told me on the bridge. She was frightened, and it wasn't of you. She asked me—" She stops. Alice: "What?" The Lost One looks at her the way she'll look at Jamie's portrait tomorrow: at something she wants that was never hers. "Nothing." Then the real horror, said gently, as a gift: "I'm not sorry for her. I'm sorry for me. I love you. I don't know whose that is anymore. I think it's mine."

    Alice looks at the thing that has been her sister for four weeks, the one that remembered her birthday, peeled her an orange and just gave her the only apology she has ever been given, and understands that her real sister never loved her this well. "Get out." The door closes on her.

4. **Harding House, breakfast.** Eldgard sits silent and diminished without his Works. Josephine runs the table now: the wedding is in seven days, and the Lambrick money will buy the Works back. Nathaniel: "Or what, Mother? You'll poison me?" Josephine, buttering toast: "If I had to choose between you and London, I chose a long time ago." Nathaniel stands. "If anything happens to the frost girl, I'll stand up in Parliament and say who poisoned Felix Hawke." She doesn't stop buttering. "Do. And tell them where the Inspector was standing when I heard him." The knife scrapes. "In your glasshouse. With four dead at the market and the Frost Killer asleep under your orange trees." Nathaniel sits down. At the end of the table Eldgard doesn't look up. She has kept her son safe the way she always has: by making sure he can't move.
5. **The core, under Crown control.** Vane and Lambrick tour it with Tamara on her father's arm. On the stair down, Lambrick, quietly, the only time he talks to her as if she were a person: "Your mother hated the green. Said it made everyone at dinner look drowned. I kept her room on coal till the end." Then he looks up at Morgana. There's no horror in his face, only arithmetic: "She could be bled faster." The man who kept one room warm out of love would freeze a city for the same reason, and calls both of them providing. On the clerk's desk sits Eldgard's seized Heartglass folio. Tamara reads one line: *Vessel: White-born.*

**Act Two**

6. **Blackwood Bridge, fog.** The Lost One, thrown out, walks to the place she first came through, and does the only thing her body knows how to do with being sent away: she cries. This time the tears don't freeze on her face. They freeze the air. *Tink. Tink. Krrrk.* The thin place on the bridge cracks like a pane of glass, and Alice's "Get out" has opened a door. Howls in the fog. Wolves of the White, white-furred and blue-eyed, pad out of nothing and bow to her. That's the most dangerous thing that could happen.
7. **Guard tower roof, their boyhood spot.** At the spot they've had since they were nine, Nixon's first words are the cause: "Cole says today." Then: "I fired the shot. I didn't know it was him." Nathaniel hits him once, hard. Nixon lets him. It's for Jamie, and for a hand on his collar in a burning gas main, and for whoever came into the main instead. Neither of them says Byron's name. "Your father told me to lie. He came down after." "Was Jamie alive when you left?" "Yes." Nathaniel walks away without a word. A friendship of fifteen years ends on a roof.
8. **A Gin Lane gin shop.** Tamara finds Alice drunk. She tells her about Lambrick's arithmetic and the line in the folio, and why she's telling her: "If that cage works, my father owns the light. Then he doesn't need my wedding. Only my silence." She pours Alice's gin on the floor. Alice: "Why would I care? She's not my sister." Tamara: "She put out a fire around four children with the last of her cold and forgot your name doing it. Did Miriam ever do that?" Alice, after a long time: "No." She looks at the gin. "Not once." She stops drinking.
9. **Whitechapel and Commercial Road, set piece.** The wolves that came through on Blackwood Bridge don't stay there. Over the old Hargrave plague pits, where the hanged were dumped, the fog splits and they pour into the gaslit streets; gas lamps freeze and burst, *tink, tink, tink*, and the first strobe frame finds bodies in the snowless street. Nixon's Lanterns respond: leapers on the rooftops, flash-bursts (the wolves scatter from the light), quench-blades. Shot the way the pilot's core raid was: black, then a strobe frame, then black. Every flash shows us a wolf somewhere new and closer. We see them only in the frames the Lanterns light, which is scarier than a wide shot and costs a tenth as much. Nixon fights like a man trying to get himself killed. Cole fights beside him with his two-finger grip. The first time his gauntlet runs dry he won't fire the three flashes Wicks are drilled to fire, because heroes don't ask, and he fights a wolf with an empty rig until Nixon finds him by luck. "Three flashes isn't scared, Wick. It's the drill." After that, every time it runs dry, he puts three flashes on the reserve straight up. *Out.* Nixon is beside him in two seconds. It happens three more times tonight, and every time, Nixon comes.
10. **The Pavilion music hall, set piece.** Whimsy into grim. We stay inside. A comic singer keeps a hundred people singing "Daisy Bell" to drown out the scratching at the doors, and the song gets louder every time the scratching does. A door panel bows inward. A child stops singing, and her mother starts her again. Nathaniel arrives on leap-boosters with a bag of flash-cartridges, and Nixon arrives from the other roof. No words. Their old move needs Nathaniel to put his back to the man who sealed a door on him and grabbed his collar in a burning main. For half a second he doesn't, the same half-second as the conduit, and the bowed panel gives. A wolf comes through. Nixon puts his forearm in its mouth. *Krrk.* Frost runs up his sleeve to the elbow. Then Nathaniel turns his back on him, on purpose, and they fight the way they did at nine years old, perfectly in sync, an hour after the friendship ended.
11. **The Pavilion stage door, and the street.** The Lost One walks into the street of wolves and says one word in a language of wind. They stop, every one of them, except the biggest. She kills it with cold, and loses another memory. Then she turns the rest, every one, and walks them back through the fog to the bridge, and the crack in the air closes behind the last of them. *Tik.* A hundred people watch a girl command wolves. By morning she'll be the Frost Witch, the one creature in London who can open the White and shut it again. Then Alice, sober now, reaches her through the street of wolves. The Lost One doesn't know her face anymore. Alice: "I'm Alice. I'm your sister." The sisters' scene: chosen, not inherited.

**Act Three**

12. **Shadwell, dawn.** In the steam of the Shadwell laundry, Josephine and Grise agree a price for the Frost Witch, delivered to Josephine, not the Crown. Grise: "Where is she?" Josephine: "Wherever my son puts her when the city gets too hot for her. Before Lantern Night that was my glasshouse, and after last night it will be again. I want her taken by hands he can't trace to me." Josephine: "She's not a girl, Grise." Grise: "None of us were, by the time we came here."
13. **St Bride's, the Harding vault, dawn.** All night Nathaniel fought beside the man who shot his brother, and one word from the roof has gone round his head the whole time: *Yes.* Jamie was alive when Nixon left. At dawn he goes to see his brother. Crowbar, lantern, the Harding vault under St Bride's. He breaks the seal on the coffin his best friend carried down these steps. *Krrk-CRACK.* Inside, laid out in Jamie's burial coat, are three sacks of grey slag from the Works vats and a family Bible for weight. No body. Nathaniel stands in the candle-dark with a fistful of slag, and Morgana's voice from Lantern Night comes back to him: *He is cold.* Above him, on the chancel wall, the brass plate still says DIED ABROAD.
14. **Harding Works, Eldgard's old gallery, empty.** Eldgard is drunk among the dead vats. Nathaniel drops a fistful of slag on the bench in front of him, the same grey as the dead vats all around them. "What did you do after Nixon left?" "Ask me at the solstice." "Now." "I finished it." He doesn't ask about the Heartglass. There's something to ask, a five-foot bore and a sum he left on the bench, and he doesn't ask it.
15. **Harding House, the long gallery, then the glasshouse.** Alice brings the Lost One in by the servants' door, and they cross the gallery of Harding portraits in the half-dark. The Lost One stops at Jamie's: twenty-five, laughing at something outside the frame, a skate-blade over his shoulder. Her hand goes to her belly without her knowing it. Her breath catches on a feeling that isn't hers, the whole of Miriam's love for him arriving in her body at once. Alice: "What's wrong?" "Nothing." Alice looks at the portrait, then at her, and decides not to ask. At the garden door, Josephine is sitting on the back stair in the dark, as if she has been waiting. She knows exactly who the lamp-girl is; she bought her. Alice doesn't know who bought her. Josephine looks at the frost girl, then at Alice, and says only: "Mind the third stair down to the garden. It creaks." An old Vykra, teaching a young one the way into her house. Alice minds it. In the glasshouse, Nathaniel is waiting, slag still grey in the creases of his hands. Alice doesn't let him start. Tamara read her the line in the folio: *Vessel: White-born.* "You built a cage for my sister." He goes white, and not because it's news: because it isn't. A five-foot bore, chalked on a bench with his father's hand on his shoulder, and a sum he never finished. She watches him find it, and that's why she hits him harder than Nathaniel hit Nixon. Then: "So you know how to break it." Before he can answer, TAMARA comes in out of the rain with the rest of what she read on her father's desk: a lunacy order, two doctors' signatures, waiting for the day she opens her mouth about his books. "A spinster's nearest relative is her father," she says. "A wife's is her husband. The wedding is in seven days. I'd rather it were you than Bethlem." Nathaniel looks at Alice. Alice: "Marry her." "Bounds—" "Marry her." Both of them mean it, and both of them hate that they do.
16. **Gin Lane rooftop.** Whimsy and grief. The Lost One asks Alice to teach her the counting game again, since she lost it. For the first time Alice tells her where it came from: the fence outside the window, eight posts, and he's done. The Lost One listens, then counts them with her, and at eight she keeps going. "Nine." Alice: "There's no nine." "There is now. Nine is the one where we get up." She gets it wrong on purpose, nine, ten, eleven, to make Alice laugh. It works. The game born under a bed has a new ending.

**Tag**

17. **Guard barracks, the infirmary.** Tamara comes in while Nixon is changing Cole's dressings. She gives Nixon a folder copied from her father's seized Harding papers: "It has your name on it." She has read it. The binding order for the Heartglass was on the same desk, and she can do the arithmetic on a word like *Reserve*. She gives him the folder and keeps the sentence. Then, a week before her wedding, she kisses him. Cole pretends very hard to be asleep. Nixon doesn't open the folder. He puts it inside his coat, against his chest, and he doesn't sleep.

## Episode 8 — "The Wedding"

*Logline:* Nathaniel marries Tamara to keep her out of Bethlem, with Alice watching from the gallery. While the vows run, the Vykra come for the girl in the glasshouse, Nixon refuses an order to fire on a crowd and finds the cellar he was grown in, and the snow comes tonight.

### Scenes

**Cold open**

1. **The core, sixteen years ago.** NATHANIEL, five, follows a lullaby down the forbidden stairs and finds a huge, gentle woman of ice who asks his name, and sings it back to him. Eldgard snatches him up and swings a lantern slowly before his eyes. "You had a dream. You never heard anything." The boy, glassy: "I had a dream. I never heard anything." It's the "nightmare" Nathaniel has had all his life, and the reason that, of everyone in his house, he's the one who hears her cry.

**Act One**

2. **The glasshouse, the night before.** He marries Tamara in the morning, and nobody has said a word about it all evening. Whimsy: the Lost One, who has never seen a wedding, insists on practising one. She plays the priest, reading with enormous solemnity from a prayer book held upside down, and makes Alice stand in for the bride. They say every word to each other, slowly, and both of them mean every word, and both of them know it. "Wilt thou have this woman…" "I will." The Lost One turns the page: "You may now kiss the—" Alice: "That part isn't ours." They stand there not stopping, and not moving either. The Lost One looks from one to the other, sincerely: "You both look sad when you look at each other. Is that what weddings are for?" Nobody answers. In the morning his mother will have a piece of paper for him.
3. **Harding House, the groom's dressing room.** Josephine straightens his tie and gives him Grise's receipt for the contract on her own son, in a clerk's hand: *The second Harding son. Agent: Alice Bounds.* It's the first time he has ever seen her first name, and it's on a bill. "She was paid to get close to you. By me." Nathaniel can't breathe. She wants his eyes on the altar today, and the lamp-girl's eyes on him, and nobody's on the glasshouse. Grise's girls go on the bell. Josephine leaves, and Eldgard takes her place at the door with Jamie's cufflinks in his palm. Nathaniel: "Did you hold his hand?" Eldgard: "I held his hand." It's the half-truth he lies in. Nathaniel takes the cufflinks and puts them on, smiling, because he knows what today is for.
4. **The glasshouse.** Nathaniel arrives with the receipt in his fist: "Were you paid?" Alice: "At first." She starts to say the rest, and he is already gone. He didn't call her Bounds. He didn't call her anything. Alice takes her coat back off, straps on her gauntlet and sets a chair facing the door: whoever comes for her sister today comes through her. The Lost One, who heard her say *cage* in this room a week ago and has thought about nothing else since, makes it easy for her: "Go. Watch him. If you don't, you'll hate yourself, and then you'll hate me. I'll be here." "Mittens on. Door locked." "Yes, Ali." The third lie. Alice goes.
5. **Guard tower.** Dawn on the wedding day. Nixon finally opens the folder he has carried against his chest for a week. His charity-school blood test, the green sliver, Eldgard's hand: *Hargrave, N. Cadet line of Harding. Reserve. Blood suitable.* He was never a charity case; he was a spare. A monster in a gas main told him so two weeks ago, and for the last week he has carried the proof against his chest so she could stay wrong. Vane's orders arrive while it's still open on his knee: arrest Eldgard Harding after the vows, then clear the crowd at the gates "by any means." Nixon folds the file away: "I'm done being anybody's reserve, Cole."
6. **The Harding chapel, the vestry.** Before the guests. Tamara, in white, comes looking for somewhere to be sick and finds Nixon, uninvited, in his captain's coat, standing over a ring box on the vestry table that is waiting for a best man nobody has named. She shuts the door. "Say one word and I'll walk out of this church with you." His file is in his coat; he can feel its corners. "This morning I found out I'm on Eldgard's books. Like the coal." She picks up the ring box, opens it, closes it, and puts it in his hand. "Then hand it to me properly." She goes out to be married. At the chapel door Josephine is waiting to settle her veil, and peels off one glove to do it. Stitched inside the glove is a Vykra knot. Tamara has carried a needle with that knot's poison on it since the gallery stairs at the hearing, and now she lets her mother-in-law see it, threaded through her bouquet ribbon. "I know who, Mother." Josephine's fingers don't stop on the lace. "Then you know what I'll do for mine. Be his wife."
7. **The Harding chapel.** Guests, lilies, the Crown. Eldgard sits alone, unaware. Nixon walks in uninvited and stops behind the groom he hasn't spoken to since the roof. Alice is beside him before he asks, and the gauntlet he built for her in the glasshouse, strap by strap, is already whining up to overload, *eeeeeeee*, the way he told her never to run it. He knows what she's about to do. "They'll hate me for a hundred years." "Us," she says, and puts the gauntlet's core against the iron. The heat comes back through the plate into her palm and she holds it there, the smell of her own glove burning, until the ice round the blade lets go. He pulls it free. His hand goes to his coat pocket, to the watch, and comes away. He cuts his palm on the blade and presses his blood and his father's wolf ring into the iron. His father's warning is in his ear: *hoping*. He can't swear his brother isn't part of why his hand is steady, and it took an undercity girl's burned hand to get the blade out. Somewhere above, a church bell strikes twelve.
6. **London.** The chain falls. The last time London went dark, the light came back within the hour. This time the green dies street by street, the same wave as the pilot, and nothing comes back. One wordless shot after another: a tram dying in the snow, its conductor still punching tickets by matchlight for passengers who aren't going anywhere, a mother on Gin Lane lighting a tallow candle with shaking hands. Under the Works, Nathaniel listens, and for the first time since he was five, nothing is crying in the walls.

**Act Two**

7. **The core.** Morgana rises out of the pit as her wounds close, nine feet tall. She looks down at Nathaniel and says his name, the way she sang it to a five-year-old on the stair: "Nathaniel." Then: "A Harding opened it." She doesn't thank him. The White pours through behind her, with wind, snow and wolves, and she goes up through the Works roof toward Tyburn hill. Alice feels the floor shift under her and knows what it means. She takes the handkerchief out of her glove, the one he gave her at his brother's grave, and tears it in two. *Rrrip.* He binds her burned hand; she binds his cut palm; neither of them is any good at it. Then, where the chain was anchored, the core floor splits, and the White comes up through the crack in a wall of wind: Alice and the half-crystal girl on the outfall side, Nathaniel and his mother on the other. Alice can't see across it. The girl in her arms is going colder by the minute, the outfall is the only way open, and the only fire Alice can count on tonight is on Gin Lane, where the Vykra are holding. She takes it.
8. **The core stair.** Nixon against Ajax: quench-blade against Black Gauntlet. Ajax, scarred, grinning, gauntlet up: "Look at you. No lantern. Welcome to the gutter, Captain. It pays better." Nixon doesn't answer him. The first piston blow comes in on the arm the wolf froze at the Pavilion, and we hear it go, *krak*. He takes the quick-takedown Nathaniel gave him in the bell foundry one-handed: blade through the wrist plate into the gauntlet's crystal, which seizes the piston, then into Ajax. He dies frozen mid-laugh, his apple rolling down the stairs. With Ajax dead, the Guard stair is clear: the one way out of the Works the wolves haven't found, and it stays clear only while somebody holds the head of it. Nixon sends his forty to the bridges and holds it himself, the broken arm strapped across his chest with his own belt. He whistles down it the third signal they made up at nine, the one they never once needed: *come on*. Nothing whistles back.
9. **London, the night the snow came early.** Nixon's forty and every loyal Lantern form flash-lines across the bridges to keep the wolves off the tenements. The Vykra hold Gin Lane with knives and candles. Alice holds the lane with the Lost One wrapped in coats in a doorway behind her, as close to the coal as Alice can get her. Every time she checks on her, her hand goes flat over the coats at the belly for a second, keeping two of them warm, and comes away before anyone sees. Every time a flash lights the sky over Tyburn, she looks. Tamara throws open every Lambrick coal depot free to the city, and there are fires in every street. On Gin Lane the Vykra hold the wolves at a ring of Lambrick coal; without it, the lane would have frozen before the wolves ever reached it. Whimsy in the dark: Cole teaches a crowd of children to make flash-shadow rabbits on the walls. Near dawn a pack breaks the ring at the far end of the lane. A Vykra girl screams for the Frost Witch. The Lost One gets up out of the coats, half crystal, and says the word in the language of wind that stopped the wolves at the Pavilion. Nothing answers. The Heartglass drank it. She sits down hard in the doorway, and the Vykra close the ring with knives.
10. **The Tyburn Road barricade.** The charges run out along Cole's line, a barricade of overturned trams across Tyburn Road, the last thing between Gin Lane's fires and the hill. He holds it with his last cartridge, two fingers short and seventeen years old, until it's empty. Then he does what he was drilled to do, what he learned in Whitechapel isn't fear: three flashes on the reserve, straight up over Tyburn Road. *Out.* Every time before, his captain came in two seconds. Half a mile off, at the head of the Guard stair, Nixon sees the three flashes go up. His whistle down the stair still hasn't been answered. If he leaves the stair, the wolves find it, and Nathaniel has no way up. In the bell tower he chose the boy. He stays. Behind Cole's line, a girl in a Vykra glove comes up the road with another girl in her arms, half crystal, heading for Tyburn, because an hour before dawn, when the wolves wouldn't answer her, the girl asked for her mother. He knows the one carrying: the ghostlight girl who burned a wolf off his hand at the Works gate. He knows the one being carried, too. The last time he was this close to her, in a bell tower, she took two of his fingers. She looks straight at him and doesn't know him; she spent that memory somewhere. He remembers for both of them. As Alice passes, the shard of lamp-glass from Westminster Bridge in his other fist, he lifts his two-fingered hand in the Wick's salute he smacked himself in the eye with on the day his captain was made, and gets it right this time, and Alice sees it. He holds the road until they're past it.
11. **The core, empty.** Nathaniel and Josephine alone, the hooks hanging empty. "I'd do it all again." He heard the same words under a beam at Pimlico. "I know." At the foot of the stair she stops. "I paid for that girl." "You paid for a week, Mother. You couldn't afford the rest." Josephine almost smiles. It's the first thing about her son she has ever failed to buy. From far above, the whistle comes down the Guard stair again, the way it has every few minutes for hours: *come on*. Nathaniel is at the stair before it has finished. And Josephine, who has controlled every door in his life, says: "Go to her." It's so unlike her that he stops with his hand on the rail and looks at her, really looks, and sees a woman who has already decided something. "Not until you tell me where you're going." She won't. So he stays, and doesn't go to the girl he loves, because he's watching his mother the way she taught him to watch everyone. He's still watching when she starts up the hill by the Guard stair. At the head of it Nixon is standing where he chose to stand. He lets them past without a word. Nathaniel, going by, gives the answering whistle, hours late. Nixon runs the other way, toward Tyburn Road.

**Act Three**

12. **Tyburn hill, set piece.** Morgana stands over London in the storm, with wolves in a ring around her. Four hundred years of grievance. Josephine climbs, and Nathaniel climbs after her. Up Tyburn Road, past the barricade a seventeen-year-old held to his last cartridge, Alice comes up the hill carrying the Lost One. Across the snow they see each other for the first time since the lock, alive, and there's no time for any of it.
13. **The Works vault, intercut with Tyburn hill.** The last hour before dawn. Lambrick has been filling satchels with the last gallowglass all night. Cut off from the chain, it dies slowly: every crystal in the vault is a shade dimmer than it was at midnight, and he's packing them as if he can outrun the dawn. Tamara finds him and offers her hand: "Come out, Father." He holds up a satchel to her, almost pleading, and in his hands the glow is going out while they talk: "This is for you. All of it. It was always for you." "Mother hated the green." He looks at the crystals. He looks at her. He can't put them down; he never could. She walks out. Behind her the wolves come into the vault. Cut back to the hill, where a mother is being asked the same question.
14. **Tyburn hill.** The Lost One sets herself down in front of Alice, between her mother and the city, the place Miriam never once stood, and sings the hymn back to her mother in the old tongue, the way it was before it had words. The wolves stop circling. "One of them let you go." Morgana: "Then come home, little frost." The Lost One looks at Alice. Alice, who has spent the season holding on, lets go: "Go, if you want to. I'll be all right." Her hand starts toward her sister's coat, toward the one thing she could say that would keep her here, and stops, and drops. She means it. She did this once before, with a nod, at an altar.
15. **Tyburn hill.** The Lost One tells her the last thing: "I've spent everything else of hers. The birthdays, the songs, her name for you. I kept this. I kept it from you. On the bridge she was still alive. She asked me something. 'Go back to Ali. Stand in front of her. I never did.' I said yes. Then I wanted to. I didn't tell you. I wanted you to need me." Miriam's last thought was Alice, after all, and her last act was to find someone braver to keep the promise she broke. Alice laughs and cries at once: her sister did love her. She just couldn't do it herself. The Lost One stays. Morgana, who tore her own hooks rather than let her daughter pay, lets her stay, which is harder. Then she names the price: "A Harding chained me. A Harding comes with me." Alice's eyes go to her sister's coat, where the only other Harding on this hill is sleeping, then to Morgana, and her mouth stays shut. She waits. It has to be given, and she won't take it. Nathaniel steps forward, and Alice steps in front of him, into the place nobody ever stood for her, her fist in his coat. Her first word to him all night: "Don't."
16. **Tyburn hill.** Josephine steps past him and says to Morgana the words Morgana said at the lever: "Not him." Morgana looks at her for a long moment and knows her. Then, to her son: "I'm a Harding. I chose the name." He catches his mother's wrist. Alice is still in front of him, her fist still in his coat. The wolves shift their weight in the snow. Josephine, quietly, the only time all season she asks him for anything: "They'll say I died of fever in Switzerland." Nathaniel: "No. They'll say your name." She hears what else that means: the Inspector, the glass of water, all of it. "All of it?" "All of it. Mine too." A long beat. His hand opens. "Go." Then, before she can turn, he takes Jamie's watch out of his pocket, winds it, *tk-tk-tk*, and puts it in her palm. Neither of them says whose it is. Alice, her fist still in his coat, sees it, and lets him. Josephine closes her hand on the watch and kisses his forehead. To Alice: "Keep him out of the Works." Alice: "You bought me for him. It's the only thing you ever bought that worked." Josephine almost laughs, and that's how she goes. She takes Morgana's hand. At the edge of the storm Morgana stops beside her daughter, and doesn't leave her the cold to stay with. She lays one hand flat on the girl's chest and draws the White out of her, the way you'd draw a splinter. The crystal goes out of the girl's skin. She will never make snow again, and she will never again hear her mother singing in a lamp. What's left is a girl, shivering for the first time. Then the two women walk into the white. The wolves follow, and the storm goes with them.

    *Why this isn't Otty again:* Otty went willingly too, and his grandfather let her. The difference between Nathaniel and every Harding before him isn't the choice. It's the lie. Otty got a brass plate that said DIED ABROAD. Josephine gets the record (scenes 18 and 19).

17. **Tyburn hill, dawn.** The snow stops in the middle of falling. Nathaniel is on his knees where his mother was, and for a long time nothing happens. Alice doesn't touch him. She kneels in front of him in the snow and lets him have it, because nobody ever let her. When it comes it isn't crying. It's a sound with no word in it, *hhh, hhh*, a man trying to breathe around something too big for his chest. She stays where he can see her until it passes. She doesn't know what to say to a man who has just said "Go" to his mother, so she gives him the most private thing she owns. Under her breath, the way Miriam taught her under the bed, she counts for him. "One post. Two post." He knows it. He heard it in a ballroom corridor five weeks ago and never asked. He counts with her. At eight she stops, because eight is where it has always ended. He keeps going. "Nine." Nobody told him about nine. He got there on his own. And then, for the first time in ten episodes, he says her name. "Alice." She hears it. He has never once said it, and she knows which bill he read it on. He's saying it now, when nobody has paid for it. He kisses her, or she kisses him; for the rest of their lives they will disagree about which.
18. **Westminster, first light.** The first dawn of the episode, grey through the tall windows, coal smoke rising from Tamara's fires. Vane names Nathaniel "the man who put out London" and signs a warrant. Tamara, Lady Harding and heir to Lambrick, opens her father's ledger at the night of the Charter vote, *A.F., Pimlico, 300 gns.*, dips the clerk's pen, signs her own name under the entry as heir, and lays it on his desk. The man the Crown put on the Works board blew in its windows, and his daughter says so with his name and hers. Nixon lays Cole's diary beside it. It has been inside his coat since the last dark before dawn, when he found the boy on Tyburn Road, sitting up against an overturned tram as if he were resting; we never saw him find it. He opens it at the burned discharge-log page, copied word for word, and under it, in a seventeen-year-old's careful hand, *2 Nov. Capt. hit him in the shoulder. No second warning. Mr Harding sent the Capt. to the gate. The man on the floor was still talking.* Below that, in pencil, a last line: *Held the road. Nobody past who shouldn't be.* That's how the room finds out. It puts Nixon's shot on the record, the warning he didn't give, and the man who stayed behind with a living boy. It is also the diary of the Wick he didn't go to. Nixon could burn that page a second time. He sets it down open. Across the desk Nathaniel looks at him, the first time he has looked straight at him since the lock. "I haven't forgiven you." "I know." "Stand where I can see you." Nixon steps up beside him, his arm in a sling, the way he did at the altar, and it means the opposite now. Vane, cornered, offers Nathaniel the pardon on one condition: the Crown seals what happened under Tyburn, for the public good. Nathaniel: "No. Write it down." Vane: "The Crown doesn't keep that kind of record." Nathaniel: "It has kept one since 1689. It's in your desk, under your signature." Vane doesn't blink; an annex in a drawer has never frightened a Lord Chancellor. Then Tamara, who hasn't sat down: "Lambrick depots are feeding every grate in London this morning, my lord. They close at noon, unless that record is read into Hansard, all of it, where anyone with a penny can buy it." Vane: "And if the Crown requisitions your depots, Lady Harding? I can sign that before noon." Tamara opens her fan, and the cipher wheel inside it ticks. "You can. Then you'll be the Lord Chancellor who seized the coal on the coldest morning in London's memory, and I'll read your signature into Hansard myself, under mine." Outside the tall windows the coal smoke is the only warmth in the city, and every chimney of it is hers. Vane looks at the smoke for a long time. "The House sits at two." Nathaniel turns to the clerk, and the clerk dips his pen. "Edric Harding—"
19. **The Record, final montage.** Nathaniel's dictation carries over everything that follows. It starts as testimony, read flat and exact like a ledger, and somewhere in the middle it stops being for the clerk. No score until line 7, then the Lantern Hymn, the tune only, the way Morgana sang it. Every image is a climax from the season, seen in the days after: the dictation takes one morning, and the pictures run on across the weeks it sets moving.

**Final scene**

20. **The White.** Hard cut on the heartbeat. Wind, and nothing else. Endless snow. Josephine walks beside Morgana through the drifts, where the chain's dead went, and stops. A man is standing in the snow with frost in his hair: JAMIE HARDING, exactly as he went down, one hand still half-raised the way it was on the lock. His breath doesn't smoke. His eyes are open and don't move. Josephine touches his face, and it's cold all the way through. Morgana, beside her: "The White keeps what it's given. It doesn't give it back." Josephine takes out the watch her other son put in her hand, winds it, *tk-tk-tk*, and holds it to Jamie's ear. The hands don't move. Nothing runs here. She winds it again anyway, and doesn't let go of him. Far behind him, small in the white, a boy of nine stands with a lantern raised, the flame still burning after four hundred years and the boy as still as the snow. Josephine sits down in the drift beside her son. Cut to black.

    The card that opened every episode comes up one last time. It doesn't count down. **DAY ONE.**

**What Season 2 inherits**

- **A London with no light.** Coal is power now, and Tamara owns the coal. The young woman who wanted to be her father's heir has become something more dangerous.
- **A Harding child carried by a daughter of the White.** Both worlds will want it. Alice has known since the first snow and never used it. Its uncle doesn't know it exists.
- **Josephine, in the White**, beside a son who can't answer her. She went in to end the debt and stayed for him, winding a watch that won't run. Nathaniel knows he put it in her hand.
- **Everyone the chain ever took** is standing in the White exactly as they went: Edric with his lantern, Otty, the Orchard's apples, Jamie. Kept, not living. Morgana said "cold, not dead" and meant it to the letter. Nobody comes back. Season 2's question: what do you owe the dead when you know exactly where they are?
- **Nixon and Tamara**: married to his cousin, holding his hand, nothing decided. Nathaniel and Alice: a kiss on Tyburn hill and no promise, because he's still Tamara's husband, and Tamara's hold on the city's coal rests on being Lady Harding. The two women have already set the terms on a Westminster bench: the paper, until the coal runs out. Three people in love, and the only marriage among them is the one keeping London warm.
