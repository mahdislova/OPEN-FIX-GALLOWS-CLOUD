# The Green Gallows — The Showrunner's Season

Oct 3, 2026 · @patrick · revised Oct 10 and Nov 8, 2026

A new ten-episode season, built from scratch on the strongest bones of the original: the Harding legacy, Miriam and the Lost One, and the sister reveal.

## The pitch

London, November 1889, and the whole city glows green. Every lamp, tram and furnace burns gallowglass, a crystal that only House Harding knows how to grow, at the Harding Works on the old gallows field at Tyburn. The Hardings say it grows from iron in the ground. In fact it grows from a woman. For four hundred years they have kept a creature from the frozen world beyond, the White, chained under the Works, and they bleed her for light. The cold outside the city is hers too: her winter seeps out around the chain and has lain over the countryside for four hundred years, and a ring of green heat walls, burning her own blood, keeps it off London. Every fifty years, at the first snow, the chain that holds her is fed with a Harding life. The last was Eldgard's own older sister. The next is due this winter, and the son raised to pay it is about to die.

**Series engine.** London runs on a secret. Every faction wants to own the secret, the girl at its centre or the boy who will inherit it. Every week, one plan for the light runs into another.

**Season question.** Can Nathaniel Harding end the bargain that lights London without becoming the father who kept it? Under it runs the sisters' question: will Alice Bounds let the thing wearing her dead sister's face choose its own life?

**Theme.** Protection as control. Every adult in this show hurts someone to keep them safe. The young ones have to learn whether love can let go. Five more themes run underneath it: bought or given, who pays for the light, what gets written down, the spare and the heir, and where you belong. The season argues every one of them from several sides and never says which side is right.

**Tone.** The dread of *Chernobyl*, the sisters of *Arcane*, *House of Cards* in the drawing rooms. Whimsy breathes between the violence: a creature learning what an orange is, a recruit tangled in laundry lines, two engaged friends who plan to be very polite to each other for forty years.

**The clock.** Every episode opens on a card: 49 DAYS TO FIRST SNOW. Snow falls on the outerlands all winter, but inside the walls it melts before it lands, so "first snow over Tyburn" means the first snow the walls can't melt. In four hundred years that has always come on the longest night, 21 December, when the dark outlasts the heat, so that's the day everyone counts to. Under the card runs a second clock the audience can see: the frost line, as the walls dim sector by sector and the cold creeps into streets that have never seen ice. At the end of Episode 8 it comes two weeks early, because the prisoner makes it fall, and the last two episodes play out over one night.

**Kept from your outline:** the Harding legacy and the 1489 massacre at the Gallows; Miriam's death and the Lost One; the sister reveal ("You're not her"); Josephine's protection turning into control; Eldgard's Snow Confession; Nixon's Hargrave blood; Tamara the operative; the Vykra; Ajax; Miriam's red mittens.

**Cut:** Edmund, Virgil, the Chapel, the Duke, the Prime Minister, Trevane. Each one did a job someone else now does, or existed only to deliver information. Morgana moves from a nun's body in the Chapel to the chain under the Works, so the villain's secret and the light's secret become the same secret.

**New:** Mother Grise, who runs the Vykra; Lord Chancellor Vane, for the Crown; the Royal Lantern Guard as a full corps with its own gear, ranks and oath; Hester Pell, Eldgard's aunt, the one Harding who ran; Kit Marrow, Alice's rival in the Vykra; and the heat walls, which put the cost of the light on everyone's horizon.

## The craft rules this season obeys

Ten rules, each taken from a writer you named, and each one checked against every episode below.

1. **Therefore and but, never "and then."** Each scene is caused by the last one or cuts across it. Parker and Stone's test: if the honest word between two beats is "and then," one of them goes. ([Perell](https://perell.com/note/but-therefore-rule/))
2. **Secrets with long fuses (Brent Weeks).** Weeks plots the character arcs and the big external climaxes, then layers in secrets he pays off books later. Here each major secret has three layers: what the public believes, what the audience learns, and what the character learns last, when it stings most. Everything serves his one rule: keep people turning pages. ([Reactor, Weeks AMA](https://reactormag.com/highlights-from-brent-weeks-r-fantasy-ama/))
3. **Plans collide.** Most set pieces happen because three or four characters have separate plans for the same night. Nobody's plan works as drawn. The explosion, the heist and the wolves in the streets all come out of choices, never coincidence.
4. **Sisters only trauma can split (Arcane).** Alex Yee: the sisters had to be "so emotionally entwined that only an event that traumatic" could drive a wedge between them. Alice and the Lost One get three episodes of closeness before the first wound and four before the break. ([SYFY, Alex Yee](https://www.syfy.com/syfy-wire/arcane-co-creator-alex-yee-unpacks-the-act-i-twists?amp))
5. **Power lives inside intimate relationships (Willimon).** "Politics itself doesn't interest me. It's power that interests me." Every marriage, friendship and parent-child pair here is also a power arrangement, shown through behaviour and hands more than speeches. ([BFI notes](https://script-consultant.co.uk/bfi-beau-willimon-interview-notes-pt1/))
6. **One vision per episode.** Each episode has one focus you could put on its poster (the ball, the morgue, the hunt, the hearing, the heist), 17 to 22 scenes for about 44 minutes, and every scene turns something. The three tent-poles (Ep 5, the midseason heist; Ep 8, the wedding; Ep 10, the finale) run 21 to 22 so their set pieces have room. The rest run 17 to 19, with scenes that only carry information folded into scenes that carry feeling.
7. **The grid.** Your colleague's Want / Obstacle / Solution / Twist table runs for the four or five characters who carry each episode. The others rest and come back with their own stories later.
8. **Whimsy earns the grim.** Each episode alternates a light beat with a dark one, gives at least one feeling that goes unsaid, and gives one young character a chance to prove they belong. Every episode ends on a tag that changes the board: a reveal, an escalation, or a choice that can't be taken back.
9. **The form keeps a secret too (Jonathan Nolan).** Nolan hides his biggest twists in the structure: the timeline (William and the Man in Black in *Westworld*), the narrator, or who caused the catastrophe (*Fallout*'s Vault-Tec, selling shelter from the bombs it dropped). Here the walls are the Vault-Tec move: the Hardings sell warmth against a winter their own chain makes, and print WARM AS A MOTHER'S ARMS on every tram. And the 1489 cold opens are read by a voice we don't place until Westminster: the season is Nathaniel's Record. Every twist is fair, with its clues on screen from the pilot.
10. **Pressure, not rooms.** If two people are talking, one of them is trying to get something, hide something or get out. Secrets are caught, not confessed. Information is stolen, not overheard. The past cuts into the present at the moment it matters, as a flash inside a scene, not at the top of the hour. The only cold opens set in the past either frame the season (1489, in Eps 1, 5 and 10) or show a night we've already seen from another angle (2 November, in Eps 4 and 7).

## The world and its four rules

Gallowglass is Morgana's frozen blood, and an iron chain under the Works holds her. Four rules run the season, and nothing else does. A viewer should be able to say each one in a sentence.

1. **The Tithe.** Every fifty years, on the night of the first snow, a Harding must walk down to the chain of their own free will before midnight, or the chain breaks.
2. **Harding blood is the key.** Only Harding blood can hold the chain, mend it or open it, and her cold can't touch it. That's how Eldgard mends the crack Jamie cut, why the Lost One can take Nathaniel's hand without freezing him, and why Nixon, a Hargrave, is Harding blood too.
3. **Breaking it has a price.** If the chain breaks, she goes free, the light goes out for good and the walls with it, the White comes through until dawn, and one of the house that chained her goes with her into the White. It has to be given. She won't take it.
4. **The Lost One's cold costs her Miriam.** The cold comes out of her when she feels too much or when she chooses to use it, and it kills by touch. Every time it comes out, it burns one of Miriam's memories. While it's in her, it holds Miriam's body exactly as it was on the bridge.

Everything else in the season is people, not rules.

**The long winter and the walls.** This isn't a fifth rule. It's what the first three look like from outside: the chain holds her, but not all of her. It didn't seal the crack between London and the White. It pinned it open a finger's width, and for four hundred years her winter has seeped out through it into the land around London. Past the walls it is always November, and worse: ice on the ponds in July, frosts that kill sheep in the field, a cold that kills a man who sleeps out in it. That cold is weather, and it kills the ordinary way. The White is something else: the wound itself, with wolves in it and time stopped, and it only comes through a damaged lock or a thin place. The walls keep out the first. Nothing keeps out the second.

- **The heat walls.** A ring of green-glass heat walls, fed off the Ring, stands round London in twelve sectors. The first went up round the City in 1502, and by 1800 they enclosed everything from Kensington to the Isle of Dogs. Past them lie the outerlands: farms, villages and the north road, cold and poor. The Hardings sell protection from a winter they made, and nobody knows it.
- **Snow.** It falls on the outerlands all winter. Inside the walls it melts before it touches the ground. *First snow over Tyburn* is the first snow that settles inside them.
- **The dimming.** Toward the end of every fifty-year cycle, as the chain frays, she bleeds less and the walls draw less. The public never notices; the Works' own gauges do. Eldgard's line: "The output dips at the end of a cycle. My father saw it in 1839. It comes back." It comes back when a Harding walks down the stair.
- **The coal network.** Before gallowglass, the walls were coal braziers. When the Hardings fitted the glass burners they left the old coal mains, valves and furnace rooms connected underneath. Lambrick owns them, the furnace plans, and the men who still know how to light them. Coal can carry a few sectors for hours. It can't heat the whole city through a night. That makes coal something people fight over, not a way out.
- **Class is geography.** Mayfair and Westminster sit deep inside, behind two rings of heat. Gin Lane, Shadwell and Whitechapel lie against the east wall, the oldest and worst-kept sector. When the walls dim, Alice's neighbours freeze first.

**What gallowglass does**

- Burned in lamps and furnaces, it gives a green-white light and steady heat. The Ring, London's lamp grid, carries it under every street.
- Ground to dust it becomes **glim**, the undercity's drug. Users hear the Weeping, which is the first clue, given in Episode 1. It is Morgana's lullaby to her daughter, and London has sung it for four hundred years as the Lantern Hymn, with new words nailed over the old tune. Her daughter can hear what nobody else can under it: the names of every life the house fed her.

**The Royal Lantern Guard.** Chartered in 1689 to protect the Ring, they are London's elite, armed by the Harding Works. Oath: *Light the dark. Keep the flame.* Ranks run Wick (recruit), Flame (trooper), Lantern-Captain.

- **Lantern gauntlet:** a forearm rig with a gallowglass core. It fires a **flash-burst**, a blinding strobe that drops a man and sends wolves of the White running.
- **Leap-boosters:** steam and crystal thrust packs for rooftop flight, thirty seconds per charge. When the charge runs out, you fall.
- **Tether-hook:** a grapple line for swings and quick climbs.
- **Quench-blade:** a glass sabre that drinks heat, built for fast takedowns. One touch numbs a limb; a long cut freezes the blood.
- **Lantern rifle:** fires crystal rounds that burst into light. Every discharge goes into a log, bound into the rifle's case and audited whenever the rifles go back to the Works. That log matters in Episodes 4 and 10.

**Undercity tech.** **Ghostlights** are bootleg gauntlets, unstable and loud. Alice builds hers from parts stolen out of Nathaniel's shop. **Ajax's Black Gauntlet** is Nathaniel's own abandoned prototype, stolen two years ago, which hits like a piston.

**The Vykra.** A guild of undercity women, spies and knives for hire, run by Mother Grise from a Shadwell laundry. Their mark is a knot stitched inside the glove. Their poison, **winter's kiss**, is distilled from gallowglass and kills with frost on the lips, so it looks like the Frost Killer's work.

**The White.** A world of snow, wind and wolves on the far side of the chain. Time stops there. Whatever this side sends into it is kept exactly as it went in, and doesn't come back.

**Places.** Harding House (Mayfair) and its glasshouse. The Harding Works over Tyburn, with the core deep below. Gin Lane tenements, against the east wall. The heat walls, and the outerlands beyond them; Hester Pell's cottage past Barnet, outside the north wall. Blackwood Bridge. The Shadwell laundry. Lambrick House and the Pimlico gasworks, where the Tyburn conduit (the old Works drain) runs under the city into the Thames. The Lantern Guard tower at Charing Cross. Parliament. Newgate.

**Why now.** 1839 plus fifty is 1889: a Tithe year, and the son raised to pay it dies seven weeks before it's due. The walls are already dimming.

**Otty, in the season.** She is never seen. She appears five times: the brass on the chancel wall in Ep 2, Eldgard's "She told me my sister was still singing" in Ep 5, the blue ribbon in Byron's pocket in Ep 6, Hester's "I know how old she was" in the same episode, and Eldgard's confession in Ep 9. By then the audience knows what Eldgard's "I raised Jamie for it" is a copy of.

**Hester Pell.** Born Hester Harding in 1812, Eldgard's father's younger sister. At seventeen a doctor said she would never carry a child, and her father stopped seating her near him at dinner. In the autumn of 1839 she watched the walls go grey, knew what it meant before her brother said a word, and ran the week before the snow. Otty went down the stair that winter. Hester married Walter Pell, a stonemason, and has lived fifty years in a cottage past Barnet, outside the north wall, on wood and his trade. She is the one Harding who walked away, and she isn't clean: other people kept paying. She appears in Ep 6, Eldgard names her in Ep 9, and she comes back inside the walls once, at the end, to hold the candle while her husband takes the lie off her niece's brass.

**The Orchard.** Under the Works, thirteen steps below the coal-hatch stair, is a gallery of bunks and iron rings where Hargraves were kept alive for four hundred years: fed well, kept healthy, and taught from the cradle that walking down the stair was holy. Every fifty years one of them did. They were the cattle in the contract. Hardings are taught never to say the word upstairs. The first name on its wall is Marian, who led the raid in 1489, and the last is Marian too, Nixon's mother: the Orchard named its girls after the first one who tried. The keepers counted the children in chalk tallies headed APPLES. By 1889 the herd is gone. Marsh's rhyme counts the seven who were left when he ran, and Nixon is the only Hargrave anyone ever brought out into the light.

**Tobias Marsh.** An Orchard man who ran in 1867 and was caught. The Crown holds him in Newgate as a madman, cell 1417, and nobody listens to what he sings. Jamie Harding visited him every Sunday for two years and learned the way down. The diagram in Jamie's boot-tree in Ep 2 shows the chain, and beside it the Orchard: a stair of thirteen steps going down from a hatch marked coal. That is why Jamie went to the Works with a plan in Ep 1, and why Nixon, tracing a dead boy's footsteps without knowing it, ends up in the Orchard in Ep 8.

**How Nixon learns it.**

1. Ep 3: his blood test glows green, and Eldgard pats his head: "Still good." Felix gives him the Newgate slip.
2. Ep 4: Marsh pinches his cheek, "Marian's chin!", and sings the rhyme. Nixon walks away not understanding it.
3. Ep 7 and 8: Tamara gives him the file, and he carries it a week before he opens it on the morning of the wedding: "Reserve. Blood suitable." He learns he is the spare, not what the spare is for.
4. Ep 8: he follows the rhyme down thirteen steps, and finds the Orchard and his mother's name. This is the scene that turns him.
5. Ep 9: he frees Marsh in the Newgate break-out. Hours later Eldgard confirms all of it in the Snow Confession, including what the spare was grown to do.
6. Ep 10: he offers himself with his eyes open. "They grew me for this the way they grew my mother."

**Supporting**

- **Lord Hector Lambrick**, 55. A coal baron losing the light war. He wants London back on coal and owned by him. He owns the old coal mains under the walls, the furnace plans and the men who still know the valves. Thirty years ago he paid the Vykra to kill Eldgard and never learned which girl failed him. When he sees Morgana, his reaction is greed, and he wants her bled faster.
- **Ajax Fink**, 35. Lambrick's enforcer, an ex-Guardsman drummed out of the corps, carrying the Black Gauntlet. He's funny, patient and terrifying, and he survives too long.
- **Kit Marrow**, 21. Grise's best knife, the girl Alice learned the trade beside, and the one who inherits Alice's work when Alice is cut loose. Faster than Alice, always. She hunts the Lost One for Grise, pours tea for her enemies with her eyes down, and leads the taking at the wedding. Beaten on the count of nine, she runs Alice's errand through the wolves.
- **Mother Grise**, 70. Runs the Vykra out of the Shadwell laundry. She trained Josephine, and keeps every contract in one book. She sells to everyone and loves no one, except, briefly, girls who beat her.
- **Inspector Felix Hawke**, 40. Met police. The only man who saw a corpse get up, and the first to die for saying so in public (Episode 4).
- **Lord Chancellor Vane**, 60. The Crown. He doesn't want the truth, only the lights on, and the one thing the Crown must never be seen to have known kept in his desk: the 1689 annex, with the Crown's signature on the chain.
- **Morgana.** Nine feet of ice and green fire in iron hooks. She speaks rarely, and every word costs someone.
- **Jamie Harding**, 25 (Episode 1 and cold opens). The heir raised to die for the chain, who chose love and sabotage instead.
- **Miriam Bounds**, 24 (Episode 1 and cold opens). Alice's older sister, carrying Jamie's child, about to leave Alice for good, and, though Alice never knew it, sent to Jamie on a Vykra contract his mother paid for. Hard, quick and funny, a Vykra runner who never flinches in a fight, and who flinched every single time it mattered.
- **Hester Pell**, 77. Born a Harding, Eldgard's aunt, the one who ran in 1839. She lives outside the north wall with her husband WALTER PELL, 80, a stonemason who cuts true names into headstones. Eldgard's authority can't manage her, and she knows what he's for.
- **Old Wren**, 60s. Gin Lane herb-woman who set Alice's arm when she was nine and never asked how it broke. She's the one who tells Alice her sister will get better.

### The wound under the sisters

When Alice and Miriam were small, their stepfather drank and kept a belt. Miriam, four years older, made Alice a promise in the dark under their bed: *nothing will ever touch you while I'm here.* Then the belt came out, and Miriam didn't move. She never once moved. What she did was teach Alice to count: the posts of the fence outside the window, one to eight, over and over, until it was over. "Eight posts and he's done." That's the counting game. The sisters have played it as a joke for fifteen years, and neither of them has ever said where it came from. The stepfather went into the river one winter, drunk, and nobody looked for him.

Alice keeps all of it **behind the eightfold fence**, her own phrase, the one she uses when anyone gets close: *that's behind the fence.* It's the root of her lie. She learned that love is holding on, because nobody held the line for her.

Miriam carried it differently: guilt, all of it, never said. When the Lost One comes through on the bridge and puts Miriam on like a coat, it gets the guilt with the memories. A creature that has never felt anything human now has fifteen years of a sister's shame, and no idea what to do with it.

**How the show handles it.** The abuse is never shown. It lives in sound (a buckle, a floorboard), in Alice's flinch, and in the counting. It's about what it did to two girls, not about the man.

**The irony at the heart of the sisters' story.** Miriam knew she owed Alice an apology and never gave it. The Lost One isn't Miriam, but she gives it, and that's how Alice knows her sister is dead.

### How the season stays producible

Morgana is a practical performer in prosthetics from the waist up, with CG for the ice and fire and the full nine feet saved for Eps 5, 8 and 10. The pilot shows only her hand. The wolves are shown the way the flash-bursts show everything: one strobe frame at a time, so we render frames, not sequences, and the audience's imagination does the rest. The walls are a glow on the horizon and a matte painting; their failure is shown through gauges, valves, frost on the inside of a window and a frost line creeping up one particular street, never wide shots of walls coming down. Ep 4 (a hearing room and a dock) and Ep 7's Pavilion (one interior, a hundred extras and a song) are the season's contained episodes, which pays for the Newgate break-out and Tyburn hill.

### The themes, argued from every side

Protection as control is the spine, and five more themes run underneath it. Every storyline argues a position on several of them at once. The season never says which answer is right: the outcomes refuse to line up into a moral, some of the wrong people win, and the viewer decides.

**1. Protection as control.** Every adult in this show hurts someone to keep them safe.

## Episode 1 — "The Engagement"

**49 DAYS TO FIRST SNOW.** 2 November 1889. **Focus:** one night, two parties: the Harding ball above, the glim run below, until the light flickers. **Set piece:** the Works blackout. **Sting:** the man who shoots the saboteur is the hero's best friend, and the saboteur is the hero's brother.

*Logline:* On the night his engagement is announced, Nathaniel Harding's brother dies at the family Works, his best friend fires the shot nobody can ever know about, and across the river a girl freezes solid on a bridge, then opens her eyes in the morgue.

| Character | Want | Obstacle | Solution | Twist |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Nathaniel | To show his father the leap-gauntlet and earn a place in the work | Eldgard sees only Jamie. Inside, Nathaniel believes he's the spare | Demonstrates it at the ball anyway | Humiliated in public, then his brother is dead by morning |
| Alice | To pass her first Vykra test and to find out who Miriam keeps sneaking off to | The contract is a Harding. Her sister is lying to her | Gets into the ball as a lamp-girl | Finds Miriam frozen on the bridge, then sees her wake up |
| Nixon | To prove the charity-school boy deserves the captaincy | First command, a dark Works, a masked saboteur | Takes the shot | It's Jamie, and Eldgard makes him the keeper of the lie |
| Jamie | To break the chain and run with Miriam | His father, the Guard, and his own blood | Cuts the lock with a glass blade and his hand | Shot by his brother's best friend, left alone with his father |

### Scenes

**Cold open**

1. **Tyburn, 1489. Night, no snow, but frost.** Over black, a young man's voice, flat and exact, like a clerk reading a ledger: "Edric Harding. Nine years old. The year of our Lord fourteen hundred and eighty-nine." We won't know whose voice it is for ten episodes. The Crown's gallows field, a hundred empty frames and one old thief turning on his rope. *Creak. Creak.* Below the ground, in a pit lined with iron, something vast breathes behind the lantern glare. We never see her face. We see a hand of blue ice the size of a cartwheel with an iron hook through it, and we hear her singing: a wordless lullaby, low and patient, a tune the audience will hear again without knowing where. ALISTAIR HARDING, wolf-crest ring on his hand, opens her wrist, and her blood runs green into a lantern. *Fwhumph.* The first gallowglass light. A boy of nine holds the lantern up for his father, solemn and proud to be useful. In the dark behind them a tiny girl made of frost slips out of the pit. Alistair sees her, and steps aside to let her pass. He has what he wanted. She runs into the white dark, and the wolves follow her. At the treeline a young woman in Harding colours, nineteen, a cousin, has seen all of it. She backs into the trees before anyone sees her face. Out of the pit a ring of frost starts across the field, slow as a tide, past the gallows frames and into the trees. The singing doesn't stop.

**Act One**

2. **Under Gin Lane, the Ring tunnels.** 1889. Through a street grating overhead, children skip rope on Gin Lane to the oldest rhyme in London: *"Green light, green light, who's in the cellar tonight?"* Nobody below listens. ALICE and MIRIAM BOUNDS run glim through the lamp-mains. Alice splices a live Verdance line to black out a rival dealer's den, and shows off doing it. The buyer, mid-deal, tilts his head: "Hear that? Crying. Always crying with this batch." Then he pulls his belt loose to tie off a leaking pipe, and the buckle rings on the iron. Alice is across the tunnel before she knows she's moved. Miriam sees it, and looks away. Then she steals an orange from him, tosses it to Alice and laughs, and the moment is gone. It's Alice's twentieth birthday. She waits all scene for Miriam to say so. Miriam doesn't; she's somewhere else. Miriam wears a ring on a cord under her collar. *Therefore* Alice clocks it and says nothing.
3. **Harding Works, the gauntlet shop.** NATHANIEL straps on his leap-gauntlet prototype and fires the booster. *Kshhhh-THOOM.* He crosses the foundry in one jump and lands in a coal bin. BYRON, the butler, picks soot from his hair: "Very graceful, sir. The coal agrees." He has been the only audience for these for years. Nathaniel plans to show his father tonight.
4. **Harding House, the study.** ELDGARD and JAMIE. Eldgard: after tonight Jamie begins "his preparation." Jamie: "I know what the Tithe is." Eldgard pours two drinks. Jamie leaves his untouched, and on his way out sets his pocket watch on his father's desk, wound, as if he won't need to know the time where he's going. Eldgard doesn't notice it until much later. *But* neither man says the word that matters.
5. **Lantern Guard tower, Charing Cross.** NIXON HARGRAVE, 23, is made Lantern-Captain, the youngest in the corps, and swears the oath with his hand on the glass: "Light the dark. Keep the flame." Eldgard, his patron since the charity school, pins the green-glass lantern to his collar: "My family looks after its own." COLE PRUETT, 17, a Wick, tries to salute and smacks himself in the eye.
6. **Vykra laundry, Shadwell.** Steam, lye, women folding knives into sheets. MOTHER GRISE gives Alice her first contract in a sealed envelope, from a client Grise won't name: the second Harding son. "Get close. Report everything." Across the steam, KIT MARROW, twenty-one, Grise's best knife and the girl Alice learned the trade beside, watches the envelope change hands: long and rangy, a shaved nape under a copper plait, a laundress's sleeves rolled over forearms cross-hatched with old practice cuts, a man's waistcoat, and soft-soled boots that never make a sound on the wet boards. She wanted that envelope. She smiles at Alice anyway, because Vykra smile. Grise closes her contract book. A ribbon marks the page before Alice's. Alice has wanted this for three years. *Therefore* she has to get into the ball tonight.

**Act Two**

7. **Blackwood Bridge stair, dusk.** Alice tails Miriam and watches her climb into a coach and kiss JAMIE HARDING. A Harding! *But* the ball starts in an hour, and the Vykra don't give second tests. A Harding tram rattles past below the stair with an enamel bill along its flank: HARDING LIGHT. WARM AS A MOTHER'S ARMS. Past the rooftops the east wall glows green against the dusk, the way it always has. Nobody looks at it.
8. **Harding House, the ballroom.** Arrivals under a thousand green chandeliers. LORD LAMBRICK, the coal baron, with his daughter TAMARA, who carries a cipher wheel inside her fan. Hawke, on the death of James Harding.* A bullet hole in front of the Crown means a Crown inquiry, and a Crown inquiry goes down the core stair and finds the Tithe waiting for her last son.
4. **Guard tower, the armoury.** A Crown order arrives from Vane after the market deaths: the Frost Killer is to be shot on sight. Cole is back, bandaged, two fingers short, desperate to be useful, and Nixon puts him on the duty roster to give him back his pride. Then Tamara comes to Nixon, the only man in London she trusts. Ajax was in her room, and her father paid Ajax the night Jamie died. She stands close. Nixon wants to say three things and says none of them. "I'll put a man on your door, miss." "I'd rather you were the man." Silence.
5. **Glasshouse workbench.** Alice's Vykra intelligence: Ajax collects from the coal docks on Thursday night. Nathaniel builds her a real gauntlet and fits it to her hand strap by strap. "Hold still, Bounds." She does, which she has never done for anyone. A love scene with no kissing in it.
6. **The glasshouse, late.** Alice is asleep at the workbench, the new gauntlet still strapped to her hand. The Lost One sits with Nathaniel by the stove, and out of nowhere, in Miriam's voice, gives him something of his brother's: "He talked about you. On the bridge stair, when he was cold. He said you'd fly before you ever learned to walk, and that you were the only one of them worth saving." Nathaniel has spent his whole life believing he was the spare. "He said that?" "To me." She's careful not to say who *me* is. He doesn't think to ask. He sits with it until the stove goes out: the first time anyone has told him he was worth something without wanting something back, and it came out of a dead woman's mouth.

**Act Two**

7. **Vykra laundry.** Grise: "Bring me the frost girl." Alice: "No." Kit holds out the scissors, handle first. Grise cuts the Vykra knot out of Alice's glove with one snip. "Then you're no one's." Alice walks out into the rain with nowhere to belong. Under the awning, she takes his handkerchief out of her coat and folds it into the glove, where the knot used to be.
8. **Coal docks, Thursday night, set piece.** Nathaniel and Alice ambush Ajax among the coal barges. The Black Gauntlet hits like a steam hammer, *WHUNK*, and Nathaniel recognises his own stolen prototype in it. He's losing. Alice blinds Ajax with a flash-burst from her new gauntlet, and together they hook him onto a crane and swing him out over the black river.
9. **The crane.** Ajax, upside down, laughs. "Your brother paid me. Lambrick paid me too, but your brother paid double. I cut the outer grid. I never touched him." Then: "Your father went down after. Ask the Guard who fired." Alice: "Pull him up. He's the only witness we have." Nathaniel's hand is on the lever, and two true things pull at it: the man who helped kill his brother, or the one voice that can put Lambrick in the dock. "He's Jamie's grave," he says, and lets go. Ajax drops into the Thames. *Splash.* The Thames keeps him, for now.
10. **Harding House, the servants' stair at three in the morning, then the back stairs.** Nathaniel can't sleep, so he waits where nobody waits: at the foot of the servants' stair that runs down to the Works passage. At three his father comes up it with a lantern, frost on his cuffs in a house that has never been cold, and a face Nathaniel has never seen on him. Caught on a stair in his shirtsleeves, Eldgard has to lie standing up, and he does it well. Nathaniel: "Jamie paid Lambrick's man. Why?" Eldgard: "Your brother was ill. Melancholy. He wanted to destroy this house." It's half true, which is how Eldgard lies. Then, before Nathaniel can ask about the frost, the bait he has wanted all his life: "After the hearing I'll take you into the work. All of it." Nathaniel takes it, and forgets to ask. On the back stairs afterwards, Josephine asks Byron where Nathaniel goes at night. Byron lies for the boy. It's the first time he has ever lied to her.
11. **Newgate, cell 1417.** Nixon, alone, in civilian coat, with the slip. The prisoner is an old man, TOBIAS MARSH, seventy if a day, in the dark at the end of the debtors' wing, picking at a string he calls his harp. Nixon: "Jamie Harding came here every Sunday. Why?" The old man hums, then pinches Nixon's cheek through the bars and cackles. "Marian's chin! Marian's chin!" Nixon goes still. "What did you say?" "Seven in the Orchard, ripe for the fall. One on the tree for the lantern's call. Thirteen steps down where the cold water drips, count the apples, kiss the stones' lips." Nixon grabs the bars: "Enough. Why was Jamie Harding visiting you? Tell me, you old fool." Marsh only cocks his head and smiles. "He cried too, you know. Same place in the song." Then he turns his back and goes back to his harp. Nixon leaves with a nursery rhyme and a cold feeling he can't name.
12. **Glasshouse.** Felix tracks Alice there. The Lost One offers him an orange and apologises for his hand, because she remembers. He sees a frightened girl. He was already listed to testify about the bullet hole in Jamie's coat. Now he'll say more: "Someone made her, and I'll say so in front of the Crown." *Therefore* he's now the most dangerous man in London. Outside, under the garden wall, Josephine has heard every word. She has known for a week where her son goes at night, whatever Byron tells her. She says nothing to her husband. A Frost Killer dragged out of the Harding glasshouse takes her son to the dock beside her, so the girl will have to be caught somewhere else.

**Act Three**

13. **Parliament, committee room, the hearing.** Lambrick argues that the Hardings are hiding something under the Works, and reads the Works' own feed figures into the record: the east wall draws a tenth less than it did in October. Eldgard is calm. Vane presides, and never once asks what. Nathaniel sits beside his father. Tamara is in the gallery, Nixon's Lanterns are at the doors, and a veiled woman sits in the back row. At the recess she crosses to the stand with a glass of water: "You look pale, Inspector." Felix drinks.
14. **The witness stand, set piece.** Felix: "The deaths at the market were caused by a girl who should be dead, and the Harding Works—" He coughs. Frost spreads across his lips and his eyes go white. He dies on the stand. The cry goes up: "The Frost Killer is here!" Doors slam. Nixon's Lanterns fire flash-bursts into the ceiling to stop the stampede. Vane orders the Frost Killer hunted "with every lamp in London." The vote is postponed, and sympathy swings toward the Hardings. In the crush on the gallery stairs, Tamara sees the veiled woman's glove, with a Vykra knot stitched inside it. A needle drops on the stair. Tamara pockets it. That night, alone in his office, Vane takes Felix Hawke's case file out of the Yard's dispatch box, reads the first page, and feeds it to his grate a sheet at a time.
15. **Glasshouse.** Nathaniel brings the news: Felix was killed by frost. Alice looks at the Lost One, who has been here with her all day. She knows the poison. "Winter's kiss. That's Vykra." *Therefore* somebody is framing her sister.
16. **Guard tower, the roof, then the armoury.** Nathaniel tells Nixon what Ajax said: ask the Guard who fired. Nixon goes very still. "Find me the name, Nix." "I'll find it." The rifles came back from Nathaniel's shop this week with their logs bound into the cases, and that night he finds it: his own rifle in the discharge log, one round, 2 November. He tears out the page and burns it in the stove. From the bunk-room door, Cole watches him do it, and later copies the page from memory into his diary.
17. **Vykra laundry.** Tamara puts the needle on Grise's counting table. "One of yours killed a policeman in front of the Crown. I want to know who. And I want in." Grise looks at her for a long time, then smiles. Alice was cut out yesterday, and Tamara is the new girl.

**Tag**

18. **Harding House, Josephine's dressing room.** Josephine lifts her veil, peels off gloves with a Vykra knot inside, and washes frost-dust from her fingertips in a porcelain bowl. The water skins over with ice. In the mirror, Byron is standing in the doorway.

## Episode 5 — "Lantern Night"

**33 DAYS TO FIRST SNOW.** The midseason turn. **Focus:** the heist into the core. **Set piece:** four separate plans on one festival night, ending with every lamp in London blazing white and bursting at once, and a crush on Westminster Bridge. (The city goes fully dark twice in this season: for an hour in the pilot, and for good in the finale. Lantern Night is the other thing light can do: it screams.) **Sting:** the light is a woman in chains, his father has always known, and the door is locked by his best friend. What the chain costs, and who is next to pay it, stay in the dark.

*Logline:* On the night London celebrates its light, Nathaniel breaks into his own family's Works to see what Jamie died for. He finds a chained mother bleeding green, learns his father has always known, and hears the door lock behind him, on Nixon's order.

| Character | Want | Obstacle | Solution | Twist |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Nathaniel | The truth about the core | His father, his best friend, his own hope that his father is good | Byron's key, Tamara's code, Alice's hands | London's light is her blood and his father has always known, and he chooses to stay with him anyway |
| Alice | To get her sister what she needs, and to keep her | The Lost One has her own plan | Goes in beside her | Morgana: "You have my daughter's hand. Give it back." |
| The Lost One | To free her mother tonight | The chain burns her, and Alice holds her back | Freezes the whole core | Her mother's pain bursts every lamp in London, people die in the crush, and she doesn't stop wanting it |
| Nixon | To obey the man who made him | The order is to trap his best friend | Warns Nathaniel with their boyhood whistle, then seals the door | Nathaniel sees his face through the glass |
| Tamara | To prove her mind is worth something | Alice's contempt, and locks that reset mid-heist | Recodes the inner door by ear | Becomes Alice's equal, and her rival |

### Scenes

**Cold open**

1. **Tyburn, the same winter, 1489.** The same voice over black, still unplaced: "The Hargraves of the Orchard. Their names are scratched on a wall under Tyburn." The Hargrave raid. Torches, young cousins of the Harding blood, and leading them the girl of nineteen from the treeline in the pilot, reaching the iron chain with a hammer. Alistair cuts her down at the lock. Somebody in the dark screams her name: "Marian!" By dawn the empty gallows field from the pilot is full: three thousand Hargraves on Harding timber, and their wives and children walked down thirteen steps into the dark. The heist we're about to watch has been tried before, and it failed.

**Act One**

2. **Harding Works, the Upper Gallery.** Eldgard brings Nathaniel into "the work" at last: a cathedral of glass vats where engineers "grow gallowglass from Tyburn iron." Nathaniel's joy is real and painful to watch. He asks about the east-wall gauges. Eldgard doesn't look up: "The output dips at the end of a cycle. My father saw it in 1839. It comes back." Then: "At the solstice I'll show you the rest."
3. **Glasshouse.** Nathaniel: "There's nothing down there but vats." The Lost One: "He showed you a nice room. My mother isn't in a nice room." Nathaniel defends his father; Alice defends her sister. "He's my father, Bounds." "She's my sister, Harding." Their first fight, and it's about family.
4. **Harding House, the silver pantry.** Byron gives Nathaniel his old Guard key on a black ribbon. Jamie borrowed it in October, had it copied, and gave it back with a note Byron has kept in his pocket ever since: If I don't come up, give it to Nat. Byron guarded the core thirty years ago: "I heard her every night. I stopped hearing it when I stopped letting myself." He lights himself back down the servants' stair with a tallow candle. He hasn't carried a green lamp since the core, and nobody in the house has ever asked why. He keeps quiet about Josephine's hands. Not yet.
5. **Vykra laundry.** Grise tests her new girl: a Works lock cipher, solved in four minutes. Grise lets slip that the inner codes change on Lantern Night. Tamara is welcomed with tea and a knife. For the first time in her life she belongs somewhere because of her mind.
6. **The Guard tower roofs, afternoon.** Lantern Night is hours away, and for the length of one scene they're nine years old again. Nathaniel turns up with two prototype boosters he isn't supposed to have, and he and Nixon race them across the Charing Cross rooftops, chimney to chimney, *kshhh-THOOM, kshhh-THOOM*, talking in the whistles they made up as boys: *left*, *mine*, *you're losing*. Nixon wins. Nathaniel lands in a coal scuttle on the last roof, and Nixon laughs so hard he has to sit down. It's the best afternoon either of them has had since the engagement. Nathaniel goes home to dress. Nixon stays on the roof and takes out the order that has been in his breast pocket all afternoon, in Eldgard's hand: *My son will come tonight. Let him through. Seal the core behind him. The girl doesn't leave.* Underneath, in answer to a question Nixon never wrote down: *He'll be with me.*
7. **Glasshouse, planning.** Whimsy. A model of the Works built out of teacups and oranges; the Lost One eats the core. Tamara arrives with the code, invited by Nathaniel. Alice and Tamara meet: the fiancée and the undercity girl, the new Vykra and the cut one. Every line is polite and every line is a knife. Nathaniel is the only one who doesn't notice.

**Act Two**

8. **Lantern Night, the Strand.** Fireworks over a blazing green city, crowds, the Lord Mayor, and jubilee banners over the Strand: HARDING LIGHT. WARM AS A MOTHER'S ARMS. In the crowd, Lambrick, and beside him Ajax, very alive, with a new scar from brow to jaw: the river police cut him out of a barge's anchor chain, gauntlet and all, and Lambrick paid their fee. He's eating an apple. Behind them, a coal-mob. Their plan: cut the outer grid during the finale and let London see Harding light fail before the vote.
9. **Thames outfall, the heist begins.** The pipe is full of black water. The Lost One freezes it into a glass road and they walk inside the river. Jamie's key opens the first grate.
10. **The gantries.** Nixon, on the high walk, sees them pass. Orders say let them through. He gives the whistle they invented at nine years old, *turn back*. Nathaniel hears it, smiles because it's Nix, and keeps going.
11. **The Strand, finale.** Ajax's crew cuts the outer grid. The East End goes dark in the middle of the fireworks. Inside the Works, every lock resets, and the heisters are trapped between two doors with the Guard alarm ringing.
12. **The shaft.** Tamara recodes the inner door by ear, listening to the tumblers, while Alice holds her by the belt over a crystal shaft four hundred feet deep. Neither of them says thank you. *Click.* It opens.
13. **The core.** They go down, and we see her whole for the first time. MORGANA: nine feet of ice and green fire, iron hooks through her shoulders and hips, her blood running green along channels into London. Her arms are spread wide in the hooks, like someone about to hold a child. She is singing, and it's the Weeping: the Lantern Hymn's tune, with none of the words.

**Act Three**

14. **The chain.** "Little frost. You came." The Lost One grabs the chain, and for one second her whole face changes: every song Miriam ever knew comes back into her at once. Then it burns her mittens to smoke. Alice pulls her back. Morgana, to Alice: "You have my daughter's hand. Give it back." Alice, holding tighter: "She's my sister. Whatever came back in her, my sister's under it." Morgana tilts her great head and looks at Alice with something terribly like pity. She doesn't correct her. The Lost One doesn't either. Then, to Nathaniel, almost gently: "You came down these stairs once, little Harding. And your brother is not dead. He is cold."
15. **The lock.** Nathaniel touches the chain, and it tightens. Morgana screams, and across London the lamps surge brighter. His blood feeds it, and for one frame, in the surge, he sees snow, and a man standing very still in it. Then it's gone, and he'll tell himself it was the dream. Jamie's glass blade is still stuck in the lock where he left it, and his diagram suddenly makes sense.
16. **The core.** Eldgard steps out of the dark; he's been waiting. "Now you know what your brother knew. Some of it." He doesn't defend it. A Harding chained her four hundred years ago, and every Harding since has kept the hooks in, because four million people are warm. "I didn't put her there, and I can't let her go. If that chain breaks, the light dies, the walls go with it, and it takes something from us. Something always does." Nathaniel: "What did Jamie know that I don't?" "At the solstice I'll show you the rest." It's the second time today he has said it, and Nathaniel files that away.
17. **The core door.** It seals with a hiss, iron on glass. Behind the glass is Nixon, following orders, his lips moving on the oath he swore on the night he was made captain, *Light the dark. Keep the flame*, over and over, loud enough in his own head to drown out whatever Nathaniel is hearing. Nathaniel: "Nix!" Nixon doesn't look away, and that's worse.
18. **The core, escape.** The Lost One, desperate, lets go of the cold. The core freezes around her mother, and Morgana screams, the way she screamed when Nathaniel touched the chain, only worse. Her blood flares through every channel at once. Across London, on Lantern Night, every lamp blazes white for one heartbeat and bursts. *Pop-pop-pop-pop*, street after street, a wave of shattering glass under the fireworks. The Lost One flinches as if she heard every one. Alice blows the door with her gauntlet. Tamara drags the Lost One into the outfall. Alice reaches for Nathaniel. He stays: "I need to hear the rest." She leaves him there.
19. **Westminster Bridge.** Glass rains on the festival crowd from the bridge lamps, then only fireworks for light, then a panicking crowd and a crush. Cole's squad hold a barricade with flash-bursts; people still die against the railings. Out past Shadwell and Kensington, two sectors of the heat walls flicker and go grey for the first time in two hundred years, and the outerland cold walks into the streets behind them. Lambrick, on a coal cart: "Remember who kept you warm!"
20. **The core, after.** Father and son, alone with the prisoner. Nathaniel: "She said Jamie isn't dead. She said he's cold." Eldgard doesn't look at Morgana when he answers. "She tells every Harding whatever will bring him to the lock. She told me my sister was still singing." He says it like a man pressing on an old scar. Then, for the first time, he looks at her. "I didn't put her in those hooks. My father didn't. But I'm the one who keeps them in. Help me build something that holds her without bleeding her, and the walls never go grey again." It's the best offer anyone has ever made Nathaniel, and the cruellest, because it's half true. He can't tell which half. He says yes. He chooses his father, and he puts the word *cold* away where he keeps the weeping in the floor.

**Tag**

21. **Gin Lane rooftop, the black city.** Alice and the Lost One, soaked and shaking. The Lost One: "I'm going to free her. Even if all of them freeze." Alice takes her burned hand anyway. Below them, London is lit in patches, every third lamp a broken stump.

## Episode 6 — "Coal"

**28 DAYS TO FIRST SNOW.** **Focus:** a city in the cold, Byron, and the aunt who ran. **Set piece:** the Pimlico gasworks explosion, five plans meeting in one tunnel. **Sting:** Byron dies before he can say what he came to say, so Nathaniel bluffs it out of his mother.

*Logline:* With two walls grey, Lambrick relighting one of them on coal, and Harding light failing, Alice and the Lost One try to reach the core through the gas tunnels on the night of the Charter vote. Ajax has rigged those tunnels, Nixon is waiting in them, Nathaniel runs in after them, and Byron runs in after him.

| Character | Want | Obstacle | Solution | Twist |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Nathaniel | To find "another way" with his father: the Heartglass | He doesn't know what the Heartglass needs | Builds it anyway, then runs to warn Alice | Byron dies pulling him out with half a sentence and a needle in his fist, and Nathaniel bluffs the rest out of his mother |
| Alice | To get the Lost One an hour beside the chain, the one place her memories came back | Tamara's warning: the tunnels are rigged | Goes anyway | The Lost One saves four children and forgets Alice's name |
| Tamara | To save Nathaniel, and herself | Her father plans to blow the Works' conduit during the vote | Warns Nathaniel in their childhood cipher | Her warning is what brings Nathaniel, and so Byron, into the blast |
| Nixon | To take the girl alive, and buy time with Cole | Cole's ultimatum, Alice's gauntlet | Ambushes in the tunnel | Carries Cole out of the fire, and owes him everything |
| Eldgard | Someone to tell him he did what he had to | His aunt Hester, who ran in 1839 and won't come home | Goes to her himself | She asks if the boy knows yet |
| Josephine | To keep Byron silent without killing him | Thirty years of love | Lets him live | Sends Eldgard the tip that sends Byron to his death |

### Scenes

**Cold open**

1. **Kensington, under the west wall, the night after Lantern Night.** A furnace vault nobody has opened since 1700: brick arches, cold fireboxes, iron valve-wheels as tall as a man. LORD LAMBRICK takes off his own coat, which no one has ever seen him do, and puts his shoulder to the main wheel beside his engineers. *Grrrnk.* Coal roars in the old fireboxes, *WHUMPH*, and through the grating overhead the crowd in the street watches the west wall flush red-gold and cheers his name. While they're cheering, he walks to the far end of the vault, where a second wheel feeds the east wall, Gin Lane's. He turns it shut, runs a chain through the spokes, snaps a padlock on it and puts the key in his waistcoat pocket. He doesn't say a word about it to anyone. In the morning his agents start buying Gin Lane leases at a shilling apiece.

**Act One**

2. **London, the cold.** Since Lantern Night half the lamps in London are broken glass, the rest come and go, and two sectors of the walls are grey. Kensington is warm, and the coal carts with LAMBRICK on the side get cheered in the street. The east wall stays grey. Gin Lane, Shadwell, Whitechapel: nobody there votes, and nobody relights it. Prices chalked up: doubled, then tripled. A child asleep in a Gin Lane doorway, Alice's doorway, with frost in her eyelashes, and a constable looking away. By morning she isn't asleep. Grim and quiet.
3. **Harding Works.** Eldgard and Nathaniel design the **Heartglass**. Eldgard pitches it as mercy: a resonance cage that will hold her without the hooks, so the bleeding stops and the walls run steady. Father and son at one bench, finishing each other's equations. It's everything Nathaniel ever wanted. Eldgard: "We'll need something of her blood to tune it." Nathaniel assumes he means Morgana.
4. **Gin Lane, a borrowed room.** Wren said the memories would come back. They're going the other way. Every time the cold comes out of her it costs one of Miriam's memories, and tonight the Lost One asks, "When's your birthday, Ali?" The same day Miriam forgot it, and the same day the Lost One remembered it with an orange. Alice: "You knew it once." The Lost One, frightened, grabbing Alice's sleeve: "There's something I have to say to you before it goes. About him. About the belt." Alice: "After." "There might not be an after." "*After*, Mim." The fence holds, and the Lost One watches the thing she needs to say slipping further away. Then, harder, because she's frightened: "If I free my mother, I'll be strong again." Alice: "If you free her, the light goes out and people die." The Lost One: "People die now. I watched one on your step." Then, quieter: "At the chain it all came back. Your birthday. The songs. For one second I had all of her. If I could sit by her an hour, I'd have Mim back for you." It's the only offer anyone has made Alice since the bridge. "You sit by her. You don't touch the lock." "Yes, Ali." The second lie, and this time Alice half knows it.
5. **Past Barnet, outside the north wall, afternoon.** The Harding carriage goes out through the north gate, and inside a hundred yards the season changes. Inside the walls it's a raw November. Out here, in the outerlands, it's always November, and worse: hedges white with rime, a pond frozen to the bottom, a rook working at something dead in a furrow. One last green heater-post stands at the mouth of a lane, guttering, *bzzzt, bzzzt*, warm for six feet. ELDGARD leaves the carriage there and walks the rest. At the end of the lane is a stone cottage with a slate roof and thin smoke, and in the yard WALTER PELL, eighty, a stonemason in a leather apron with a cold pipe in his teeth, is cutting a name into a headstone, *tink, tink, tink*, between coughs. HESTER PELL opens the door before Eldgard knocks. She is seventy-seven, born a Harding, his father's younger sister. She has the family's height and jaw and grey eyes, and she stoops under her own lintel. Her white hair is in one braid pinned flat round her head like a crown nobody gave her. She wears a brown wool dress gone soft at the elbows and a man's grey cardigan with the sleeves shoved up, sawdust in the wool, over big chapped hands, a brass wedding band worn thin, and hobnailed boots unlaced at the top. "You've got old." "It's been fifty years." "Fifty this winter. I can count too." Inside: one room, a wood fire, mended plates, a candle on the sill not yet lit, a low woodpile, and a dresser with a leg missing; you can guess where it went. Nothing green anywhere. "The heaters won't last the winter. Come back with me." "You brought a carriage and left room for my trunk." "I hoped you'd be sensible." "You hoped I'd already agreed." She puts another piece of wood on the fire, and he watches it catch, *fssh, pop*, like a man who hasn't watched a wood fire since he was a boy. When the doctor said she would never carry, her father stopped seating her near him at dinner. "No use to the table. It took me twenty years to see that was the kindest thing that house ever did me. I've never had a child I had to hand to anybody." He gives her the accusation he came with. "You left the week before the snow." "I did." "Otty was nineteen." "I know how old she was. I watched the walls go grey that autumn, and I knew what it meant before Father said a word. I left because I was afraid. You stayed, and called it duty." She doesn't claim the better side of it: other people kept paying, and she has known that every winter for fifty years. She just won't live with it in a house that runs on it. Then: "Does the boy know yet?" "I'm finding another way." "That wasn't what I asked." Silence, and the kettle ticking toward the boil, *tk, tk, tk*. He came to bring her in out of the cold, and to be told he did what he had to. Whether he also came because she is a living Harding, and the chain asks for nothing more than that, he may not admit even to himself; she can see all of it. "I'm not staying because I'm happy, Eldgard. You can't see it, and it's sitting right here by the fire." He goes. Walter lifts two fingers off the chisel to him, a mason's courtesy to a stranger, and Eldgard doesn't return it. At the mouth of the lane the last heater-post gives one more *bzzzt* and goes grey. Behind it, in the cottage window, Hester strikes a match and lights the candle on the sill. *Fssst.* That night Eldgard comes into the Works still in his coat, crosses to the bench where his son is bent over the Heartglass, and puts his hand on Nathaniel's shoulder.
6. **Harding Works, the shop window, midnight.** Alice climbs the Works wall to tell the only person who would understand that her sister has forgotten her birthday. Through the lit glass she sees Nathaniel and Eldgard still at one bench, finishing each other's equations, his father's hand on his shoulder, the Heartglass half-built between them. It's everything he has wanted his whole life, and she can see that it is. She doesn't knock. Her breath fogs the pane. She wipes it clean with her glove, the one with his handkerchief folded inside it, and goes back down the wall. A second later Nathaniel looks up, at a window with nobody in it.
7. **Lambrick House, a dinner party, then the gun room.** Twenty guests, the Crown board's men among them, and the west-wall heat coming up through the floor grilles like applause. Tamara plays the perfect daughter through four courses, pleads a headache, takes a candle up the back stair, and opens the gun-room cabinet where her father keeps what he won't put in the safe. Charges along the Tyburn conduit where it meets his Pimlico gas main, set for the night of the vote, so the Works look rotten to the Crown. Under the plan, a lunacy order with one doctor's signature, Dr Cutler's, and her own name on it, waiting for a second. She learns her price twice in one breath. Footsteps: AJAX, sent up for cigars. She's behind the curtain with the wick pinched out between two fingers, *tss*, and the plan still in her other hand. Ajax takes the cigars, smells hot wax, and stands very still. "Your father says, if you're up here, you're to come down for the pudding." He leaves the door open. She goes down for the pudding, and puts the plan back before breakfast.

**Act Two**

8. **A Covent Garden tea shop.** Alice swallows her pride and asks Tamara, the new Vykra with a coal baron's maps, for a way into the core. Tamara tells her about the charges. The only route in runs through Ajax's tunnels. Tamara: "Then go another night." Alice: "There isn't one. She's forgetting me." Two girls who dislike each other, starting to understand each other. The girl who pours their tea keeps her eyes down and her cap low, and has a knot stitched inside her glove. Alice, the lamp-girl nobody ever looked at, doesn't look at the help either. Tamara does, once, and knows her: Kit, Grise's best knife. She takes it for Grise keeping an eye on her new girl, and says nothing. It isn't Tamara that Grise is watching.
9. **The frozen canal at Little Venice.** They meet in secret under a bridge, snowless cold, their breath smoking between them. He tells her about the Heartglass: "We can save both of them." She tells him the Lost One is forgetting her. When she pulls off a glove to warm her hands, something falls out of it into the frost: his handkerchief, folded small, kept where a Vykra keeps the thing that says whose she is. He picks it up. "You kept it." "Bounds keeps things." For once in his life he doesn't wait to be reached for. He kisses her, and finds out she has been waiting since the ballroom. *Tik. Tik.* Ice ticking off the bridge above them. Then both of them use the kiss to hide the dangerous part: she's going in tonight, and he still doesn't know what the Heartglass is for.
10. **Harding House, the linen room, then the study.** Byron confronts Josephine. "I saw your hands." "I did it for Nathaniel." "You always say that." She has a needle in her sleeve. *Flash:* the core gate, thirty years ago. Young BYRON, a Lantern sergeant on night watch, catches a Vykra girl with a knife sent to kill young Eldgard. It's JOSEPHINE, nineteen, and she's caught because she stopped: behind the gate a woman is weeping, and she listened a breath too long. "Who sent you?" "A coal man's son." He lets her go. A year later she marries the man she was sent to kill, and Byron serves at the wedding with a faded blue ribbon in his tunic pocket, from a different night, fifty years gone. *Back:* she doesn't use the needle. He takes it out of her sleeve himself, the way you'd take scissors off a child, and pockets it. "Thirty years, Josie. I let you go at the gate, and everything since came through it." He leaves. She sits on the linen and doesn't cry. Then a maid brings her a folded note in Grise's hand, bought an hour ago from Kit, the tea-girl in Covent Garden: *Tyburn conduit. Vote night. The girl.* Josephine takes it straight down to her husband's study. "How do you know?" "I'm your wife." Eldgard sends for Nixon. "You stood at my core door and let her walk out of it. Bring her back alive, or bring me your lantern." The Heartglass needs her. The woman who has just spared Byron's life has set the trap he will die in, and neither of them knows it.
11. **Harding Works, then the Harding House hall.** A cipher note from Tamara in their childhood code reaches Nathaniel: *The conduit. Charges. She's going in.* He runs, grabbing the leap-gauntlet as he goes. In the hall, Byron sees him tear out of the yard. He hasn't carried a green lamp since the core. He takes his old Lantern down from its glass case on the wall, still charged after thirty years, and when it lights he hears her again, as clearly as he did on night watch. He follows anyway.

**Act Three**

12. **The Tyburn conduit, set piece.** Alice and the Lost One go down. Ajax's men are lighting fuses in a side drain. Four tunnel children are sleeping in the warm pipe-nook beyond it. Nixon's squad waits in the dark ahead, Cole among them. In the dark, Cole holds up his diary: "Tell Mr. Harding, sir, or I will." Nixon: "After the vote." Cole agrees, because he still believes in him.
13. **The ambush.** Flash-bursts. Nixon corners the Lost One, and she grabs his bare wrist. No frost. He doesn't freeze, the way Nathaniel didn't, and in the strobe and the shouting nobody sees it but the camera. She goes white anyway. She can smell Harding on him the way she smelled it on Nathaniel, and under it the cellar beneath her mother; and one of the names in the song she has heard in every lamp since the bridge belongs to the same blood: "They told you your mother had a fever. She didn't." He lets go as if burned. Alice fights Nixon, gauntlet to gauntlet, in a tunnel too narrow to swing in: the two leads' first real clash. Neither of them wants to win. He's better, and she's losing, so she does the one thing Nathaniel told her never to do with the gauntlet he built her: she runs it past the red. It throws Nixon down the tunnel, *KRAANG*, then dies in her hand, and the backwash scorches her palm through the glove. Now she knows exactly what pushing it costs. Then Nathaniel arrives screaming "Gas!" Ajax, seeing the Guard, lights early. The Lost One's cold meets a flash-burst in the main.
14. **Pimlico, then Parliament.** The gasworks erupts. *WHOOOM.* Fire rolls along the river. A mile downriver the Charter vote is three names from done: Eldgard, Lambrick and Vane in their places, Nathaniel's chair empty, and Josephine looking at it when the windows blow in on the voting lords.
15. **The rubble.** In the smoke, a green flash, then another: Byron's Lantern, finding the boy under the rubble. Byron lifts the beam off Nathaniel and takes its weight himself, and it crushes him. Dying, he has breath for one sentence, and starts the wrong one: "Your mother—" He stops. He looks at the boy he has watched land in coal bins for twenty years, and spends the breath on the other thing instead: "You were always enough." He dies with his hand on Nathaniel's face. When Nathaniel prises his fists open, one holds the blue ribbon. The other holds a Vykra needle with a knot on the cap and frost still on the point.
16. **The river stair.** Nixon carries Cole out of the fire. Alice comes up with the Lost One, who uses the last of her cold to smother the flames around four trapped children. As the cold goes out of her we get half a second of what it takes with it: two small girls under a bed, one whispering *Ali*, the memory frosting over and cracking like a pane. Then she looks at Alice politely, like a stranger. "Thank you, miss." She doesn't know her name. Alice tells herself it's the shock again, worse this time. It's the last time she'll manage to.
17. **Parliament, broken glass.** Vane, bleeding from the temple, announces that the Charter is suspended and the Works pass to a Crown board. Lambrick takes a seat on it: the man who relit the west wall. Eldgard has lost the Works.

**Tag**

18. **Harding House, the music room.** Nathaniel walks in covered in ash. Josephine is at the piano. "Byron's dead." She weeps, really. He sets the needle on the music stand in front of her. "He told me about Inspector Hawke." He's bluffing: Byron got out one word. The music stops. A long beat. "Then you know I love you." It's a confession, and she knows she has just made it.

## Episode 7 — "The Girl Who Isn't"

**21 DAYS TO FIRST SNOW.** **Focus:** two reveals on one day, the sister and the best friend, and a third that Nathaniel digs up himself. **Set piece:** wolves of the White run through Whitechapel, and Nathaniel and Nixon fight side by side an hour after the friendship breaks. **Sting:** Alice chooses the creature anyway, and Tamara hands Nixon a folder with his name on it that he cannot make himself open.

*Logline:* Alice finds the letter Miriam never left her and learns that the thing she has been protecting is not her sister. Nixon finally tells Nathaniel who fired the shot, and Nathaniel opens his brother's coffin and finds his own name in his father's ledger. Then the chain thins enough for the White to come through, and the only way through the night is together.

| Character | Want | Obstacle | Solution | Twist |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Alice | The truth about who she's been protecting | The truth: Miriam was leaving her, and this isn't Miriam | Throws the Lost One out | Runs into a wolf attack for her, and chooses her: "I'm Alice. I'm your sister." |
| The Lost One | To be wanted for herself | She has lost Alice's name, Alice has thrown her out, and Miriam's love for Jamie keeps surfacing in her body | Goes to the place she first came through | Commands the wolves in front of the city, and becomes the Frost Witch |
| Nixon | To put the lie down | Nathaniel's love for him | Confesses at their boyhood spot | Is handed a folder with his name on it, and carries it a week unopened |
| Nathaniel | To know what his father did, and what he made | Every adult he loves is lying | Opens Jamie's coffin himself, then corners Eldgard with a fistful of slag | His brother's coffin holds no body, his own name is in the Keeper's ledger under Jamie's, and Alice tells him what the Heartglass he built is for |
| Tamara | To stop her father, and to stop being polite | A wedding in seven days | Takes the seized Harding papers and gives Nixon his file | Kisses Nixon a week before she marries his best friend |

### Scenes

**Cold open**

1. **Gin Lane, 2 November, morning.** Miriam's last day, from Miriam's side. She is radiant, sick in a basin, and hand on her belly. Alice is asleep on the other side of the bed. Miriam starts a letter: *Ali, about him, when we were small—* She stares at it a long time. She scores it out until the paper tears. She starts again, and this time writes it fast: three short lines we don't get to read. She means to leave it on Alice's pillow, loses her nerve, and sews it into her coat lining. Then she pulls on the red mittens.

**Act One**

2. **Gin Lane, a borrowed room.** Afternoon, and frost on the inside of the window: the east wall has been grey for twelve days. Alice mends the Lost One's coat, burned in the gasworks, and her needle catches on something in the lining. Folded paper, Miriam's hand. She reads it twice. *I'm leaving with him. I'm sorry, Ali. Don't follow me.* Miriam was leaving her and never meant to say goodbye. Then the door bangs. The Lost One, home from the market with an armful of oranges she is enormously proud of, chattering about a man who sells hot chestnuts out of a drum. She doesn't use Alice's name; she hasn't since the gasworks. Alice puts the letter in her apron pocket, smiles, and peels her an orange. She says nothing. She says nothing all evening, and we watch her not say it.
3. **The same room. The peak of the sisters' story.** Late, one candle. Alice holds out the letter. "You were leaving." The Lost One, who lost Alice's name in the gasworks, still knows this face and still knows this hurt. "Yes." And the fence comes down, because Alice is too angry to hold it up: "You always left. You left me under that bed. Every time. You promised nothing would touch me, and then you counted fence posts while he—" She can't finish. Fifteen years, and she has finally said it out loud, to the only person who was there.

    The Lost One has been waiting four weeks to be let in. She has the guilt, and it's the last thing of Miriam's left in her, because the cold took the birthdays and the songs and the name and couldn't get its teeth into this. She kneels on the floor so she's lower than Alice, the way you'd kneel to a child under a bed. "I should have stood in front of you. Every time. I'm sorry. I was so frightened of him that I let you be frightened alone, and I've hated myself every day since." Then the thing Miriam never could have said: "If he came through that door now, I'd stand in front of you. I'd let him freeze."

    It is the apology Alice has wanted her whole life, word for word, and it's perfect. That's how she knows. Miriam couldn't have said it. Miriam *flinched*. Alice, barely a breath: "Say my name." The Lost One opens her mouth, and there's nothing there. A long silence. "You're not her."

    "No." Plainly: Morgana's daughter, who came through on the bridge, wearing Miriam's body and Miriam's memories like a coat. Alice has known since the core that something else came back in her sister. What she built her whole theory to avoid knowing is whether Miriam is still under it. "Is she in there? Under you?" "No. She was gone before I opened her eyes. I'm sorry. She was going to leave you, Ali. She told me on the bridge. She was frightened, and it wasn't of you." Then the real horror, said gently, as a gift: "I'm not sorry for her. I'm sorry for me. I love you. I don't know whose that is anymore. I think it's mine."

    Alice looks at the thing that has been her sister for four weeks, the one that remembered her birthday, peeled her an orange and just gave her the only apology she has ever been given, and understands that her real sister never loved her this well. "Get out." The door closes on her.

4. **Harding House, breakfast.** Eldgard sits silent and diminished without his Works. Josephine runs the table now: the wedding is in seven days, and the Lambrick money will buy the Works back. Nathaniel: "Or what, Mother? You'll poison me?" Josephine, buttering toast: "If I had to choose between you and London, I chose a long time ago." Nathaniel stands. "If anything happens to the frost girl, I'll stand up in Parliament and say who poisoned Felix Hawke." She doesn't stop buttering. "Then it had better not happen from my hands."
5. **The core, under Crown control.** Vane and Lambrick tour it with Tamara on her father's arm. On the stair down, Lambrick, quietly, the only time he talks to her as if she were a person: "Your mother hated the green. Said it made everyone at dinner look drowned. I kept her room on coal till the end." Then he looks up at Morgana. There's no horror in his face, only arithmetic: "She could be bled faster." The man who kept one room warm out of love would freeze a city for the same reason, and calls both of them providing. Lambrick picks up the folio after her, reads the same line, and smiles: put the daughter in the chain, and the Crown never needs a Harding again. On the clerk's desk sits Eldgard's seized Heartglass folio. Tamara reads one line: *Vessel: White-born.*

**Act Two**

6. **Blackwood Bridge, fog.** The Lost One, thrown out, walks to the place she first came through, and does the only thing her body knows how to do with being sent away: she cries. This time the tears don't freeze on her face. They freeze the air. *Tink. Tink. Krrrk.* The thin place on the bridge cracks like a pane of glass, and Alice's "Get out" has opened a door. Howls in the fog. Wolves of the White, white-furred and blue-eyed, pad out of nothing and bow to her. That's the most dangerous thing that could happen.
7. **The Guard tower armoury before dawn, then the roof, their boyhood spot.** The rifles are back in Nathaniel's shop for the cold-weather refit, and he's checking the bound logs against the Works' ammunition audit, because that's what an engineer does at four in the morning when he can't sleep. Captain's rifle: thirty rounds issued, twenty-nine accounted for. The binding has a stub where a page was cut out close to the thread. He sits with it until the light comes up. This morning Nixon got a medal for carrying Cole out of the gasworks, and the ceremony was pure irony. Afterwards Cole, two fingers short, held out the diary: "Today, sir." So this afternoon Nixon climbs to the spot they've had since they were nine, to tell him, and Nathaniel is already there with the rifle log open on his knee. Nixon: "I fired the shot." Nathaniel: "I know. I counted." Then it's a fight, a real one, the first of their lives, across a slate roof sixty feet up with no boosters: fists, a shoulder into the chimney stack, both of them half over the gutter, and not one whistle between them. Nixon stops defending first. "I didn't know it was him. Your father told me to lie. He came down after." "Was Jamie alive when you left?" "Yes." Nathaniel walks away without a word. A friendship of fifteen years ends on a roof.
8. **A Gin Lane gin shop.** Tamara finds Alice drunk. She tells her about Lambrick's arithmetic and the line in the folio. Alice: "Why would I care? She's not my sister." Tamara: "She put out a fire around four children with the last of her cold and forgot your name doing it. Did Miriam ever do that?" Alice, after a long time: "No. Miriam never stood in front of anybody." She stops drinking.
9. **Whitechapel, set piece.** The wolves that came through on Blackwood Bridge don't stay there. Where the chain is thinnest, over the old Hargrave plague pits, where the hanged were dumped, the fog splits. Wolves pour into the gaslit streets. Gas lamps freeze and burst, *tink, tink, tink*. Bodies in the snowless street.
10. **Commercial Road, set piece.** Nixon's Lanterns respond: leapers on the rooftops, flash-bursts (the wolves scatter from the light), quench-blades. Shot the way the pilot's core raid was: black, then a strobe frame, then black. Every flash shows us a wolf somewhere new and closer. We see them only in the frames the Lanterns light, which is scarier than a wide shot and costs a tenth as much. Nixon fights like a man trying to get himself killed. Cole fights beside him with his two-finger grip.
11. **The Pavilion music hall, set piece.** Whimsy into grim. We stay inside. A comic singer keeps a hundred people singing "Daisy Bell" to drown out the scratching at the doors, and the song gets louder every time the scratching does. A door panel bows inward. A child stops singing, and her mother starts her again. Nathaniel comes in through the skylight on leap-boosters with a bag of flash-cartridges, and for the first time in his life he sticks the landing: one knee on the stage boards, *THOOM*, a hundred faces turned up at him, the boy from the coal bin flying when it counts, and Nixon arrives from the other roof. No words. They fight back to back the way they did at nine years old, perfectly in sync, an hour after the friendship ended.
12. **The Pavilion stage door, and the street.** The Lost One walks into the street of wolves and says one word in a language of wind. They stop, every one of them, except the biggest. She kills it with cold, and we see the memory go: a market stall the winter Alice was twelve, Miriam pulling a pair of red mittens off a hook and running, the whole picture frosting over and cracking. Then she turns the rest, every one, and walks them back through the fog to the bridge, and the crack in the air closes behind the last of them. *Tik.* A hundred people watch a girl command wolves. By morning she'll be the Frost Witch, the one creature in London who can open the White and shut it again. In the fog at the end of the street, a big man in a scarred coat is watching too, eating an apple, the Black Gauntlet ticking on his arm: Ajax, taking a good look at what his master means to buy. He's gone before anyone thinks to fire. Then Alice, sober now, reaches her through the street of wolves. The Lost One doesn't know her face anymore. Alice: "I'm Alice. I'm your sister." The sisters' scene: chosen, not inherited.

**Act Three**

13. **Shadwell, dawn.** By morning Vane's martial-law notice is pasted over every FROST KILLER bill in London: the Frost Witch is to be taken alive. In the steam of the Shadwell laundry, Josephine lays one glove on Grise's counting table, knot side up, and they agree a price for the Frost Witch, delivered to Josephine, not the Crown. Grise: "Where is she?" Josephine: "Wherever my son puts her when the city gets too hot for her. Before Lantern Night that was my glasshouse, and after last night it will be again. I want her taken by hands he can't trace to me." Grise looks across the steam at Kit, who is already folding a sheet over a knife. Josephine: "She's not a girl, Grise." Grise: "None of us were, by the time we came here."
14. **St Bride's, the Harding vault, dawn.** All night Nathaniel fought beside the man who shot his brother, and one word from the roof has gone round his head the whole time: *Yes.* Jamie was alive when Nixon left. At dawn he goes to see his brother. Crowbar, lantern, the Harding vault under St Bride's. He breaks the seal on the coffin his best friend carried down these steps. *Krrk-CRACK.* Inside, laid out in Jamie's burial coat, are three sacks of grey slag from the Works vats and a family Bible for weight. No body. Nathaniel stands in the candle-dark with a fistful of slag, and Morgana's voice from Lantern Night comes back to him: *He is cold.* Above him, on the chancel wall, the brass plate still says DIED ABROAD.
15. **Harding Works, Eldgard's old gallery, empty.** The Crown has the core, and nobody has bothered with the gallery. Eldgard is asleep drunk among the dead vats. Nathaniel doesn't wake him. He opens the lock of the Keeper's desk with a hairpin in four seconds, the way a lamp-girl once showed him, and finds the book Hardings never name upstairs: the Keeper's ledger. One line every fifty years, each in a different hand. *1489. Edric Harding, nine.* Then a column of first names with no surnames, 1539 to 1789, as if whoever they were didn't rate one. *1839. Ottoline Harding, nineteen*: the girl on the brass. *1889. James Harding*, scored through. Under it, in his father's hand, dated 3 November, the morning after Jamie "fell": *Nathaniel.* In the margins a Keeper's notes on the wall-feed run month by month, every cycle back to 1539, each one sagging toward a first snow and climbing again the week after. The dips he has been charting since the engagement are a countdown. He drops a fistful of slag on the open page, the same grey as the dead vats all around them. Eldgard wakes. "What did you do after Nixon left?" "I finished it." "Why not you?" Eldgard looks at the page a long time. "Ask me at the solstice." "It's my name, Father." "Then you know why I'm building it." The mercy was never for her. The Heartglass was always meant to keep his son off the stair; Nathaniel doesn't know yet what it's meant to hold instead. Then, almost to himself: "Your mother knew what this house spends. She found it out at my gate with a knife in her hand, and she married it anyway."
16. **Harding House, the long gallery, then the glasshouse.** Alice brings the Lost One in by the servants' door, and they cross the gallery of Harding portraits in the half-dark. The Lost One stops at Jamie's: twenty-five, laughing at something outside the frame, a skate-blade over his shoulder. Her hand goes to her belly without her knowing it. Her breath catches on a feeling that isn't hers, the whole of Miriam's love for him arriving in her body at once. Alice: "What's wrong?" "Nothing." Alice looks at the portrait, then at her, and decides not to ask. In the glasshouse, Nathaniel is waiting, slag still grey in the creases of his hands. Alice doesn't let him start. Tamara read her the line in the folio: *Vessel: White-born.* "You built a cage for my sister." He didn't know. She watches him not know, and hits him anyway, harder than Nathaniel hit Nixon. Then: "So you know how to break it." Before he can answer, TAMARA comes in out of the rain with the rest of what she read on her father's desk: a lunacy order, two doctors' signatures, waiting for the day she opens her mouth about his books. "A spinster's nearest relative is her father," she says. "A wife's is her husband. The wedding is in seven days. I'd rather it were you than Bethlem." Nathaniel looks at Alice. Alice: "Marry her." "Bounds—" "Marry her." Both of them mean it, and both of them hate that they do. Later, at the stove, Nathaniel tells Alice about the coffin full of slag and the ledger with his name under his brother's. She hears that part and doesn't say anything for a long time. Then he tells her what Morgana said on Lantern Night: Jamie isn't dead, he's cold. Alice, hours after losing her sister for good: "Don't live in it. I lived in it four weeks. It's warm, and then it isn't." From the dark under the orange trees, the Lost One, who isn't supposed to be listening: "My mother doesn't lie." Two answers, and Nathaniel can only believe one of them. Twenty-one days, and everyone wants somebody in the chain.
17. **Gin Lane rooftop.** Whimsy and grief. The Lost One asks Alice to teach her the counting game again, since she lost it. For the first time Alice tells her where it came from: the fence outside the window, eight posts, and he's done. The Lost One listens, then counts them with her, and at eight she keeps going. "Nine." Alice: "There's no nine." "There is now. Nine is the one where we get up." She gets it wrong on purpose, nine, ten, eleven, to make Alice laugh. It works. The game born under a bed has a new ending.

**Tag**

18. **Guard barracks, the infirmary.** Tamara comes in while Nixon is changing Cole's dressings. She gives Nixon a folder copied from her father's seized Harding papers: "It has your name on it." Then, a week before her wedding, she kisses him. Cole pretends very hard to be asleep. Nixon doesn't open the folder. He puts it inside his coat, against his chest, and he doesn't sleep.

## Episode 8 — "The Wedding"

**14 DAYS TO FIRST SNOW.** 7 December. **Focus:** one morning, one chapel, and the girl left alone across the lawn. **Set pieces:** the wedding, the taking and the mutiny at the gates. **Sting:** the Lost One lets herself be taken while Alice watches the wedding, and her mother's grief brings the snow two weeks early.

*Logline:* Nathaniel marries Tamara to keep her out of Bethlem, with Alice watching from the gallery. While the vows run, the Vykra come for the girl in the glasshouse, Nixon refuses an order to fire on a crowd and finds the cellar he was grown in, and the snow comes tonight.

| Character | Want | Obstacle | Solution | Twist |

| Nathaniel | To marry Tamara and keep her out of her father's hands | His mother tells him Alice was paid to love him, and his name is next in the ledger | Says "I will" with Alice at the gallery rail, before the attendants reach the altar | Married, and the price is Alice's sister |

### Scenes

**Cold open**

1. **The Harding lawn, before dawn.** Frost on the grass, the chapel dark, the glasshouse a grey shape across the lawn. KIT MARROW walks from the chapel door to the glasshouse with a pocket watch open in her palm, counting under her breath. Then she walks it again, faster. Then she runs it. Four minutes walking, ninety seconds running. Three Vykra girls follow her route in her footprints. She tries the glasshouse door: unlocked, and the girl inside asleep under the orange trees. Kit doesn't go in. She snaps the watch shut and looks up at the house, where a first-floor curtain falls back into place. Josephine has watched the whole rehearsal. Now the audience knows exactly what will happen during the vows, and when, and nobody else on screen does.

**Act One**

2. **The glasshouse, the night before.** He marries Tamara in the morning, and nobody has said a word about it all evening. Whimsy: the Lost One, who has never seen a wedding, insists on practising one. She plays the priest, reading with enormous solemnity from a prayer book held upside down, and makes Alice stand in for the bride. They say every word to each other, slowly, and both of them mean every word, and both of them know it. "Wilt thou have this woman…" "I will." The Lost One turns the page: "You may now kiss the—" Alice: "That part isn't ours." They stand there not stopping, and not moving either. The Lost One looks from one to the other, sincerely: "You both look sad when you look at each other. Is that what weddings are for?" Nobody answers. In the morning his mother will have a piece of paper for him.
3. **Harding House, the groom's dressing room.** Josephine straightens his tie and gives him Grise's receipt for the contract on her own son, in a clerk's hand: *The second Harding son. Agent: Alice Bounds.* It's the first time he has ever seen her first name, and it's on a bill. The page was torn from Grise's book, and a stub of the page before it came away on the thread. Two lines survive on the stub: *The first Harding son. Agent: Miriam Bounds.* Under it, in the same hand: *Closed, 2 Nov.* His mother bought a Bounds sister for each of her sons. "She was paid to get close to you. By me. Whatever she's felt, I bought it." Nathaniel can't breathe. She wants his eyes on the altar today, and the lamp-girl's eyes on him, and nobody's on the glasshouse. Grise already knows the hour. Josephine leaves, and Eldgard takes her place at the door with Jamie's cufflinks in one hand and Jamie's pocket watch in the other, wound, swinging a little on its chain. Nathaniel watches it swing. *Flash:* the core, sixteen years ago. NATHANIEL, five, follows a lullaby down the forbidden stairs and finds a huge, gentle woman of ice who asks his name and sings it back to him. Eldgard snatches him up and swings a lantern slowly before his eyes. "You had a dream. You never heard anything." The boy, glassy: "I had a dream. I never heard anything." *Back:* the watch stops swinging. It's the "nightmare" Nathaniel has had all his life, and the reason that, of everyone in his house, he's the one who hears her cry. For the first time, he knows it wasn't a dream. Nathaniel: "Did you hold his hand?" Eldgard: "I held his hand." It's the half-truth he lies in. Neither of them mentions the ledger, or the date under his name. Nathaniel takes the cufflinks and puts them on, smiling, because he knows what today is for.
4. **The glasshouse.** Nathaniel arrives with the receipt in his fist: "Were you paid?" Alice: "At first." She starts to say the rest, and he is already gone. He didn't call her Bounds. He didn't call her anything. Alice stands in the doorway with her coat half on, and the Lost One, who heard her say *cage* in this room a week ago and has thought about nothing else since, makes it easy for her: "Go. Watch him. If you don't, you'll hate yourself, and then you'll hate me. I'll be here." "Mittens on. Door locked." "Yes, Ali." The third lie. Alice goes.
5. **Guard tower.** Dawn on the wedding day. Nixon finally opens the folder he has carried against his chest for a week. His charity-school blood test, the green sliver, Eldgard's hand: *Hargrave, N. Cadet line of Harding. Reserve. Blood suitable.* He was never a charity case. He was a spare. Vane's orders arrive while it's still open on his knee: arrest Eldgard Harding after the vows, then clear the crowd at the gates "by any means." Nixon folds the file away: "I'm done being anybody's reserve, Cole."
6. **The Harding chapel, the vestry.** Before the guests. Tamara, in white, comes looking for somewhere to be sick and finds Nixon, uninvited, in his captain's coat, standing over a ring box on the vestry table that is waiting for a best man nobody has named. Tamara has carried a needle with that knot's poison on it since the gallery stairs at the hearing. Her mother-in-law's fingers settle the lace. Both women know; neither says a word. Within the hour, this room will hold a strait-coat.

Nathaniel, not turning: "Stand where I can see you." Nixon steps up beside him. A whisper: "You look terrible." "I haven't slept." "Neither have I." He waits for the bride he kissed a week ago. Up in the gallery, at the back, where a lamp-girl would stand, is Alice in a borrowed hat.

**Act Two**

10. **The vestry door.** At the groom's shoulder, Nixon sees the vestry door ease open: AJAX, in the room where Tamara asked him an hour ago, with two Bethlem attendants, a strait-coat and a lunacy order for *Tamara Lambrick, spinster*: her father's signature as nearest relative, two doctors', and the Chancery seal, because lunatics are the Lord Chancellor's wards and Vane is sitting in the front pew. Lambrick doesn't need a Harding son-in-law any more. Since the gasworks he sits on the Crown's board, and the Works are half his. What he needs is a daughter who can't read his books aloud, and he has waited for the one room where the Lord Chancellor can seal her mad in front of everyone. Nixon whistles their old signal, *danger*. Nathaniel looks, sees Ajax, and understands they have the length of one question. In the front pew Eldgard sees it too and half rises; a marshal's hand settles on his shoulder, and he sits.
11. **The glasshouse.** Across the lawn, nobody is watching. The Lost One sits under the orange trees with her mittens off, humming the Lantern Hymn to them in a key no church uses, and waits. When Kit and Grise's girls come through the glass on the minute, with Josephine's directions, she doesn't fight. She stands and holds out her wrists to Kit, who has heard what that touch does and takes a long breath before she puts the irons on. She breathes once on the pane by the door. Frost writes itself across it. She couldn't have walked to the core: it's Crown iron now, and the cage that can put her in her mother's place is in Crown hands, so the only way in is as somebody's prisoner. The attendants come up the aisle with the strait-coat, and the priest stops with his mouth open, looking to Vane. Nixon's hand is on his rifle and his company is at the gates; he could carry her out of this chapel in a minute. Nathaniel sees him think it and shakes his head once: carry her out and she's a runaway lunatic with a Chancery seal on her for the rest of her life. The order names a spinster. Make her a wife and it names nobody, and her nearest relative is her husband. It was always the plan. It just has to happen in the next thirty seconds. Nathaniel looks up and finds Alice at the gallery rail. She nods once, small, like a permission. She is letting him go. "Go on," he tells the priest, and doesn't wait for him: "I will." Voiding a marriage in front of them means a Chancery hearing, and a Chancery hearing goes on the record. "This is addressed to a spinster. I see a wife." The attendants fold the strait-coat. When Nathaniel looks up again, the gallery rail is empty.

The marshals take his watch, his cane and his collar studs. They leave the wolf-crest ring on his hand, because nobody outside the family knows what it closed. He looks at his wife and understands. "Josie." She doesn't look away.

**Act Three**

15. **The gates, set piece.** Nixon walks out of the chapel still holding the empty ring box. The crowd sees Eldgard in irons and surges. Coal-men and Frost Witch believers clash. Neither mentions it. Across the lawn, a lamp comes on in the glasshouse. He stands at the window and watches it until the candle in his hand burns down to his fingers, and then he goes.

He reads the glass. Alice: "She heard me say *cage* in this room. She's gone to get in it." Nathaniel: "Then it fails. No mother lets her child hang in her place." Alice: "She went because she wanted to. She thinks she can make her." It's Alice's own lie, love as holding on, coming back at her from the other side of the glass.

He finds the coal-hatch stair behind the Works and counts down. Thirteen. Each name is a Hargrave. The first, cut deep and worn almost smooth, is MARIAN, 1489. The tallies beside the names are a keeper's count, and over them, in a keeper's chalk, one word: APPLES. *Count the apples.* The children were the apples. A skipping rhyme every London child knows goes through his head, *Green light, green light, who's in the cellar tonight?*, and he is standing in the cellar. The last name on the wall is MARIAN too, with a date four years after he was told his mother died of fever. For four hundred years the Orchard named its girls after the first one who tried. He sits on her bunk. *Creak.* A wooden spoon is still under the pillow. Over the lintel, in old chalk, are two initials: J.H. Jamie was here first. Nixon understands what he was picked out of, and who kept it from him.
19. **Newgate.** Josephine visits Eldgard through the bars. "You made a deal with Lambrick," he says. "The girl to the chain, our son kept out of it, and me to Newgate." "Yes." "You sold me." "I sold the man who sold our son." "How did you bring Lambrick in?" "He paid Grise to have you killed when he was twenty-five. I was the knife. He never knew which girl failed him, until last week." Eldgard almost laughs. Then, quieter: "Grise's book. The Bounds girl on Jamie. That was you too." "I bought him a reason to run. He ran to the lock instead." He asks one thing: "Was any of it real?" "Thirty years. All of it." It's the first time in a generation neither of them lies. "Our son lives." Eldgard: "You've killed him. I've stood in front of her for forty years, and she's never once given a Harding anything he could keep. She'll break that glass before she lets her child hang in it, and when it breaks they'll need a Harding anyway." Josephine: "Four hundred years in hooks. Nobody refuses the door." Eldgard: "You would." It lands. Josephine's face: she may have made a mistake, and it's too late to unmake it.
20. **The core.** Lambrick and Vane watch the Heartglass, Nathaniel's design, close around the Lost One beside her mother. She speaks to Morgana in the language of the White: "I've come to take your place." Morgana understands. For four hundred years she has wanted the door, and here it is, held open by her own child. She screams. It's grief, not rage, and it's no.

**Tag**

21. **London.** The scream rises through the Works and into the sky, and it begins to snow, two weeks early. This time the walls can't melt it, and for the first time in living memory snow settles on London. Alice in the glasshouse door. Nixon and his forty on a rooftop. Tamara at a window in her wedding dress. Josephine on the Newgate steps. Eldgard at his cell window, flakes on his face. He has counted to the solstice like every Keeper before him, and in four hundred years of ledgers she has never once been early. "First snow." Whatever is owed is owed by midnight. Card: **0 DAYS.**

## Episode 9 — "The Snow Confession"

**FIRST SNOW.** Dusk to midnight. **Focus:** the assault. Everyone's plan runs toward one room. **Set pieces:** the Newgate break-out, then storming the Works through snow and wolves. **Sting:** Eldgard dies telling the truth, and it's worse than the lies.

*Logline:* With the snow falling and the binding set for midnight, Nathaniel breaks his father out of Newgate with Nixon's mutineers, Alice takes the Vykra from Mother Grise, and Tamara steals her father's keys. At the Works gate, Eldgard takes a blow meant for his son and spends his last breath on everything he did.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Nathaniel | To reach the core before midnight | Crown troops, Lambrick, his mother, and a debt to Alice he can never repay | Rebuilds the alliance, beginning with Nixon | His father dies in his arms and leaves him the way out, and his ring |

| Tamara | To save her husband from her father's backup plan | Her father wants her to deliver Nathaniel | Pretends to obey and steals the master key | Faces Ajax down with Josephine's own needle |
| Eldgard | To be useful to his son, once | Prison, age, guilt | Leads them in, and takes the blow | "Don't be me." |

### Scenes

**Cold open**

1. **London, dusk, the first snow.** For the first time in living memory, snow lies in London's streets. Gin Lane children who have never seen it stand with their tongues out. A cabman in the Strand stops his horse to stare at the white on its back. A lamplighter on his ladder holds out his palm, watches a flake not melt, and laughs. Then, on Tyburn hill, where the drift lies deepest, the snow begins to shape itself: a muzzle, shoulders, blue eyes opening in the white. A Crown sentry fires a flash. In the strobe the drift is only a drift. In the dark after it, it isn't.

**Act One**

2. **The glasshouse, snow falling through the broken panes.** Nathaniel says it first, because he has had all afternoon to see that frost on the pane: "You nodded, and I said it, and while we were saying it they took her." Alice: "She'd have been in Bethlem by supper." "Your sister—" "Sent me up there. She'd already decided. She was going, Nathaniel. I only chose who got saved on the way." He owes her something he can never pay back, and both of them know it, and it makes him cruel for exactly two lines: "My mother's receipt had your name on it. That's where I learned it. On a bill." Alice doesn't flinch, which is how he knows it went in. So he gives her the second one, and hates himself while he does it: "Your sister's was on the page before. The first Harding son." That one she didn't know. She sits down on the workbench: Miriam sneaking off for weeks, the ring on the cord, the kiss in the coach, a contract, and then not. "So she was paid." "At first," Nathaniel says, and hears himself say it. Then she gives him the rest of the sentence from this morning, all of it, the way she gave her sister fifteen years in one breath: "At first I was paid. Then you flew across that foundry into a coal bin for an audience of one, and you looked at me like I was the first person who'd ever watched. After that I'd have done it for nothing. I did do it for nothing. Grise cut me loose a month ago, and I kept coming." Snow ticks on the broken glass. "I'm married." "I know. I nodded." He reaches for her across the workbench. She lets him get halfway, then stands. "Midnight. The core. After that, we'll see what either of us is allowed." Then they argue it like engineers, because it's the only way either of them can bear to. He spreads the wall map on the bench: if the light dies, every sector goes dark at once, coal can hold the west for six hours, and he doesn't know how long a city lasts after that. Alice puts her finger on the east wall. "Which London? Gin Lane's been on a dead wall for three weeks. We've got the laundry boilers and the Vykra's coal, and every door in the lane already knows how to stop a draught. We can hold one night. Can Mayfair?" He doesn't have an answer. It's the first time anyone has made him count the people he was saving. For the first time it's her plan and he follows it: midnight at the core. They need the wolf-crest ring, the one Alistair closed the chain with, and it's on Eldgard's hand in a Crown cell. And they need Nixon.
3. **An abandoned bell foundry, Whitechapel.** Whimsy. Forty disgraced Lanterns cook sausages on flash-gauntlets set to low. Nathaniel walks in. "You shot my brother." "Yes." "My father did the rest. I think." Silence. "What do you need?" "Newgate, then the Works." Nixon: "You know we're cousins? Cadet line." "Then you're family. God help you."
4. **Lambrick House.** Lambrick: "If the girl fails, the Crown needs a Harding by midnight, and it has to walk down on its own feet. Your father-in-law would hang first. The boy will walk if he thinks it saves somebody. You'll bring your husband." Then, kindly: "Bring him, and I'll burn the order at Bethlem. Don't, and remember that a widow's nearest relative is her father again." Tamara: "Yes, Father." He believes her, because he has never once looked at her. In the study an hour later, she opens the safe one last time and takes the Works master key, and this time the ledger itself, every page, and the cipher maps of the old coal mains with every live valve marked: six months of payments to A.F., down to the night of the Charter vote. *A.F., Pimlico, 300 gns.* Ajax is in the doorway with an apple. This time she holds up a winter's kiss needle, Vykra issue: "Try it." He steps aside, then goes to tell her father.
5. **Gin Lane, Old Wren's door, dusk.** Snow on Gin Lane. On her way to take the Vykra, Alice stops at OLD WREN's door, because there may not be another chance. "You knew. The first night. You held the mirror under her mouth, and you knew." Wren doesn't deny it. "I knew you couldn't hear it yet. Five weeks you had a sister. I'd give them to you again." Alice: "And the child?" Wren: "Still there. Cold holds what it holds. It held her heart still on that bridge, and it's holding the baby the same way. I didn't know if either would ever start again, so I told you it was gone." Alice can't decide whether to thank her or hate her, and there isn't time to decide. Wren touches the arm she set when Alice was nine: "Nobody stood in front of you then, either. Go on." Alice goes.
6. **Vykra laundry.** Grise: "I sold your sister because she's worth more than you." Alice calls a challenge by Vykra law, and Vykra law says she goes through the mother's best knife first. Kit steps out of the steam. A knife fight among the sheets, *fwip, fwip*, shadows through linen, two girls who learned every move side by side. Kit is faster. Kit was always faster. Alice wins on a rhythm Miriam taught her, the counting game, the count she used to hide inside. One post, two post, through eight, and on *nine* she moves, the count the Lost One gave her, the one where you get up. Kit ends up on the wet boards with her own knife at her throat, laughing: "You never once looked at the girl with the pot. You of all people." "I'm looking now." Alice lets her up. Grise lays her knife on the counting table without being asked: she can count too. The Vykra girls pick up their knives. "Where?" "Tyburn." Kit picks hers up last.
7. **Harding House, the snowlit hall.** Josephine alone beside Byron's empty chair. She opens a hidden box of Vykra knives she hasn't touched in thirty years and puts them on.

**Act Two**

8. **Newgate yard, set piece.** Nixon's forty come over the walls on leap-boosters, *kshhh-THOOM* in the snow. Flash-bursts strobe the yard. Nathaniel cuts the cell bars with his gauntlet. Eldgard: "You came for me." "I came for your ring." Two cells down, Nixon quietly opens 1417 as well. Old Marsh shuffles out into the yard, looks up at the snow, and says, perfectly lucid, "They always count from the first snow." Cole persuades a turnkey to unlock the gate with extreme politeness and a lantern rifle.
9. **Newgate rooftops.** Crown guards on the roofs. Eldgard can't leap, so Nixon carries him: the spare carrying the man who bred him. Eldgard: "Thank you, Captain." Nixon: "Don't."
10. **The Works, the gallery and the core below.** Snow thickens over Tyburn. Where it lies deep, wolves start to shape themselves out of the drifts. Vane is nervous. In his breast pocket is the sealed 1689 annex to the Harding Charter, the Crown's own signature on the chain; every Lord Chancellor has kept it locked in his desk, and tonight he took it out. Lambrick: "Midnight. The girl, or the boy." Below them, cracks spread through the Heartglass around the Lost One: every time the glass hums toward the trade, her mother pulls against her own hooks, and another crack runs. *Tik. Tik.* Morgana, hoarse: "You were free. Why did you come back?" The Lost One: "Because you're my mother." Morgana doesn't answer. Another crack runs. Footsteps on Byron's old Guard stair: Josephine is coming down.
11. **The outfall, outside the Works.** The whole young alliance stands together for the first time: Alice's Vykra, Nixon's Lanterns, Tamara with the key, Nathaniel, and Eldgard leaning on a rifle. Whimsy before the storm. Cole asks Tamara if married ladies are allowed to fight. She hands him a knife. Then Tamara's key turns. The Lanterns go over the walls and the Vykra go up the outfall.

**Act Three**

13. **The courtyard.** Ajax and the Black Gauntlet, *WHUNK*, drive Nathaniel into the ground. Ajax grins down at him, scarred from the Thames: "You should've pulled me up, Harding." The fist goes up for the killing blow, and Eldgard steps under it. His ribs give way. Alice, who told him to pull Ajax up, drives the man off with flash after flash, and he retreats inside. Inside is the core, and her sister, and the clock on the Works tower says twenty to twelve. She has the charge left to chase him. She looks at the door. She looks at Nathaniel on his knees in the snow with his father across his lap. She stays. Eldgard, through blood: "Not here. The Guard stair. I know the way down." Nathaniel takes Jamie's key off its black ribbon, and it opens Byron's old Guard stair. He and Nixon lift his father between them.
14. **The Snow Confession, I: the Guard stair.** Down into the dark, Eldgard's arms over his son's shoulder and the captain's, Alice backing down behind them with her gauntlet up, Crown boots on the stair above and flash-bursts lighting the stairwell white, *fzzt*, then black. On the other shoulder, Nixon remembers the weight on the steps of St Bride's, and doesn't break step. Nathaniel: "She says he's cold. Not dead." "Don't go looking for him, Nathaniel. Eldgard's legs go, and they lay him on the stone. Snow has followed them down the stairwell. Alice kneels at Nathaniel's back with her forehead against his shoulder blade, out of the conversation, spending minutes her sister doesn't have. Eldgard: "When you were five—" Nathaniel: "I know. 17. **The core floor.** Nathaniel and Alice reach the bottom. The Lost One is screaming inside the cracking Heartglass, Morgana is straining at her hooks, Lambrick and Vane are on the gallery, and Josephine is at the lever.

**Tag**

18. **The core.** Nathaniel: "Mother. Step away." Alice: "That's my sister." Josephine, gently, to neither of them: "Your father said her mother would never allow it." Nathaniel: "Then don't." "Four hundred years in hooks, Nathaniel. Nobody refuses the door." It's the choice she made at breakfast in Episode 7, between her son and everything else, made one last time with her hand on iron. If the girl holds, no Harding dies tonight, and Josephine can live with what it cost. She pulls the lever. Cut to black.

## Episode 10 — "The Tithe"

**THE LAST NIGHT.** Midnight to dawn, two weeks before the longest one. **Focus:** the choice Alistair refused. **Set piece:** every light and every wall in London goes dark for good, and the White comes through. **Double twist:** Nathaniel refuses every sacrifice offered to him, Morgana still demands a Harding, and Josephine goes in his place. **Final image:** "The Record," a montage of the season's climaxes under Nathaniel's voice, ending on a heartbeat, then a hard cut to Jamie, kept in the White.

*Logline:* The Heartglass cracks, the Crown comes for the last Harding son, and Nixon offers himself instead. Nathaniel does what no Harding has done in four hundred years. He opens the lock, and London pays in light.

**Cold open**

**Act One**

2. **The core, ten minutes to midnight.** The lever drops. The Heartglass floods the Lost One with green fire, and she screams, and the door swings open for her mother: the hooks loosen, and for the first time in four hundred years Morgana could step out. She doesn't. She takes the chain in both hands and drags herself back onto the iron, hooks tearing deeper, pulling against the trade until the glass around her daughter cracks from her side. *Krrrrk.* "Not her." The chain shudders and doesn't take. At the lever, Josephine watches a mother refuse the door and hears her husband in Newgate: *You would.* Lambrick, from the gallery: "Then the boy. He'll walk if it saves somebody. Take the girl he came for." Vane's marshals start down the stairs, for Alice.
3. **The core floor.** Alice smashes the Heartglass with her gauntlet, *KSSHHH*, and drags the Lost One out, half turned to crystal. Josephine draws her knives to put the girl back in the cracked glass, because the marshals are already on the stairs to make her son walk, and a broken cage is still a cage until midnight. She fights like a woman who has just been shown she's wrong, and they fight, Vykra against Vykra, the mother against the girl she paid to love her son. Josephine is better. Nathaniel steps between them: "Mother."
4. **The core floor.** Nixon comes down the stair bleeding, with Tamara behind him. "I'm Harding blood. Take me. They grew me for this the way they grew my mother. Let me be something on purpose." He knows he's doing exactly what Eldgard grew him to do, and he does it with his eyes open. Cole: "Sir, no." Lambrick, from the gallery: "Harding blood is Harding blood. Let him." The marshals turn for the captain.
5. **The lock.** Nathaniel: "No one goes in the chain again." Jamie's glass blade is still in the lock where he left it on the first night, frozen into the iron by the cold that has leaked out of it for four hundred years. Nathaniel pulls, and it doesn't move. *Krrk.* His hands slip on it. Alice is beside him before he asks, and the gauntlet he built for her in the glasshouse, strap by strap, is already whining up to overload, *eeeeeeee*, the way he told her never to run it. He knows what she's about to do. "If this works, every light in London goes out, and every wall with it. They'll hate me for a hundred years." She has already counted Gin Lane's coal and its doors. "Us," she says, and puts the gauntlet's core against the iron. The heat comes back through the plate into her palm and she holds it there, the smell of her own glove burning, until the ice round the blade lets go. He pulls it free, cuts his palm on it, and presses his blood and his father's wolf ring into the iron, knowing what his father told him in the snow it costs: when the chain breaks, one of the house goes with her. His father's warning is in his ear: *hoping*. He can't swear his brother isn't part of why his hand is steady. Harding blood closed it, and Harding blood opens it, but it took an undercity girl's burned hand to get the blade out. Somewhere above, a church bell strikes twelve.
6. **London.** The chain falls. The last time London went dark, the light came back within the hour. This time the green dies street by street, the same wave as the pilot, and nothing comes back. On the horizon the walls go out sector by sector, the one thing nobody was ever allowed to switch off, and the outerland cold comes in over them like a tide. One wordless shot after another: a tram dying in the snow, its enamel bill still promising HARDING LIGHT. WARM AS A MOTHER'S ARMS, a hospital ward going black, a mother on Gin Lane lighting a tallow candle with shaking hands. Under the Works, Nathaniel listens, and for the first time since he was five, nothing is crying in the walls.

**Act Two**

7. **The core.** Morgana rises out of the pit as her wounds close, nine feet tall. She looks down at Nathaniel and says his name, the way she sang it to a five-year-old on the stair: "Nathaniel." Then: "A Harding opened it." She doesn't thank him. The White pours through behind her, with wind, snow and wolves, and she goes up through the Works roof toward Tyburn hill. Where the chain was anchored, the core floor splits, and the White comes up through the crack in a wall of wind: Alice and the half-crystal girl on the outfall side, Nathaniel and his mother on the other. Alice can't see across it. The girl in her arms is going colder by the minute, the outfall is the only way open, and the only fire Alice can count on tonight is on Gin Lane, where the Vykra are holding. She takes it.
8. **The core stair.** Nixon against Ajax: quench-blade against Black Gauntlet. Nixon takes the quick-takedown: blade into the gauntlet's crystal, which freezes the piston, then into Ajax. He dies frozen mid-laugh, his apple rolling down the stairs. With Ajax dead, the Guard stair is clear: the one way out of the Works the wolves haven't found. Nixon whistles down it the third signal they made up at nine, the one they never once needed: *come on*.
9. **London, the night the snow came early.** Nixon's forty and every loyal Lantern form flash-lines across the bridges to keep the wolves off the tenements. The Vykra hold Gin Lane with knives and candles. Alice holds the lane with the Lost One wrapped in coats in a doorway behind her, as close to the coal as Alice can get her, and between wolves she grabs Kit, who fought her for the Vykra six hours ago. "Go to the Works. Find out if he's alive. Don't tell him I asked." Kit goes. Alice is three steps after her before she knows she's moving; behind her a child starts to scream, *aaaAAH*, and she turns back to her knife. Every time a flash lights the sky over Tyburn, she looks. Tamara throws open every Lambrick coal depot free to the city and opens the old valves on her father's maps. Coal can't hold the walls, not all of them, not all night, so she doesn't try: she sends it where the people are, and there are fires in every street. On Gin Lane the Vykra hold the wolves at a ring of Lambrick coal; without it, the lane would have frozen before the wolves ever reached it. Whimsy in the dark: Cole teaches a crowd of children to make flash-shadow rabbits on the walls.
10. **The Tyburn Road barricade.** The charges run out along Cole's line, a barricade of overturned trams across Tyburn Road, the last thing between Gin Lane's fires and the hill. He holds it with his last cartridge, two fingers short and seventeen years old, until it's empty. Behind his line, a girl in a Vykra glove comes up the road with another girl in her arms, half crystal, heading for Tyburn, because an hour before dawn the girl woke by the fire and asked for her mother. He knows her: the ghostlight girl from the market, who fought beside him at the Works gate. He doesn't know why she's carrying the Frost Witch toward the hill, and he doesn't ask. He holds the road until she's past it. Nixon finds him in the last dark before dawn, sitting up against an overturned tram as if he were resting, his diary open in his lap. Under the burned log page, a last line in pencil: *Held the road. Nobody past who shouldn't be.*
11. **The core, empty.** Nathaniel and Josephine alone, the hooks hanging empty. "I'd do it all again." "I know. That's why I can't be you." At the foot of the stair she stops. "I paid for that girl." "You paid for two of them, Mother, a week each. You couldn't afford the rest of either." Josephine almost smiles. It's the first thing about her son she has ever failed to buy. Boots on the stair: Kit, bleeding from the scalp. "Gin Lane's holding. Just. She said not to tell you she asked." Nathaniel is at the stair before she has finished. And Josephine, who has controlled every door in his life, says: "Go to her." It's so unlike her that he stops with his hand on the rail and looks at her, really looks, and sees a woman who has already decided something. "Not until you tell me where you're going." She won't. So he stays, and doesn't go to the girl he loves, because he's watching his mother the way she taught him to watch everyone. He's still watching when she starts up the hill, by the Guard stair Nixon cleared.

**Act Three**

12. **Tyburn hill, set piece.** Morgana stands over London in the storm, with wolves in a ring around her. Four hundred years of grievance. Josephine climbs, and Nathaniel climbs after her. Up Tyburn Road, past the barricade a seventeen-year-old held to his last cartridge, Alice comes up the hill carrying the Lost One. Across the snow they see each other for the first time since the lock, alive, and there's no time for any of it.
13. **Tyburn hill.** The Lost One sets herself down in front of Alice, between her mother and the city, the place Miriam never once stood. Alice sees it, and her breath goes. The Lost One sings the hymn back to her mother in the old tongue, the way it was before it had words. The wolves stop circling. "They let you go. Let them go." Morgana: "They bled me for four hundred years." "One of them let you go."
14. **The Works vault, intercut with Tyburn hill.** The last hour before dawn. Lambrick has been filling satchels with the last gallowglass all night. Cut off from the chain, it dies slowly: every crystal in the vault is a shade dimmer than it was at midnight, and he's packing them as if he can outrun the dawn. Tamara finds him and offers her hand: "Come out, Father." He holds up a satchel to her, almost pleading, and in his hands the glow is going out while they talk: "This is for you. All of it. It was always for you." "Then leave it." He looks at the crystals. He looks at her. He can't put them down; he never could. She walks out. Behind her the wolves come into the vault. Cut back to the hill, where a mother is being asked the same question.
15. **Tyburn hill.** Morgana: "Then come home, little frost." The Lost One looks at Alice. Alice, who has spent the season holding on, lets go: "Go, if you want to. I'll be all right." She means it. She has done this once before, with a nod, at an altar.
16. **Tyburn hill.** The Lost One tells her the last thing: "I've spent everything else of hers. The birthdays, the songs, her name for you. I kept this. On the bridge she was still alive. She asked me something. 'Go back to Ali. Stand in front of her. I never did.' I said yes. Then I wanted to." Miriam's last thought was Alice, after all, and her last act was to find someone braver to keep the promise she broke. Alice laughs and cries at once: her sister did love her. She just couldn't do it herself. The Lost One stays. Morgana, who tore her own hooks rather than let her daughter pay, lets her stay, which is harder, and doesn't leave her the cold to stay with. She lays one hand flat on her daughter's chest and draws the White out of her, the way you'd draw a splinter. The crystal goes out of the girl's skin. She will never make snow again, and she will never again hear her mother singing in a lamp. What's left is a girl, shivering for the first time. Then she names the price: "A Harding chained me. A Harding comes with me." She waits. It has to be given, and she won't take it. Nathaniel steps forward, and Alice steps in front of him, into the place nobody ever stood for her, her fist in his coat. Her first word to him all night: "Don't."
17. **Tyburn hill.** Josephine steps past him and says to Morgana the words Morgana said at the lever: "Not him." Morgana looks at her for a long moment and knows her. Then, to her son: "I'm a Harding. I chose the name." He catches his mother's wrist. Alice is still in front of him, her fist still in his coat. Two women holding him, one from each side of his life, and both of them are right. The wolves shift their weight in the snow. Josephine, quietly, the only time all season she asks him for anything: "They'll say I died of fever in Switzerland." Nathaniel: "No. They'll say your name." A long beat. His hand opens. "Go." She kisses his forehead. To Alice: "Keep him out of the Works." It's the contract she paid for in Episode 2, now given as a blessing. Alice: "You bought both of us. I'm the only thing you ever bought that worked." Josephine almost laughs, and that's how she goes. She takes Morgana's hand and they walk into the white. The wolves follow, and the storm goes with them.

    *Why this isn't Otty again:* Otty went willingly too, and his grandfather let her. The difference between Nathaniel and every Harding before him isn't the choice. It's the lie. Otty got a brass plate that said DIED ABROAD. Josephine gets the record (scenes 19 and 20).

18. **Tyburn hill, dawn.** The snow stops in the middle of falling. Nathaniel is on his knees where his mother was, and for a long time nothing happens. Alice doesn't touch him. She kneels in front of him in the snow and lets him have it, because nobody ever let her. When it comes it isn't crying. It's a sound with no word in it, *hhh, hhh*, a man trying to breathe around something too big for his chest. She stays where he can see her until it passes. Then she takes the handkerchief out of her glove, the one he gave her at his brother's grave, and tears it in two. *Rrrip.* Half goes round the palm he cut at the lock. He takes the other half out of her fingers without asking and binds the hand she burned for him. Neither of them is any good at it. She doesn't know what to say to a man who has just said "Go" to his mother, so she gives him the most private thing she owns. Under her breath, the way Miriam taught her under the bed, she counts for him. "One post. Two post." He knows it. He heard it in a ballroom corridor five weeks ago and never asked. He counts with her. At eight she stops, because eight is where it has always ended. He keeps going. "Nine." Nobody told him about nine. He got there on his own. And then, for the first time in ten episodes, he says her name. "Alice." She hears it. He has never once said it, and she knows which bill he read it on. He's saying it now, when nobody has paid for it. He kisses her, or she kisses him; for the rest of their lives they will disagree about which.
19. **Westminster, first light.** The first dawn of the episode, grey through the tall windows, coal smoke rising from Tamara's fires. Beyond them the walls stand grey on the horizon for the first time in four hundred years. Vane names Nathaniel "the man who put out London" and signs a warrant. Tamara, Lady Harding and heir to Lambrick, puts the Heartglass folio on his desk, with the Crown's seal on the binding order. Beside it she lays her father's ledger, open at the night of the Charter vote: *A.F., Pimlico, 300 gns.* The man the Crown put on the Works board blew in its windows, and his daughter says so with his name and hers. Last, a needle, Vykra issue, the one she picked up on the gallery stairs the day Felix Hawke died. Nixon lays Cole's diary beside it, taken from the dead boy's lap: the burned discharge-log page copied word for word, and under it, in a seventeen-year-old's careful hand, *2 Nov. Capt. hit him in the shoulder. Mr Harding sent the Capt. to the gate. The man on the floor was still talking.* It puts Nixon's shot on the record, and the man who stayed behind with a living boy. Nixon could burn that page a second time. He sets it down open. Across the desk Nathaniel looks at him, the first time he has looked straight at him since the lock. "I haven't forgiven you." "I know." "Stand where I can see you." Nixon steps up beside him, the way he did at the altar, and it means the opposite now. Vane slides a captain's lantern across the desk to Nixon, the price of his silence. Nixon leaves it there: "The Guard will keep its own flame." Vane, cornered, offers Nathaniel the pardon on one condition: the Crown seals what happened under Tyburn, for the public good. Nathaniel: "No. Write it down." Vane: "The Crown doesn't keep that kind of record." Nathaniel: "It has kept one since 1689. It's in your desk, under your signature." Vane doesn't blink; an annex in a drawer has never frightened a Lord Chancellor. Then Tamara, who hasn't sat down: "Lambrick depots are feeding every grate in London this morning, my lord. They close at noon, unless that record is read into Hansard, all of it, where anyone with a penny can buy it." Outside the tall windows the coal smoke is the only warmth in the city, and every chimney of it is hers. Vane looks at the smoke for a long time. "The House sits at two." Nathaniel turns to the clerk, and the clerk dips his pen. "Edric Harding—"
20. **The Record, final montage.** Nathaniel's dictation carries over everything that follows. Its first lines are the ones we heard over the 1489 cold opens in Episodes 1, 5 and 10, and only now do we know whose voice that was: the season has been his testimony. It starts as testimony, read flat and exact like a ledger, and somewhere in the middle it stops being for the clerk. No score until line 7, then the Lantern Hymn, the tune only, the way Morgana sang it. Every image is a climax from the season, seen in the days after: the dictation takes one morning, and the pictures run on across the weeks it sets moving.

**What Season 2 inherits**

- **A London with no light.** Coal is power now, and Tamara owns the coal, and the leases on Gin Lane. The young woman who wanted to be her father's heir has become something more dangerous.
- **A Harding child carried by a daughter of the White.** Both worlds will want it.
- **Josephine, in the White**, beside a son who can't answer her. She went in to end the debt and stayed for him.
- **Everyone the chain ever took** is standing in the White exactly as they went: Edric with his lantern, Otty, the Orchard's apples, Jamie. Kept, not living. Morgana said "cold, not dead" and meant it to the letter. Nobody comes back. Season 2's question: what do you owe the dead when you know exactly where they are?
- **Whether the winter lifts.** The long winter over the outerlands was hers, seeping round the chain. She's free now. The walls stand grey, the frost line hasn't moved yet, and nobody knows whether the thaw will come, or what will come with it.
- **Nixon and Tamara**: married to his cousin, holding his hand, nothing decided. Nathaniel and Alice: a kiss on Tyburn hill and no promise, because he's still Tamara's husband, and Tamara's hold on the city's coal rests on being Lady Harding. The two women have already set the terms on a Westminster bench: the paper, until the coal runs out. Three people in love, and the only marriage among them is the one keeping London warm.
