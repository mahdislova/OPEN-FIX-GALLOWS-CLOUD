# The Writer's Brain — The Green Gallows

v0 · drafted 11 Nov 2026 from the season document and its revision history · **not yet calibrated**

This file is how the showrunner thinks. Read it before reworking any scene or episode. Everything marked **(?)** is a guess drawn from the work so far and has not been confirmed. The **Rulings** at the bottom outrank everything above them.

---

## 1. Why the current draft reads lean and tame

Four causes. Fix the cause, not the symptom.

1. **It's written as a report of the scene, not the scene.** Every scene is one summary paragraph: who's there, what happens, what it means. Dialogue arrives as single lines. Beat-sheet register is right for planning, but it's the default for everything now.
2. **The last eight passes were subtractive.** Cut to four rules, de-load Ep 7, edit to 17–19 scenes, close continuity gaps. Logic passes sand edges off. Only one pass ("Romance pass and five longing scenes") ever *added* heat.
3. **The camera leaves at the peak.** Eldgard kneels by Jamie: *cut away*. The wardrobe stops at a hand over a mouth. The core raid is "snapshots." Some of this is a weapon (the Eldgard cut-away seeds Ep 9). Some of it is a flinch. The brain has to know which is which.
4. **The prose explains itself.** "That's why…", "It's about what it did to two girls, not about the man", "*Therefore* every scene these two share…" Telling the reader what a beat means takes the charge out of it.

## 2. Taste, as shown by the choices

What was kept, what was cut, and whose rules were chosen say more than any description would.

- **Comps:** the dread of *Chernobyl*, the sisters of *Arcane*, *House of Cards* in the drawing rooms.
- **Writers whose rules were adopted:** Parker and Stone (therefore/but), Brent Weeks (secrets with long fuses, three layers), Alex Yee (only trauma splits the sisters), Beau Willimon (power lives in intimate relationships).
- **Loves:** objects that carry story (red mittens, Jamie's watch, the wolf-crest ring, Otty's ribbon); a sound before a sight (*Creak. Creak.*, the buckle on iron); whimsy right next to horror; secrets the audience holds before the character does; plans colliding on one night; a tag that changes the board.
- **Kept from the original outline no matter what:** the Harding legacy and 1489, Miriam and the Lost One, "You're not her," Josephine's protection turning to control, the Snow Confession, Nixon's Hargrave blood, Tamara the operative, the Vykra, Ajax, the mittens.
- **Cuts readily:** characters who only deliver information; rules a viewer can't say in a sentence; mechanics that crowd out people.

## 3. The dials

Where each one sits now, and where the default goes from here. Confirm or move each one.

| Dial | Now | Proposed default (?) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Format of a rework** | Beat-sheet paragraph | Rendered scene: slug, blocking, full dialogue, sound. Beat sheet only when asked |
| **Violence** | Implied, strobe-lit, cut away | On screen and specific when it costs someone. Cut away only when the gap is a secret the season pays off |
| **Body horror** (frost, the chain, winter's kiss) | Clean: "freezes stiff," "frost blooms" | What it does to skin, lips, fingernails, sound. The cat dying should be unbearable |
| **Heat** (Alice/Nathaniel, Tamara/Nixon) | Longing, one touch, a hand withheld | Longing that breaks. When adults are alone with time and want, the scene follows through |
| **Cruelty in the family** | Stated ("The leash goes on") | Played. Let Eldgard and Josephine be pleasant while they do it |
| **Dialogue** | One-liners, quips | Longer exchanges with subtext; people talking around the thing |
| **Whimsy** | Cute (cat, orange, laundry lines) | Funny, and a little wrong. Every light beat has a splinter in it |
| **Explanation** | The text says what scenes mean | Never. Show it, trust the audience, let the ledger hold the logic |
| **Profanity** | Almost none | Period-true and in character: the undercity swears, the drawing room wounds politely |
| **The sisters' abuse** | Never shown; sound and flinch | Keep unseen (this restraint is working). Make the sound and the flinch worse |

## 5. House style

- CAPS on a character's first appearance, then a head-to-toe introduction: build, face, clothes, hands, how they stand, what they smell like.
- Sound spelled out, in italics: *kshhhh-THOOM*, *krrrk*, *tk-tk-tk-tk*, *fff*. Sound comes before the image it belongs to.
- Every space has weather, temperature, smell and clutter. Gaslight is green; London is cold; nothing is clean.
- Scenes end on an action or a cut, never a question.
- In beat sheets, keep the *Therefore* / *But* tags. In rendered scenes, drop them: the causality should be felt, not labelled.

## 6. Rework protocol

When handed a scene or an episode:

1. **Keep its job.** Check the scene's entries in the setup-and-payoff ledger and the four world rules. Every setup it plants and every payoff it lands survives.
2. **Escalate before trimming.** Ask what the scene is afraid of, and go there. Cut only beats that are "and then."
3. **Render it** per the dials: blocking, hands, sound, full dialogue.
4. **Never cut a beat silently.** If something goes, say so.
5. **After the scene, a short change note:** what was escalated, what moved, any continuity it touches elsewhere in the season.
6. If the change breaks continuity elsewhere, flag it, don't quietly patch three other episodes.

## 7. Open questions for the showrunner

- Who is the reader of the rework: a writers' room, a network, yourself? It sets the format.
- Which audience voice: general adult, literary, or crass and direct?
- Teleplay format (sluglines, centred dialogue) or prose that reads like a novel?
- Which cut-aways are weapons and which are flinches? Name three you'd open up.
- Is there anything the show never shows, on principle?
