# The Green Gallows — The Prestige Showrunner Pass

Oct 18, 2026 · a full-season pass on *FULL NEW SEASON.md* as revised Oct 14, 2026

**What this is.** A season-wide diagnosis and revision of *The Green Gallows*, Season 1, built from the season document and the tracker's arc notes, with every change specified down to the scene. The season document is canon. Nothing here has been folded into it yet: each revision is written to be dropped in by scene number, and Deliverable 8 lists every line of the bible it touches.

**What this pass does not do.** It adds no characters, factions, rules, powers, objects or scenes. All 190 scenes stay, and every episode keeps its scene count (the tent-poles at 21, the pilot at 20). Every item on the "Kept from your outline" list survives, including the Snow Confession, which loses its exposition and gains a fight. About thirty scenes change, most of them by a line or two.

**How to read it.** Scene references ("Ep 6 sc 3") point at the season document as it stands. *Reading:* marks an interpretation of canon rather than a fact on the page. Proposed changes carry an R-number, R1 to R14, and are specified in full in Deliverable 5. Deliverables 6 to 8 apply them episode by episode, scene by scene, and row by row.

## The short version

*The Green Gallows* is better engineered than most produced television. Its causality is real: every set piece is a collision of named plans, and almost nothing happens "and then." Its four rules can be said in a sentence each. The sisters' reveal, where the creature proves she isn't Miriam by apologising better than Miriam ever could, is a genuine dramatic invention, and Ep 7 executes it with discipline. Fourteen passes have made the machine immaculate.

What fourteen passes haven't done is make the hero dangerous. "Every adult in this show hurts someone to keep them safe," and Nathaniel is shielded from that sentence at exactly the points where the season's question needs him inside it. He is a dupe at the Heartglass, a rescued man in the rubble and the snow, and a spectator at the choice that ends the season. *Can Nathaniel end the bargain without becoming the father who kept it?* is a fine question, but the season never once puts him in danger of becoming his father.

This pass gives him the family disease and makes him choose against it. He drops a witness into the Thames to bury his house's secret (Ep 4). He doesn't ask what his cage is tuned to (Ep 6). He keeps his mother's murder from the girl whose sister is being hunted for it (Ep 7). Then, in the snow with twenty minutes to midnight, his dying father offers to walk down the stair himself and buy fifty more years of light, and Nathaniel says no (Ep 9). Everything after that, his mother walking into the White included, is a bill he chose. The Record stops being a sermon and becomes his confession.

Around that spine, the finale's price gets a rule that makes it necessary, the Lost One gets a fight inside the cage, Tamara pays for her freedom with somebody else's sister, Josephine finally says the sentence her season is built on, and Byron tells the truth while it can still cost him.

## Deliverable 1: Executive diagnosis

### What works, and must be protected

1. **Causality.** The season obeys its own "therefore and but" rule better than most produced drama. Lantern Night, the conduit, the wedding and the last night all happen because three or four named people made separate plans for the same hour. Protect it: nothing in this pass adds a coincidence.
2. **The sisters.** The apology that's too perfect to be Miriam's (Ep 7 sc 3) is the best idea in the show. The fence, the counting game, eight posts and then *nine*, and the irony that the Lost One gives the apology Miriam never could, are built with care and pay off three times without wearing out.
3. **The rules.** Four rules, each sayable in a sentence. Rule 4 is the season's quiet masterstroke: every act of power costs the Lost One a piece of the sister she's pretending to be, so her strength and her love are in direct competition.
4. **The adults.** Josephine and Eldgard are the season's best-written characters. Neither is ever wrong about the problem, only about what they're willing to spend on it. "Then it had better not happen from my hands" and "I held his hand" are the work of a show that knows how power talks at a breakfast table.
5. **The ending's refusal.** Jamie is kept, not living. "Cold, not dead" is honoured to the letter and isn't a cheat, and the White gives nobody back. Most shows of this kind would have blinked.
6. **Objects that turn.** The handkerchief, the oranges, the oath with four meanings and the lantern Edric is still holding all change meaning instead of just recurring.
7. **The pilot.** Two parties, one blackout, one shot, one bridge, one morgue. The joke "You're the son he actually wanted, Nix" is a bomb under every scene those two men share afterwards.

### The nine problems that matter

**P1. The hero's hands are clean where the question needs them dirty.**
At the Heartglass, "Nathaniel assumes he means Morgana" (Ep 6 sc 3), and in Ep 7 "He didn't know. She watches him not know." This is the engineer who found Alice in a mile of tunnels by the pitch of his own design (Ep 3 sc 16), and yet he can't work out what his own cage is tuned to. That's a character-intelligence problem and a missed moral test in one beat. At the crane (Ep 4 sc 9) he drops Ajax seconds after Ajax says "I never touched him," and "He's Jamie's grave" could mean vengeance or burial. The choices table calls it justice for his brother, which the scene contradicts. And when Byron names his mother as Felix's killer (Ep 6), Nathaniel never tells Alice, whose sister is hunted under martial law for that murder. The bible never notices that he hasn't.
*Cost:* the season question is never live. "No one goes in the chain again" (Ep 10) is a principle he has never had to overcome in himself. The Record turns into an accusation from a man with clean hands, which is the one thing a prestige ending can't afford.

**P2. He is rescued, not tested, and the season's decisive choice is made for him.**
Three parents buy his survival: Byron in Ep 6, Eldgard in Ep 9, Josephine in Ep 10. Two of them die in the same shape. A father figure takes a blow meant for Nathaniel, dies in wreckage, and spends his last breath on a family confession and a blessing ("You were always enough" / "Don't be me"). At the climax Alice stands in front of him and Josephine steps past him, so his act on the hill is opening his hand. The Snow Confession, the outline's sacred scene, is a dying monologue to a son who only receives. It even re-explains what he already knows: the lock "opens with what closed it: Harding blood, this ring, and a blade that's already in it," when he went to Newgate for the ring precisely because he knew.
*Cost:* by Ep 9 the audience recognises the shape, and the season's biggest scene plays as a repeat. The protagonist becomes the person things are done for.

**P3. The finale's price is mechanically optional.**
Rule 3 says the White comes through "until dawn" and one of the house goes with her: "It has to be given. She won't take it." Eldgard tells his son "it takes one of us with it" (Ep 5 sc 16). The two contradict each other, and neither says what happens if nobody gives. If the White goes home at dawn regardless, Josephine's walk buys nothing.
*Cost:* the season's emotional climax collapses under one viewer question: *why does anyone have to go?* It also leaves Morgana waiting on Tyburn hill all night for no stated reason.

**P4. The Lost One is cargo for the run-in, and the season's mythic pair barely speak.**
From Ep 8 sc 11, when she holds out her wrists, to Ep 10 sc 13, when she sings, the co-lead of the sisters' story is either in a cage (all of Ep 9) or unconscious in a doorway (Ep 10 sc 3 to 10). Her Ep 9 is one exchange. Morgana and her daughter share about six lines before the finale. When Morgana draws the White out of her daughter (Ep 10 sc 16), she resolves the girl's fate with a power nobody has mentioned before that scene. And "I've spent everything else of hers... I kept this" pays off a spending we never watch her choose.
*Cost:* the climax's most important new relationship, a mother and daughter each refusing to let the other pay, arrives unbuilt, and the ending's mercy looks like a gift from the writers' room.

**P5. The romance's crisis is one-sided, and it hangs on an interrupted sentence.**
Ep 8 sc 4: "At first." She starts to say the rest, and he is already gone. That's the oldest romance-plot device there is, the misunderstanding that one more sentence would clear. Meanwhile the only betrayal on the table in Eps 8 and 9 is hers: she was paid. His betrayal, keeping his mother's crime from her, is invisible, so Ep 9's "whole fight" plays as a trial of Alice.
*Cost:* the most adult relationship in the show gets its most juvenile rupture, and the reconciliation reads as her earning his forgiveness.

**P6. Tamara reaches power without paying for it.**
She's a victim (of her father, Ajax, Bethlem) and a hero (the ledger, the door, the key, the coal), but never a compromised person. Her Vykra thread, the reason she joined ("I want to know who") and the theme map's "She outgrows it," evaporates after the code in Ep 5. At Westminster she threatens to close the depots on a freezing city unless the record goes into Hansard, yet the theme map files those depots under "A gift nobody can price." The season's best Tamara scene contradicts the season's own reading of her.
*Cost:* "the player who holds both houses" arrives by plot rather than by choice, and Season 2's "more dangerous" Tamara has no first step.

**P7. The drawing-room war is reported, not staged, and the mirrors the bible built never face each other.**
The tone promises "*House of Cards* in the drawing rooms." But the light war between Eldgard and Lambrick, two patriarchs trading one marriage, never puts them in a room together. The Charter vote is blown up before it's held, and the board seizure and martial law arrive as announcements. Josephine and Alice are two knife-girls sent to a Harding thirty years apart ("That girl holds her wrist like a knife-girl." "You'd know, madam."), and they never have a conversation.
*Cost:* the political register is furniture. Lambrick's escalation from a hearing-room argument to bombing Parliament's vote has no "therefore."

**P8. Josephine's whole season rests on a sentence nobody says.**
She placed the contract on her younger son weeks before her elder son was due to die (Ep 2 sc 9). She held Jamie's scarf on the bank while Eldgard taught him to be brave (Ep 9 cold open), and she "knew what this house spends" (Ep 7 sc 15). Every move she makes protects one son, and the other is never in her mouth. The final image, Josephine sitting down beside Jamie in the White, answers a question the season never asks aloud.
*Cost:* the final scene is beautiful and unearned in exactly one respect: we don't know it's an answer.

**P9. The last hour preaches, and it stacks.**
The Record's voice-over states the thesis outright: "For four hundred years this city was warm because somebody was cold... ask who is paying for it." That breaks the bible's own promise that the season "never says which side is right." Ep 10 sc 18 asks one scene to hold grief for a mother plus five romance payoffs: the handkerchief, the counting, nine, the name and the kiss. "The most hated man in London" is asserted and never shown, and Vane's warrant is signed and then forgotten. The Snow Confession, meanwhile, spends its length on facts the audience already has.
*Cost:* the finale tells the audience what to feel, in sequence, and the protagonist's price stays abstract.

## Deliverable 2: The season's dramatic thesis

**The central dramatic question.** *Once Nathaniel Harding can hear who has been paying for his family's light, will he keep the lamps lit as every Harding before him did, or end the bargain knowing the ending must also be paid for, and by someone he loves?* The sisters' question sits under it unchanged: will Alice Bounds let the girl wearing her sister's face choose what she pays?

In one line for the room: **can you stop someone paying for you without choosing who pays instead?**

**The primary external conflict.** By midnight on the first snow, a Harding must walk down to the chain of their own will, or it breaks. Every faction is racing to make somebody else be the one who pays. The Hardings want a raised son or a bred spare. Lambrick wants a girl in a glass cage and his coal back in the grates. The Crown wants the lights on and its signature kept in a drawer. The Vykra sell to whoever is buying, and the Guard obeys until it doesn't. And the creature in the chain wants out.

**The central emotional conflict.** Nathaniel believes that being useful is the same as being loved, and his father's love really is indistinguishable from use. The son who most wants to be useful is the one who can hear what usefulness costs, and he has spent sixteen years putting that sound away. Alice is his mirror. She believes love means holding on, because nobody ever held the line for her, and she has to learn that letting go and standing in front are different acts, and which one each person in front of her needs.

**The thematic argument.** A city's comfort is kept up less by villains than by decent people who stop asking: Vane never asks, Byron "stopped hearing it when I stopped letting myself," the glim-runners blame the batch, and Nathaniel files things away. Protection is the respectable name for control. The only clean payments are given, never bought or taken. The season does *not* argue that truth redeems anyone. Its argument is narrower and harder: the price gets paid either way, and the only difference between complicity and responsibility is whether you write down who paid. Otty got a brass plate that lied; Josephine gets the record. Once R12 lands, Nathaniel gets the record too, with his own name in it.

**The principal contradiction.** Nathaniel can only end the bargain that spends Hardings by breaking the chain, and breaking the chain spends a Harding. Every exit from the system runs through its own arithmetic. He refuses to let anyone else pay, his father in Ep 9 and Nixon in Ep 10, and means to pay himself, but the people who love him won't let him. So the season ends on the transaction it opened with, run backwards. In 1489 a father handed his son a lantern and sent him down the stair; in 1889 a mother takes her son's place. That inversion is the only progress the season allows, and it still costs a life.

**The irreversible transformation.** London has no light. The Harding line of payment is over, and Morgana is free. Josephine sits in the White beside the son she didn't choose. The Lost One is mortal, warm and carrying Jamie's child. Nixon commands a Guard that answers to no one. Tamara holds the city's heat and has learned to use it as a lever. The Crown's crime is in Hansard, and its annex is still locked in Vane's drawer. Nathaniel goes from heir to witness, and from witness to defendant.

**How these elements interact.** The external clock forces the emotional question, because each faction's plan to make somebody else pay lands on one character's ability to stop asking or to start hearing. The theme is argued by choices rather than speeches, and the outcomes refuse to line up into a moral: Grise survives everything, Vane keeps his annex, Tamara learns her father's arithmetic, the truth saves nobody, and the ending is "worse, but ours." Every major character answers the season's question differently:

## Deliverable 5: Revised seasonal architecture

The bible's four movements are sound and stay: the promise and the sisters (Eps 1–3), the engine proven with the midpoint in Ep 5 (Eps 4–5), everything worse and the trap sprung (Eps 6–8), and one night (Eps 9–10). What changes is the line running underneath them. Each movement now turns Nathaniel's complicity one notch, so the external escalation and the protagonist's moral escalation climb together instead of in parallel.

| Movement | External turn | Nathaniel's turn (revised) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I. The promise (1–3) | The shot, the morgue, the hunt | He hears the weeping and puts it away (1). He hands the case to the shooter (2). His first crime is for a stranger: he hides the Frost Killer from his father (3) |
| II. The engine (4–5) | Felix dies on the stand; the light is a woman | His first crime for the house: he drops the only witness to keep his brother's secret, and his father pays him for it with the gallery (4, R3). He chooses his father (5) |
| III. Worse (6–8) | The Works fall; the sister and the friend break; the girl is taken; the snow comes | He doesn't ask (6, R2). He breaks a seal and keeps a secret in the same episode (7, R4). He marries to save someone while his silence costs Alice her sister (8) |
| IV. One night (9–10) | The assault; the lock; the hill | His father offers to pay and he says no (9, R1). He opens the lock, tries to pay himself, lets his mother go, and writes all of it down, himself included (10, R12) |

### Turning points

1. **The end of Ep 1.** The shot, then the morgue. Nixon takes the leash, and the sisters' story is reborn wrong.
2. **Ep 3 sc 16.** Nathaniel hides the Frost Killer, his first act against his own lie.
3. **Ep 4 sc 9 to 10 (strengthened).** The drop. Father and son now share a secret, and the son is paid for it.
4. **The midpoint, Ep 5 (unchanged).** The light is a woman, Nathaniel is the next Tithe, and he chooses his father. With R3 the midpoint gains a hook: the gallery he walks into in sc 2 was bought with a man in the Thames.
5. **Ep 6 sc 3 (new weight).** The unasked question. The temptation of the clean solution, accepted by not looking at it.
6. **Ep 7.** Seals break: "You're not her," the coffin, "Did you ask?"
7. **Ep 8.** A morning of silences, where every protective act is also a betrayal: Alice's "Yes," Tamara's door, Josephine's sale, the Lost One's wrists, and Nathaniel's promise coming due.
8. **The central crisis, Ep 9 sc 15 (moved into the protagonist's hands).** The offer and the refusal. The season's decisive choice now belongs to the person whose question it is.
9. **The climax, Ep 10 sc 5 and sc 16 to 17.** The lock. The price, now necessary (R5). "Not him." "Go."
10. **The resolution, Ep 10 sc 19 to 21.** The warrant stands, the Record is a confession, and the White keeps what it was given.

**Why this is stronger.** In the bible as it stands, the back half escalates by things done *to* Nathaniel: the Works taken, Byron killed, the coffin, the receipt, his father arrested and then dead. In the revision, every back-half episode costs him something because of a choice he made. The drop in Ep 4 is why his father dies in Ep 9. His silence in Ep 7 is why Alice is in the gallery and not the glasshouse in Ep 8. His "No" in Ep 9 is why his mother is on the hill in Ep 10. The plot was already causal; now the protagonist is one of its causes.

### The revision register

Each substantial change is set out in eight parts: the weakness, the opportunity, the change, its effect on character and relationships, its effect on the season arc, the setup it needs, the payoff it needs, and any continuity conflict. The problems each revision answers are in brackets.

#### R1. The father's offer *[P1, P2, P9]*
- **Weakness.** The Snow Confession is a second deathbed confession (after Byron's), a monologue to a son who only listens, and it re-explains the lock mechanics Nathaniel already knows. The season's decisive choice, who pays, is made on the hill by Alice and Josephine.
- **Opportunity.** Already in canon: "I could have gone down that stair myself. I raised a boy for it instead." A dying Harding can still walk down before midnight, and Rule 1 doesn't care how old he is.
- **Change.** After that line, with twenty minutes on the tower clock, Eldgard offers: get me to the stair, I'll walk the last of it, the chain holds, you'll have fifty years. Nathaniel: "And in fifty years?" "You'll be seventy-one." "And somebody will be twenty-one." Then: "No." The lock-mechanics line is cut and the annex is compressed to one line. In Ep 10 sc 11 Josephine learns her husband is dead, that he offered to walk, and that her son refused, and that her son means to be the price. That is what sends her up the hill first, and it's why "Go to her" is a lie.
- **Character and relationships.** Nathaniel's founding lie (usefulness is love) dies in the act of refusing to let his father be useful. The father–son story ends in a real exchange instead of a recital. Josephine's final act gets a visible cause. Alice, kneeling at his back, says nothing: her sister went into the glass to free the woman in the hooks, so her silence is consent, and "His hand finds hers behind his back" becomes agreement.
- **Season arc.** The central crisis moves to the protagonist. The hill becomes the bill for a choice he made with his eyes open.
- **Setup.** Ep 5 sc 16 ("Why not you?", his silence). Ep 9 sc 2 (the plan to open the lock). Rule 1.
- **Payoff.** Ep 10 sc 11, then sc 16 to 17, then the Record: "At a quarter to twelve he offered to walk down... I said no."
- **Continuity.** "It has to walk down on its own feet" (Lambrick, Ep 9 sc 4) still holds: Eldgard means to walk the last of it. Snow Confession I (sc 14) is unchanged. The Ep 9 grid, the choices table and the tracker arc notes need updating (Deliverable 8).

#### R2. The unasked question *[P1]*
- **Weakness.** The engineer doesn't understand his own device. At the one moment the season could make him complicit in his father's arithmetic, it makes him a dupe.
- **Opportunity.** Not asking is already Nathaniel's established habit: "He doesn't ask about the counting. He keeps it" (Ep 1). "He doesn't think to ask" (Ep 4). "He puts the word *cold* away" (Ep 5). And it's the house's sin: Vane "never once asks," and Byron stopped letting himself hear.
- **Change.** Ep 6 sc 3: "Of her blood." His pencil stops. It's one line of resonance arithmetic and he doesn't run it. Ep 6 sc 8: "We can save both of them" is said by a man who hasn't checked his sums. Ep 7 sc 16: "I didn't know." "Did you ask?" He has no answer, and she hits him for that.
- **Character and relationships.** He becomes complicit through his own habit, not through stupidity, and Alice's anger now has a true target.
- **Season arc.** His arc becomes legible: from the son who doesn't ask to the man who puts himself on the record.
- **Setup.** The three "doesn't ask" beats above, all existing.
- **Payoff.** Ep 7 sc 16, and the Record: "I built a cage on my father's bench and didn't ask who it was for."
- **Continuity.** The Ep 6 grid ("He doesn't know what the Heartglass needs") changes to "He doesn't ask what the Heartglass needs."

#### R3. Jamie's grave *[P1]*
- **Weakness.** Nathaniel drops Ajax right after Ajax clears himself ("I never touched him"). "He's Jamie's grave" is ambiguous, and the choices table reads the drop as justice for his brother.
- **Opportunity.** Ajax is the one witness who could tell the Crown that Jamie bought the blackout and that "your father went down after." Burying him buries the house.
- **Change.** Upside down over the river, Ajax bargains: pull me up and I'll sing it to the Crown, Lambrick's gold and your brother's, your father on the stairs after, your whole house in the dock. "He's Jamie's grave." He lets go. In Ep 4 sc 10, Eldgard opens with "Lambrick's man is in the river, I hear." "I heard that too." Then the bait: "After the hearing I'll take you into the work."
- **Character and relationships.** This is the first values clash between the leads: Alice says "Pull him up" because she wants the truth out, and he buries it. Father and son share their first secret, and the son is paid for it.
- **Season arc.** It plants the Harding act at the season's quarter mark, and the Ep 5 midpoint now opens on a reward for a crime.
- **Setup.** Ep 4 sc 9's existing "Your father went down after."
- **Payoff.** Ep 9 sc 13 ("You should've pulled me up, Harding") and the Record: "I let a man drop into the Thames so he couldn't tell the Crown what my brother did."
- **Continuity.** The choices table's Ep 4 cost column changes from "Justice for his brother" to "The case against Lambrick, and the truth about his own house."

#### R4. The silence about Felix *[P1, P5]*
- **Weakness.** Nathaniel learns his mother poisoned Felix (Ep 6) and never tells Alice, whose sister is hunted under martial law for that murder. The bible never registers the silence, so the betrayal in Eps 8 and 9 is one-sided.
- **Opportunity.** He has a credible reason to keep it: it's his only leverage over his mother ("If anything happens to the frost girl...", Ep 7 sc 4), and leverage only works while it's private. His mother's answer, "Then it had better not happen from my hands," was a plan, not a surrender.
- **Change.** Ep 7 sc 16: Alice says they'll come for her sister here. He doesn't tell her. He says "Nothing will happen to her from this house. I've seen to it," and means it. Ep 9 sc 2: Alice has put it together from the poison, the receipt, the pew smile and "Josephine's directions." "When did you know?" "The night Byron died." Then she quotes his promise back to him.
- **Character and relationships.** Each lead held something back, and both things carry Josephine's handwriting. The fight becomes fair, and the reconciliation is mutual.
- **Season arc.** It makes the taking in Ep 8 partly his fault, which earns the "debt he can never repay" the bible already names.
- **Setup.** Ep 4 sc 15 (Alice: "Winter's kiss. That's Vykra."), Ep 6 (Byron), Ep 7 sc 4.
- **Payoff.** Ep 9 sc 2, and Josephine's entry in the Record.
- **Continuity.** None. It uses silence the canon already contains.

#### R5. The price, defined *[P3]*
- **Weakness.** Rule 3 contradicts Ep 5 sc 16 ("it takes one of us" against "She won't take it"), and nothing happens if nobody gives.
- **Change.** Rule 3 becomes: *If the chain breaks, she goes free, the light goes out for good, and the White comes through behind her and stays, winter on London, until one of the house that chained her goes with her into it. It has to be given. She won't take it.* Eldgard in Ep 5 sc 16: "...the White comes through, and it doesn't close until one of us goes with her." Ep 10 sc 16 shows it: she waits, the wolves lie down, the storm stands still over London, and down the hill Gin Lane's coal burns towards its last ring.
- **Character and relationships.** It explains Morgana's all-night vigil, gives Alice's "Don't" a cost (if he doesn't go and nobody does, the city freezes), and makes Josephine's gift necessary.
- **Season arc.** The climax stops being optional.
- **Setup.** The world rules and Ep 5 sc 16.
- **Payoff.** Ep 10 sc 16 to 18: the snow stops in the middle of falling when she's paid.
- **Continuity.** The logline's "midnight to dawn" holds, because payment comes at dawn. The holding actions now hold until somebody pays rather than until sunrise, which plays identically.

#### R6. The cage *[P4]*
- **Weakness.** The Lost One is inert from Ep 8 sc 11 to Ep 10 sc 13. The mother–daughter core is underwritten, the drawing-out of the White is unplanted, and "I kept this" pays off a spending we never see.
- **Change.** Ep 9 sc 10 expands, with no new scene. Every time Morgana pulls against her hooks a crack runs, and every time the daughter breathes it shut, paying one of Miriam's memories, and we see which one goes. They argue in the language of the White: "Let it break." "No." Morgana: "I could take the White out of you. You'd be warm. You'd be theirs. You'd die like them." The daughter keeps one thing back.
- **Character and relationships.** The Lost One gets her last real fight. Morgana gets a voice, still spare. The two of them trying to pay for each other is the mother-and-daughter version of "Not her" and "Not him."
- **Season arc.** It explains why she's half crystal in Ep 10, and why Alice has to carry her.
- **Setup.** Rule 4, and Ep 8 sc 20.
- **Payoff.** Ep 10 sc 2 (the mother wins the tug-of-war because the daughter has nothing left to seal with), Ep 10 sc 16 ("I kept this," and the drawing-out).
- **Continuity.** The tracker's Lost One Ep 9 arc note changes from "Cracking the cage" to "Holding the cage."

#### R7. Byron tells him alive *[P2]*
- **Weakness.** Byron's truth arrives as a dying confession, the same shape Eldgard's takes three episodes later, from a man with nothing left to lose.
- **Change.** He tells the boy at the conduit grate on Tyburn Road, after lighting the green lamp and letting himself hear her to the end of the phrase: "Your mother killed the inspector." "Not now, Byron." "I've been waiting for a better time for thirty years." Under the beam he says only the two lines that aren't information.
- **Character and relationships.** His arc completes as a choice with a price, not a deathbed reflex. His betrayal of Josephine is made by a man who expects to walk home and face her.
- **Season arc.** It leaves Eldgard's as the only deathbed confession, and that one becomes an offer (R1).
- **Payoff.** The Ep 6 tag is unchanged ("He told me about Inspector Hawke." "Then you know I love you.").
- **Continuity.** The Ep 6 sting and grid change. The ledger's Felix row should cite Ep 6 sc 10, not sc 14.

#### R8. "Yes." *[P5]*
- **Weakness.** "At first. She starts to say the rest, and he is already gone": the interrupted explanation.
- **Change.** She reads the receipt, looks at his wedding coat and the clock, and knows what the rest of that sentence would do to the next hour: a groom who can't say "I will," and a bride in a strait-coat by supper. "Yes." The Lost One watched her do it: "You let him think it." "He has to get married." Then "Go. Watch him," and "Yes, Ali," a loving lie learned a minute earlier from the expert.
- **Character and relationships.** Alice rehearses letting go two episodes before the hill, and the Lost One learns how to lie for love from her sister.
- **Payoff.** Ep 9 sc 2's "rest of the sentence" becomes a gift she held back, not a correction.
- **Continuity.** None.

#### R9. Tamara's door *[P6]*
- **Weakness.** Tamara is never compromised. Her Vykra thread dies after Ep 5, and the chapel-door glove beat is a look whose meaning is old news.
- **Change.** In Ep 7 sc 13, after the veiled client leaves, Grise has her newest girl encipher the job: *One girl. The Harding glasshouse. The hour to follow.* Ep 8 sc 6, at the chapel door: the glove, the knot. "The glasshouse." Josephine, settling the lace: "Is your wedding." One word to the groom and he's across the lawn, and the order on her father's desk finds a spinster at the altar. Tamara takes her bouquet.

- **Season arc.** Her coal threat at Westminster becomes the culmination of an arc, not a twist. The master key in Ep 9 and the coal for Gin Lane in Ep 10 become repayment nobody names.

- **Payoff.** Ep 8 sc 6, then Eps 9 and 10, then Season 2. The fuse is left lit on purpose.
- **Continuity.** Theme table 6's "She outgrows it" becomes "She uses it once, and buys her freedom with somebody else's sister." Theme table 2's depot row is rewritten (Deliverable 8).

- **Change.** In Ep 2 sc 9, Grise, counting the gold: "You placed this before your elder boy died." Josephine: "I placed it for the younger." In Ep 8 sc 19, Eldgard: "Sold him? You held his scarf on the Serpentine while I taught him to be brave." Josephine: "I had two sons. I chose one."

- **Season arc.** The Ep 9 cold open (the scarf on the bank) turns into a visual confession that lands one scene after the words. The final scene (she sits down beside him) becomes the answer to a question we've now heard asked.
- **Continuity.** It's consistent with Ep 7 sc 4 ("I chose a long time ago"), which now has its true meaning.

### 8. The cage (Ep 9 sc 10) · R6

**Problem.** The Lost One spends the run-in as cargo, the mother and daughter barely speak, and Morgana's mercy in the finale is unplanted.
**Intended result.** A silent war nobody on the gallery can see: a mother and daughter each paying for the other, with the price visible on the girl's face.

**10. The Works, the gallery and the core below.** Snow thickens over Tyburn. Where it lies deep, wolves start to shape themselves out of the drifts. Vane is nervous. In his breast pocket is the sealed 1689 annex to the Harding Charter, the Crown's own signature on the chain; every Lord Chancellor has kept it locked in his desk, and tonight he took it out. Lambrick: "Midnight. The girl, or the boy."

Below them the Heartglass hums towards the trade, and there's a war in it. Every time the glass leans the trade towards the daughter, her mother hauls against her own hooks and a crack runs, *tik*. Every time, the Lost One puts her mouth to the inside of the glass and breathes, and the crack frosts shut, *krrk*. And something goes. We see it go, a flicker over her face each time. The chestnut man and his drum. *Tik. Krrk.* The pigeons on the loft. *Tik. Krrk.* House rules in chalk, cocoa allowed. *Tik. Krrk.* A long ruined spiral of orange peel. Gone.

Morgana, hoarse, in the language of the White: "Let it break." "No." "You were free. Why did you come back?" "Because you're my mother." *Tik. Krrk.* "Every crack you close costs you hers. You'll come out of that glass with nothing of her in you." "Then I'll come out with nothing." Morgana hangs in her hooks and looks at her child the way she once looked at Alice, with something terribly like pity. "I could take the White out of you. You'd be warm. You'd be theirs. You'd die like them." *Tik.* The Lost One doesn't answer. She is holding one thing very carefully, the way you'd carry a full cup up a stair, and she won't spend it, not even on her mother. *Krrk.* Up on the gallery, Lambrick watches the glass hold and calls it a good sign. Footsteps on Byron's old Guard stair: Josephine is coming down.

### 9. The offer and the bill (Ep 9 sc 15, then Ep 10 sc 11) · R1

**Problem.** The second deathbed confession in four episodes: a monologue that repeats what the son knows, followed by a climax where others decide who pays.
**Intended result.** The confession becomes a two-sided scene with the season's hardest choice at the end of it, and the next episode sends him the bill.

### 10. Dawn, Westminster, and the Record (Ep 10 sc 18–20) · R13, R12

**Problem.** The dawn stacks five romance payoffs on a mother's death. The Record states the thesis, and every name in it belongs to somebody else. The hero's price, the city's hatred and the warrant, is never shown.

**18. Tyburn hill, dawn.** The snow stops in the middle of falling. Nathaniel is on his knees where his mother was, and for a long time nothing happens. Alice doesn't touch him. ## Deliverable 9: Final quality-control pass

Read cold, as a showrunner who didn't write it.

**Does every major character have meaningful agency?** Yes, and more evenly than before. Nathaniel now makes the season's decisive choice himself (Ep 9) instead of having it made for him on the hill. The Lost One fights through the run-in (R6) instead of hanging in a cage. Tamara's power now comes from a choice with a victim (R9). The thinnest agency left is Vane's: he's a functionary by design, and that's acceptable for a season-one Crown.

**Are the important conflicts generated by credible motives?** Yes. Two motives were soft and are now one line each: Lambrick striking at the altar (R14) and Lambrick escalating to the bomb (R11). Morgana's early snow stays grief, not strategy, which is defensible but noted below.

**Do the relationships change because of what happens?** Yes. The revisions add consequences that persist: Alice's trust after "I've seen to it," Tamara's unspoken debt, Josephine learning what her son refused. Nathaniel and Nixon still end unforgiven, which is correct.

**Does the season escalate through consequences rather than shocks?** It already did externally. Now it does through the protagonist too: the drop leads to Ajax and his father's death, the silence to the taking, and the "No" to his mother on the hill.

**Are the reveals properly prepared?** Morgana's drawing-out of the White is now planted in Ep 9. Josephine's choice is planted in Ep 2 and said in Ep 8. The baby, the Orchard and the annex were already well prepared.

**Are characters contradictory without becoming inconsistent?** Nathaniel is a decent man with his house's disease. Tamara is a victim who becomes a beneficiary. Josephine never repents and pays anyway. Each contradiction is set up before it's used.

**Does the dialogue carry both the practical and the emotional?** It's better in the new places: "Grandchildren, Hector, if you behave." "Is your wedding." "And somebody will be twenty-one." "Did you ask?" The Record no longer sermonises. The remaining risk is the Snow Confession, which is still long even trimmed. Directors must play it as a struggle between three people, not a speech to two.

**Does every major subplot feed the larger system?** Yes. Tamara's Vykra thread, which was dead after Ep 5, now carries the season's most consequential secret.

**Does the finale resolve the central conflict through character choices?** Yes: Nathaniel's "No" and the lock, Alice's "Us" and "Don't," the Lost One's choice to stay, Morgana's mercy and her price, and Josephine's "Not him." With R5, the price is necessary rather than ceremonial.

**Does the ending create a genuinely changed world?** Yes. No light, no Tithe, a free Morgana, a mortal girl carrying a Harding child, an independent Guard, a coal queen, a Crown with its crime in Hansard and its annex in a drawer, and a hero under warrant.

**Have new clichés crept in during the repair?** Four risks, all manageable:
1. *The secret debt* (Tamara) turns soap if Season 2 detonates it for shock. It must go off through a choice, ideally Tamara's own.
2. *The refused deathbed wish* can read as Nathaniel being cruel. The ambiguity line protects it ("how much of that No is for the woman in the hooks..."), but the actor has to play love, not punishment.
3. *The memory-flicker* in the cage can become a montage device. Limit it to four flickers, with no score, and never use it again.
4. *"Did you ask?"* can play as a gotcha. Alice isn't scoring a point; she's asking because she wants him to say yes.

**Has the revision preserved the original's identity?** Yes. There are no new characters, factions, rules, objects or scenes, and every scene count is unchanged. Every "Kept from your outline" item survives. Every quotable moment survives except the kiss, which moves house.

### What this pass can't fix without a deeper redesign, in priority order

1. **Convergence fatigue.** Five of ten episodes use one engine: several plans collide on one night (Eps 5, 6, 8, 9, 10). By Ep 9 the audience can predict that every faction will arrive in the same room. The real fix is to rebuild one of Eps 6 or 9 in a different shape. The strongest candidate is Ep 6 told from the Lost One's side as she forgets Alice, a single-point-of-view episode with the gasworks seen from inside the forgetting. That rebuilds an episode the bible has iterated many times, so it's offered, not done.
2. **Payoff saturation.** Ninety-eight ledger rows. Even after the cuts above, the density of rhymes makes the season feel engineered, and prestige television leaves some debts unpaid. The fix is a dedicated subtraction pass that removes fifteen to twenty callbacks, which is a writers'-room decision about which ones the show loves.
3. **The White and the Lost One's four hundred years are never seen** until the final scene. Every cold open looks back at Harding history and none looks out of the White. One cold open from the daughter's side (the White in 1489, the wolves, the long nothing) would give the Lost One an interior beyond Miriam's borrowed one. The cost is that it spends the final scene's first glimpse of the White, so it's a real trade.
4. **Morgana's agency.** She acts by grief (the snow, the scream, the refusal) rather than by intent. A creature with four hundred years to plan is passive until the finale. Making the early snow a choice, breaking the timetable before the cage can close, would make her an antagonist as well as a victim. It risks the rule that she "speaks rarely, and every word costs someone," so it needs care.
5. **The political world is thin.** Parliament, the Charter and the Crown board are announcements, and Vane has no private contradiction. Season 2's warrant and trial are the natural place to fix it. Doing it now would mean a Vane subplot this season doesn't have room for.
6. **Byron and Eldgard never share a scene.** One man spared the assassin, the other married her, and Byron serves at the table of the man the woman he loved chose. It's a small, unclaimed chord, and the only fix is a scene the season doesn't have space for.
