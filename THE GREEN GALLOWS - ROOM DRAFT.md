# The Green Gallows — Season One, the Room Draft

Nov 10, 2026 · a ground-up redraft of `FULL NEW SEASON.md`, written after the surgical edit

Nine episodes in three acts. Same London, same sisters, same boy and lamp-girl. Half the machinery is gone, and what is left has to be carried by people.

## What this draft is

**Kept, because it was already the show:** the green city and the woman under it; Jamie's death and the best friend who fired; Miriam on the bridge and the thing that wakes up in her; "You're not her"; the counting game and the eightfold fence; the lamp-girl with the match; "Bounds"; the handkerchief; the wedding she watches from the gallery; "Us"; the Snow Confession; the name he says at dawn; Josephine walking into the White.

**Cut, because it was plumbing:** the Orchard, the Hargrave herd, Tobias Marsh and his rhyme; the Heartglass and its lever; the Crown board, Lord Chancellor Vane, the 1689 annex and Hansard; the lunacy order; Jamie paying Ajax for the blackout; Tamara in the Vykra, the needle and the tea-girl; the Lost One opening and shutting the White; the rule that a Harding must go with Morgana.

**Changed:**

- **Nixon is Eldgard's unacknowledged son.** No cellar, no file. He is a bastard placed in a charity school and watched for twenty years. The boy who wants to belong is offered the family name, and the price is the stair.
- **The firstborn pays and the second inherits.** That is the Harding custom. Nathaniel has spent his life believing he was the spare nobody looked at. He was the one being saved, and his father could not look at him without counting what it cost.
- **The last night is a three-way choice with three different outcomes.** Pay, open, or do nothing. Each one is a different London.
- **The wedding is Nathaniel's plan, not a rescue.** He marries Tamara because her coal is the only thing that will keep people alive on the night he puts out the lights. Alice tells him to.
- **The villain's sin is the founder's sin.** Lambrick nets the daughter and bleeds her, exactly as Alistair did in 1489, and that is what brings the snow.

**New:** paired gauntlets that double when two people fire in time; the clockwork gauntlet that runs on nothing stolen; Sergeant Ida Voss, the Lantern who keeps the oath as written; and a name the Lost One picks for herself.

Where it is not, it is my reading of the work on screen, and it should be taken as that.

**What each table brought, and the house rule it became**

1. **Write the last scene first.** Amanda Overton has said the final scene between Arcane's sisters was written years early and stayed the room's "guiding light" ([GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/entertainment/animation-shows/vi-and-jinxs-final-arcane-scene-has-never-changed-since-being-written-six-years-ago-it-has-stayed-our-guiding-light-for-the-entire-series)). Isayama has said the same of his ending, and that he could not soften it without changing what the story was ([SlashFilm](https://slashfilm.com/1439560/attack-on-titan-creator-planned-ending-start)). Ours is two scenes: a girl on a hill telling her sister "Go, if you want to," and a boy saying a name nobody paid for. Every episode is built backward from those.
2. **Start the lovers as far apart as the world allows.** The Arcane room decided its central romance early, began the pair as far apart as possible, and held that big romances need obstacles ([Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/arcanes-amanda-overton-talks-love-conflict-and-all-things-caitvi-2000537334), [Inverse](https://www.inverse.com/gaming/arcane-season-2-interview-showrunner-christian-linke-amanda-overton)). Alex Yee calls the best relationships the ones where two people "have to reach across that divide" ([ONE Esports](https://www.oneesports.gg/lol/arcane-hit-series-creators-christian-alex/)). Ours has four divides stacked: class, an engagement, a contract, and the fact that his family keeps her sister's mother in chains.
3. **Only trauma splits sisters.** Yee again: the sisters had to be "so emotionally entwined that only an event that traumatic" could part them ([SYFY](https://www.syfy.com/syfy-wire/arcane-co-creator-alex-yee-unpacks-the-act-i-twists?amp)). Five episodes of closeness before the break.
4. **Know every answer, and spend them slowly.** Dan Erickson keeps a long lore document and has a final scene in his head, and still cuts reveals to leave the audience room ([Hollywood Reporter](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/tv/tv-news/severance-season-2-theories-dan-erickson-interview-1236110099/), [GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/entertainment/sci-fi-shows/severance-season-2-finales-original-script-explained-more-about-lumons-agenda-but-creator-dan-erickson-wanted-to-leave-room-for-people-to-discuss)). So: four rules, each sayable in a sentence, and no character ever acts on a fact we have not watched them receive.
5. **Shock only moves story.** Eric Kripke's rule is that the room can be violent and shocking "only if it advances the story" and touches a character ([SlashFilm](https://www.slashfilm.com/eric-kripke-interview-the-boys/), [Hidden Remote](https://hiddenremote.com/2019/07/25/the-boys-showrunner-eric-kripke)). His other one: "Bad for the world, good for the show" ([Salon](https://salon.com/2024/12/28/the-boys-eric-kripke)). Every death here changes what someone alive does next.
6. **The cage is self-built.** Marks and Kondo wrote Mariko so that most of her restrictions are self-imposed, and gave her no superhuman skill; her absence does more than her presence ([Salon](https://www.salon.com/2024/04/17/shogun-anna-sawai-mariko)). That is Josephine, Byron and Nixon. Nobody locks them in. And Byron, Jamie and Otty do more after they are gone than before.
7. **The sisters are the beating heart.** Alison Schapker's phrase for the Harkonnen sisters ([Inverse](https://inverse.com/entertainment/dune-prophecy-interview-showrunner-cast)). When a plot beat and a sister beat want the same minute, the sister beat gets it.
8. **Every power has a bill.** This is my reading of the mangaka's table, from the work: a technique is interesting in proportion to what it costs its user, and the audience should be able to do the arithmetic. The Lost One's cold costs her a memory every time. The gauntlets cost someone else's blood, and from Episode 5 the heroes know it.
9. **The past is a cold open, never a lecture.** Also from the work: Isayama and Kobayashi deliver history as scenes with a child in them. Nine cold opens, eight in the past, each with someone young being asked to be brave.

## The pitch

London, November 1889, and the whole city glows green. Every lamp, tram and furnace burns gallowglass, a crystal only House Harding knows how to grow, at the Harding Works on the old gallows field at Tyburn. The Hardings say it grows from iron in the ground. It grows from a woman. For four hundred years they have kept a mother from the frozen world beyond, the White, chained under the Works, and they bleed her for light. Once every fifty winters, on the night of the first snow, a Harding walks down and gives the lock their life. The last was a girl of nineteen who went down singing. The next was Jamie Harding, and on the night of his brother's engagement he tried to open the chain and died on it.

**Series engine.** London runs on a secret. Every week, someone who loves someone decides what they are allowed to know.

**Season question.** Can Nathaniel Harding end the bargain that lights London without becoming his father? Under it runs the sisters' question: will Alice Bounds let the thing wearing her dead sister's face choose its own life?

**Theme.** Protection as control. Every adult in this show hurts someone to keep them safe. The young ones have to find out whether love can let go.

**Tone.** The dread of *Chernobyl*, the sisters of *Arcane*, the drawing rooms of *Shōgun*. Whimsy breathes between the violence: a creature learning what an orange is, a recruit hanging in someone's laundry, two friends who agree to be polite to each other for forty years.

**The clock.** Nobody knows the date. The Tithe falls on the first snow, whenever it comes. Every episode opens on the great snow-glass over the Works gate and a card: **9 NOVEMBER. NO SNOW.** Every grey sky is a threat. At the end of Episode 7, it snows.

## The world and its four rules

A viewer should be able to say each of these in one sentence by the end of Episode 5.

1. **The light is her blood.** Gallowglass is Morgana's blood, frozen. It burns green and warm. Get close enough to it, or grind it into the drug called glim, and you hear her crying.
2. **The chain is iron and a promise.** In 1489 she agreed to three things: she stays, her daughter goes free, and once every fifty winters, on the night of the first snow, a Harding lays a hand on the lock by choice and the lock takes them. A forced hand feeds nothing. If any term is broken, the chain breaks, and then she owes London nothing: the White comes through and stays.
3. **There is a way out, and every Keeper has known it.** The lock opens to what closed it: Harding blood freely given, and the wolf-crest ring. Open it and she goes home, the door shuts behind her at dawn, and the light goes out for good.
4. **The daughter's cold costs her Miriam.** It gets loose when she feels too much, and it kills. Every time, it burns one of Miriam's memories. It cannot freeze Harding blood, and cold iron burns her, so she can never touch the lock herself.

**The three roads on the last night.** This is the whole finale, and it is on the table from Episode 5.

| If, by midnight on the first snow | London gets |
| --- | --- |
| A Harding pays | Fifty more years of light. She stays in her hooks. |
| A Harding opens the lock | One night of the White, then a dark, cold city that is still a city. |
| Nobody does either | The chain breaks. The White stays. London is kept the way everything given to the White is kept: standing, silent, exactly as it was. |

**What the White is.** Snow, wind and wolves on the far side of the chain. Time stops there. Whatever this side gives it stays exactly as it went in and does not come back. Everyone the lock ever took is standing in it.

**The crack.** Jamie had no ring, so he tried a gallowglass blade and his own blood. The lock half-opened and took him. Since that night it has been cracked, and the White leaks through the thin places: frost on one bridge and nowhere else, then a wolf in the fog, then more.

**The gear**

- **Lantern gauntlet.** A forearm rig with a crystal core. Fires a flash-burst that drops a man and scatters wolves.
- **Leap-boosters.** Thirty seconds of rooftop flight per charge. When it runs out, you fall.
- **Tether-hook** and **quench-blade.** A grapple line, and a glass sabre that drinks heat: one touch numbs a limb.
- **Lantern rifle.** Crystal rounds, and a discharge log bound into every case.
- **Ghostlight.** A bootleg gauntlet, loud and unstable. Alice builds hers from Nathaniel's stolen schematic and his own offcuts, bought from the undercity fence he sells to.
- **The Black Gauntlet.** Nathaniel's first prototype, stolen two years ago. On Ajax's arm it hits like a steam hammer.
- **The Hairpin.** The gauntlet Nathaniel builds for Alice in Episode 4, narrow and fast, fitted strap by strap.
- **The duet (new).** Two cores tuned to the same pitch double each other when they fire on the same beat. The Guard drills it in pairs. It needs two people who can count together, which is why it becomes the show's love language.
- **The Tallow (new).** A spring-and-steam gauntlet with no crystal in it. Weak, loud and ugly. Nathaniel starts it the morning after he learns what crystal is, because from then on he can hear her in his own gauntlet every time he fires. On the last night it is the only one still working.

**The Royal Lantern Guard.** Chartered to protect the lamp grid, armed by the Works, loyal on paper to the Crown and in practice to Eldgard. Oath: *Light the dark. Keep the flame.* Ranks: Wick, Flame, Sergeant, Lantern-Captain.

**The Vykra.** A guild of undercity women, knives and eyes for hire, run from a Shadwell laundry. Their mark is a knot stitched inside the glove. Their poison, winter's kiss, kills with frost on the lips.

### The wound under the sisters

Unchanged, because it was right. When they were small their stepfather drank and kept a belt. Miriam, four years older, promised in the dark under the bed that nothing would touch Alice while she was there. Then the belt came out and Miriam did not move. She taught Alice to count the fence posts outside the window instead, one to eight, until it was over. The sisters have played the counting game as a joke for fifteen years and never said where it came from. Alice keeps all of it "behind the fence." Miriam carried it as guilt and never spoke it.

When the Lost One puts Miriam on like a coat, she gets the guilt with the memories. The cold burns the birthdays and the songs. It cannot get its teeth into the shame.

The abuse is never shown. It lives in a buckle, a floorboard, a flinch and a count.

**The irony the sisters' story turns on.** Miriam owed Alice an apology and could not give it. The Lost One is not Miriam, and gives it perfectly, and that is how Alice knows.

### The two comfortable lies

The season runs two lies in parallel and breaks them in the same hour.

- **Alice** tells herself the girl in her room is her sister, shocked and ill, and will come back.
- **Nathaniel** tells himself his best friend could not have fired the shot, and that the page missing from Nixon's rifle log is what Nixon says it is.

Both of them have the evidence by Episode 4. Both of them choose not to look, for the same reason: the person is all they have left. Episode 6 takes both.

## Act I — The promise

### Episode 1 — "The Engagement"

**2 NOVEMBER. NO SNOW.** One night, two parties: the Harding ball above, the glim run below, until the light flickers.

*Logline:* On the night his engagement is announced, Nathaniel Harding's brother dies at the family Works, his best friend fires the shot nobody can ever know about, and across the river a girl freezes on a bridge and opens her eyes in the morgue.

| Carries it | Want | Twist |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nathaniel | To show his father the leap-gauntlet | Laughed off in public; his brother is dead by morning |
| Alice | To pass her Vykra trial and find out who Miriam keeps sneaking off to | Finds her sister frozen, then sees her wake |
| Nixon | To deserve the captaincy | Shoots a masked man who turns out to be Jamie |
| Jamie | To open the chain and run with Miriam | No ring. The blade only cracks it |

**Cold open. Tyburn, 1489.** Frost, no snow. A gallows field, a pit lined with iron, something vast breathing in it. We see one hand of blue ice the size of a cartwheel with a hook through it, and hear a wordless lullaby. Alistair Harding, wolf-crest ring on his finger, opens her wrist into a lantern. *Fwhumph.* The first green light. His son Edric, nine, holds the lantern up, solemn and proud to be useful. Behind them a small girl made of frost runs from a cut net into the dark. Alistair lets her go. Then he puts a hand on his son's shoulder and turns him toward the stair.

**The beats**

1. **Under Gin Lane.** Alice and Miriam run glim through the lamp-mains. Overhead, children skip to the oldest rhyme in London: *"Green light, green light, who's in the cellar tonight?"* A buyer tilts his head: "Crying. Always crying with this batch." His belt buckle rings on a pipe and Alice is across the tunnel before she knows she has moved. Miriam sees it and looks away, then steals an orange and tosses it to her. It is Alice's twentieth birthday. Miriam does not say so. There is a ring on a cord under her collar.
2. **The gauntlet shop.** Nathaniel fires his leap prototype, *kshhh-THOOM*, and lands in a coal bin. Byron picks soot out of his hair: "Very graceful, sir. The coal agrees." Byron has been his only audience for years.
3. **The study.** Eldgard tells Jamie his "preparation" begins tomorrow. Jamie: "I know what the Tithe is." He leaves his drink, and leaves his pocket watch on the desk, wound.
4. **The Guard tower.** Nixon is made the youngest Lantern-Captain in the corps. Eldgard pins the lantern on him himself: "My family looks after its own." Cole Pruett salutes and hits himself in the eye. Sergeant Voss does not laugh, and makes a note.
5. **The laundry.** Mother Grise sets Alice's trial: get into the Harding ball and come out with something a Harding will miss.
6. **Blackwood Bridge stair.** Alice tails Miriam and watches her kiss Jamie Harding in a coach.
7. **The ballroom.** A thousand green chandeliers. Tamara and Nathaniel, engaged by their fathers, agree to be very polite to each other for forty years. Nixon, on the door, watches Tamara and looks away when she looks back. In the service corridor Nathaniel's gauntlet shorts, and a lamp-girl fixes it with a hairpin in four seconds, counting under her breath: "One post, two post, three, four." Up in the gallery, Josephine to Byron: "That girl holds her wrist like a knife-girl." "You'd know, madam."
8. **The humiliation.** Nathaniel steps up to show his father the gauntlet. Eldgard, smiling for the room: "Not tonight. Toys after supper." On his way out of the light Nathaniel passes Nixon: "He pinned your lantern himself. You're the son he actually wanted, Nix." Nixon laughs it off. Neither of them will ever find it funny again. Jamie slips out, and only Nathaniel sees him go.
9. **The core.** Jamie goes down with a copied Guard key. We see the prisoner only as moving green light. He drives a crystal blade into the lock and presses his cut palm to the iron. The lock groans half open.
10. **The dark.** Every chandelier dies, then London, street by street. In the black ballroom Nathaniel hears a woman crying inside the walls. One match flares: the lamp-girl. "Do you hear that?" She listens honestly. "Hear what, sir?" "Someone crying." "In this house? Half the room is. They've lost their light." He asks her name. "The girl who lights your wicks." "That isn't a name." "It's the only one your house pays for." The match burns to her fingers and she does not flinch. "Ask me again when the lights come back on." *Fff.* In the dark, at his ear: "Congratulations on your engagement, sir." Her hand is in his coat, and then it is not.
11. **The Works, set piece.** Alarm in the core. Nixon goes down the gantries ahead of his squad, Cole trailing. Flash-bursts cut the dark into stills: pipes, steam, a masked figure with his hand on the lock. In one frame, far up in the vault level, a big man with an apple, who is not supposed to be there either. "Step away!" The figure turns with something bright in his hand. *KRAK.* He falls against the lock and does not come off it.
12. **Blackwood Bridge.** Miriam waits in red mittens. Frost climbs the rail on a night with no frost anywhere else. A small girl made of frost is standing beside her. Miriam, teeth chattering, asks her something we cannot hear. The girl nods.
13. **The core.** Nixon pulls the mask. Jamie, shot through the shoulder, alive, his palm frozen to the iron. Eldgard steps out of the dark. "Go, Captain. Wait at the gate. Speak to no one." Nixon goes. Up on the gantry, forgotten, Cole watches the old man stand perfectly still while the boy on the lock says something to him.
14. **The ballroom.** The chandeliers come back, all at once. Applause. Nathaniel looks at the clock without knowing why: eight minutes to midnight.
15. **The bridge, then the gate.** Alice finds Miriam frozen solid, eyes open. Her scream. At the Works gate, Eldgard to Nixon: "Jamie fell. The saboteur escaped. You never saw his face. This family never forgets who keeps its secrets." The leash goes on.
16. **Dawn.** Nathaniel gets as far as the stables before he breaks. Nixon finds him and holds him, with Jamie's blood still under his fingernails.

**Tag. The morgue.** Inspector Felix Hawke logs a ring with a wolf crest, taken off a cord round the dead girl's neck, and puts it back. Alice touches her sister's hand and the frost runs off it. *Drip. Drip.* The eyes move. "Ali."

### Episode 2 — "The Wake"

**4 NOVEMBER. NO SNOW.** What came back?

*Logline:* Alice hides a sister who sat up on a slab and is not quite right, and decides it is a miracle. London buries an empty coffin. Nathaniel finds a Guard cartridge where his brother died and takes it to the one man he trusts.

| Carries it | Want | Twist |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Alice | To get Miriam well | She is hired to get close to the boy she already wants to be near |
| The Lost One | To get warm and find the crying | She tells her first lie, and it is a kindness |
| Nathaniel | To see his brother's body | Hands the case to the man who fired |
| Josephine | To know what her son knows | The eyes she buys are the ones he cannot stop looking at |

**Cold open. The morgue, a minute later.** The thing in Miriam's body sits up and screams, *eeeeiiiKKK*, a sound like a pane splitting. Two attendants drop where they stand. Felix's hand goes numb on his revolver. She runs barefoot into the night and Alice runs after her.

**The beats**

1. **The rooftops.** Alice follows frost from roof to roof and finds her curled inside a lamp tower, hands cupped round the crystal, drinking its warmth. Alice talks her down with the counting game, and the creature counts back, because the body knows it.
2. **Old Wren's.** Wren listens at that chest for a long time. She hears a heart that is barely there. Lower down, she hears another one that is perfectly fine. "She's had a shock, love. Ice does this. The memories come back." She says nothing about the second heart. Alice's face is begging her not to say anything at all.
3. **The parlour.** A closed coffin. Eldgard will not open it. Josephine takes Nathaniel's face in her gloved hands: "You're what's left. You will stay out of that place." Then, to her husband, when the room is empty: "What is in that box?" He does not answer her.
4. **The tenement.** Whimsy. The Lost One meets bread, a kettle (terror) and a cat (delight). She picks up the cat and it goes stiff in her arms. She does not understand death. Alice does, and makes rules: mittens on, no touching. Miriam's red mittens go on. "You'll be yourself again soon, Mim." A long look. "Yes, Ali." The first lie.
5. **St Bride's.** Nixon carries the coffin and it is wrong in his hands: too heavy, nothing shifting. On the chancel wall, among the Harding brasses: OTTOLINE HARDING, 1820–1839, DIED ABROAD. Nathaniel asks Byron who she was. "Your father's sister, sir." Under the hymn, Nathaniel hears the crying again, the same tune a half-step down.
6. **The graveside.** Alice is there because Miriam loved this man's brother. Nathaniel knows the lamp-girl. He gives her his handkerchief, the only thing he has done all day that nobody told him to. She keeps it.
7. **The laundry.** Josephine, veiled, across Grise's counting table. "The lamp-girl who lifted my son's pocket at my own ball. She's yours. I want her on him. Every door he opens, I hear about it." Grise names a price. Josephine doubles it. The audience now knows two things: Josephine was Vykra, and Alice has just been paid to do what she had already started.
8. **The core stair.** Nathaniel forces the outer gate, is turned back at the core door, and finds a spent Guard cartridge on the steps.
9. **The Guard roof.** "This is one of yours." "Half the corps was down there after." "Then find me the one who was down there before." Nixon takes the case. That night he cuts the page from his own rifle log and burns it. Cole, from the bunk-room door, watches, and copies it into his diary from memory.
10. **Jamie's bedroom.** Alice breaks in Vykra-quiet and finds Nathaniel already searching. Then the door handle turns, and the only place to go is the wardrobe, chest to chest among a dead man's coats. Through the slats, Eldgard sits on Jamie's bed and winds his son's watch. *Tk-tk-tk.* He does not say a word. Nathaniel is about to step out to him. Alice puts her hand over his mouth, and is not quick enough taking it away. In the boot-tree afterward: Jamie's drawing of a lock, with a ring-shaped socket in it, and two words. *Not his.* At the window she gives him one true thing: "Bounds." He asks for the rest. "Girls on Gin Lane don't give the rest to a Harding."
11. **Gin Lane.** Felix has two dead attendants, a missing corpse, and a wolf-crest ring he logged himself. He knocks. Alice is out. The Lost One opens the door, frightened, and frost blooms. His hand freezes to the frame, and she runs.
12. **The Works gate.** Drawn by the crying, she lays a mittened palm on the iron. Every lamp on Tyburn Road leans its flame toward her. Nixon, on night watch, sees it, and runs straight to Eldgard. Eldgard goes grey. "Alive, Captain. Unharmed. If a Lantern kills that girl, every lamp in London goes out with her. Do not ask me why."

**Tag. The core.** The chain jerks. For the first time we see her face: blue ice, green fire. "My daughter is here."

### Episode 3 — "Lanterns"

**9 NOVEMBER. NO SNOW.** Nixon's hunt.

*Logline:* Eldgard sets his captain on the Frost Killer and gives his son the job of arming the hunt. When Nixon has her in front of his rifle, he has to choose between his patron's order and a frozen boy.

| Carries it | Want | Twist |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nixon | To bring her in quietly and repay Eldgard | Saves Cole and lets her go. She touches him and nothing happens |
| Alice | To get her sister out of the net | Uses the boy she likes, and people die anyway |
| Nathaniel | To be the useful son at last | Finds the girl, and throws the chance away |
| Josephine | The truth | Gets it |

**Cold open. A charity school, ten years ago.** Boys in a line. Eldgard pricks each finger and touches the blood to a sliver of crystal. They stay dark. Thirteen-year-old Nixon's glows faintly green. Eldgard pats his head. "Still good." It plays as kindness.

**The beats**

1. **The shop.** Eldgard gives Nathaniel his first real job: re-equip the Guard for the hunt. Nathaniel glows. In the corridor, to Nixon: bring her in alive, tell no one, not the Yard and not my son.
2. **A pigeon loft.** The Lost One holds out an orange, peeled badly in one long ruined spiral. "It was your birthday. On the bridge day. I forgot." A piece of Miriam's regret, surfacing in hands that do not know why. Alice eats the whole thing, pith and all.
3. **The linen room.** Josephine and Byron. "Thirty years ago you caught me at that gate and let me go. I heard something behind it and I have never asked you what. I am asking." Byron has kept silent for half a century. He tells her: the woman, the lock, and what her firstborn was raised for. She does not cry. She asks one question. "And now that Jamie's gone?" "The second son inherits, madam. That's the custom. Unless there's nobody else."
4. **The shop.** Alice comes "to return the handkerchief" and lets him show off. He flies across the foundry into the coal bin again, for an audience of one. She reads the sweep roster upside down: Gin Lane, tonight. She feels sick at how easy it was, and sicker at how much she liked watching. She leaves with the handkerchief.
5. **The Guard roof.** Cole fires a booster early, sails off the roof and ends up hanging in someone's bloomers. Nixon laughs for the first time since the core, remembers, and stops. Voss sees both halves.
6. **The crystal market, set piece.** Alice moves her through the Christmas crowds, because crowds are cover. The Lost One sees a hundred lanterns and reaches out, enchanted. Every crystal in the market flares. Leapers launch, *kshhh-THOOM, kshhh-THOOM*. Alice runs the roofs on a ghostlight that coughs. Below, cornered, the Lost One screams, and every crystal bursts. A chestnut-seller freezes mid-shout. Three dead. That night the Yard posts its own order, which is not Eldgard's: shoot on sight.
7. **The bell tower.** Cole corners her alone. She reaches for him, *please*, and he freezes from the hand up. Nixon lands and she grabs his bare wrist to shove him back. No frost. She stares at her own hand, then at him. "You're one of them." Cole is going blue. Nixon can take her to Eldgard or carry the boy to a furnace. He takes the boy.
8. **Guy's Hospital.** Cole loses two fingers. "Did I do good, sir?" "You did good." Eldgard, in the corridor: "You had her and you let her go. I know what you did at the core, Captain. So do you."
9. **A lamp-tunnel under the Works.** Alice holds her with gloves on and says what you say: "You didn't mean it." It is the wrong sentence. It is what Miriam whispered under the bed. Something runs down the Lost One's face and freezes. *Tink.* "What is this?" "You're crying, Mim. You always cried for both of us." And the creature is full of a memory she never lived: a buckle, a small back, her own body not moving. "Ali. The belt. I should have—" Alice goes hard as iron. "We don't talk about him. That's behind the fence."
10. **The tunnel mouth.** Nathaniel is waiting. He found her the way an engineer would: her ghostlight whines at his pitch, and he walked the tunnels with a listening horn until he heard his own design. Alice tells him the truth as she believes it. The Lost One steps out and takes his bare hand on purpose. Nothing. All three stare. He could take the Frost Killer to his father tonight and be, at last, the useful son. He looks at the girl holding his hand, and at Alice. He does not. It is the first time he acts against his own lie, and it is a crime.

**Tag.** He turns to go. The Lost One, scenting the air: "You smell like the man who keeps my mother."

## Act II — The lies

### Episode 4 — "The Inquest"

**14 NOVEMBER. NO SNOW.** Who fired?

*Logline:* Nathaniel and Alice hang Lambrick's enforcer over the Thames and learn the shot came from the Guard. Nathaniel finds the page missing from his best friend's log and lets himself be lied to. Then the inspector who knows too much dies on a witness stand with frost on his lips.

| Carries it | Want | Twist |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nathaniel | Jamie's killer | Has the answer in his hands and chooses not to read it |
| Alice | To keep the Vykra and her sister both | Loses the Vykra |
| Nixon | To keep his friend | Lies to his face, then sells his secret to Eldgard to keep him safe |
| Josephine | Her son out of the dock | Kills a decent man in public |

**Cold open. The core gate, thirty years ago.** A young Lantern sergeant catches a Vykra girl with a knife, sent to kill young Eldgard. She was caught because she stopped: behind the gate a woman was weeping, and she listened a breath too long. He lets her go. A year later she marries the man she was sent to kill, and the sergeant serves at the wedding. In his pocket is a faded blue ribbon from another night entirely.

**The beats**

1. **The glasshouse.** Nathaniel hides them in the abandoned orangery on the estate. The Lost One makes snow for the first time, a flurry over her palm, and laughs like a child. Alice gives him her theory: "Something came back in her from the cold. But Mim's under it." He has felt that hand not freeze his. He does not argue. Later the Lost One tries again: "Ali. Under the bed—" Alice hands her an orange. "Eat that."
2. **The Serpentine.** Tamara takes her fiancé skating, as expected of them. He is terrible. She passes him a page copied from her father's cipher ledger: *2 Nov, Works vault, 200 gns, A.F.* Ajax Fink was inside the Works the night Jamie died.
3. **The workbench.** Nathaniel builds Alice a real gauntlet and fits it strap by strap. "Hold still, Bounds." She does, which she has never done for anyone. A love scene with no kissing in it. Late, with Alice asleep, the Lost One gives him something in Miriam's voice: "He talked about you. He said you'd fly before you learned to walk, and you were the only one of them worth saving." Nobody has ever told him he was worth something without wanting something back.
4. **The laundry.** Grise wants the frost girl; the Yard's bounty is large. Alice: "No." Grise cuts the knot out of her glove with one snip. "Then you're no one's." Under the awning, in the rain, Alice folds his handkerchief into the glove where the knot was.
5. **The coal docks, set piece.** They ambush Ajax among the barges. The Black Gauntlet, *WHUNK*, and Nathaniel recognises his own stolen prototype. He is losing. Then Alice's count carries across the dark, "three, four," and their two gauntlets fire on the same beat. The burst doubles. Neither of them knew it could. They hook Ajax to a crane and swing him over the river.
6. **The crane.** Ajax, upside down, laughing. "I was in your father's vault, not his cellar. I was paid for drawings. But I had a fine view. A Lantern put a round in your brother from the gantry, and your father sent that Lantern away and stayed. Ask the Guard who fired." Alice: "Pull him up. He's the only witness we have." Nathaniel's hand is on the lever. "He watched," he says, and lets go. *Splash.*
7. **The shop, 3 a.m.** The Guard's rifles are racked on his own bench for refit, each with its log bound in the case. He reads every one. In the captain's, a stub where a page was cut.
8. **The Guard roof.** "There's a page gone from yours." Nixon has had ten days to prepare this. "A misfire on the range, my first week. I didn't want it on the record." It is thin, and Nathaniel knows it is thin. He looks at the only friend he has. "All right, Nix." He chooses. That night Nixon follows him home out of plain fear for him, sees the lamp in the glasshouse, sees who is in it, and takes it to Eldgard. He tells himself it is to keep Nathaniel alive.
9. **The glasshouse.** Felix has followed the wolf-crest ring to this door. The Lost One offers him an orange and apologises for his hand. He sees a frightened girl. "Somebody made her, and it's somebody in that house. I'm at the market inquest tomorrow. I'll ask the coroner for a warrant on this estate." Under the garden wall, Josephine hears every word. A warrant finds the girl in her son's keeping, and five people are dead.
10. **The inquest, set piece.** An upstairs room over a public house, packed. A veiled woman brings the witness a glass of water. Felix stands. "The deaths at the market were caused by a girl who should be dead, and I can tell this court where—" Frost crawls over his lips. His eyes go white. "The Frost Killer is here!" The room breaks for the stairs. Nixon's Lanterns fire flash into the ceiling to stop the crush.
11. **The glasshouse.** Alice hears how he died, looks at the girl who has been beside her all day, and knows the poison. "Winter's kiss. That's Vykra." Somebody is killing in her sister's name.

**Tag. Josephine's dressing room.** She peels off gloves with a knot stitched inside and washes frost-dust from her fingers. The water skins over with ice. In the mirror, Byron is standing in the doorway.

### Episode 5 — "Lantern Night"

**18 NOVEMBER. NO SNOW.** The midseason turn.

*Logline:* On the night London celebrates its light, Nathaniel breaks into his own family's Works. He finds a chained mother bleeding green, learns there have always been three roads and his family has only ever taken one, and hears the door lock behind him on his best friend's order.

| Carries it | Want | Twist |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nathaniel | The truth about the core | Gets all of it, and stays with his father anyway |
| Alice | To bring him back up | Leaves without him |
| The Lost One | To free her mother tonight | Cannot touch the iron. Needs a Harding |
| Nixon | To obey the man who made him | Warns his friend, then seals him in |
| Tamara | To prove her mind is worth something | Opens a door nobody else could, twice |

**Cold open. Harding House, 1839.** A boy of eight in a nightshirt at the top of a stair. Below him his sister Otty, nineteen, in a white dress, is walking down it and singing. A page of fourteen holds her hair ribbon and is trying not to cry. Their father's hand comes down on the boy's shoulder. "Look, Eldgard. That is what a Harding is for." The boy looks. He never stops looking.

**The beats**

1. **The Upper Gallery.** Eldgard brings his son into "the work": a cathedral of glass vats where the crystal is "grown from Tyburn iron." Nathaniel's joy is real and hard to watch.
2. **The glasshouse.** "There's nothing down there but vats." The Lost One: "He showed you a nice room. My mother isn't in a nice room." "He's my father, Bounds." "She's my sister, Harding." Their first fight, and it is about family.
3. **The orangery door.** Josephine comes to the glasshouse herself, alone, and Alice learns who has been paying her. "I know what you are because I was it. He will go down to that cellar; I can't stop him and neither can you. So you'll go with him, and you'll bring him back up. That is all I'm buying now." She looks at the frost girl asleep under the orange trees. "And after tonight that thing is off my land."
4. **The silver pantry.** Byron gives Nathaniel his old Guard key on a black ribbon. Jamie borrowed it in October and returned it with a note Byron has carried ever since: *If I don't come up, give it to Nat.* "I stood on that gate eleven years, sir. I heard her every night. I stopped hearing it when I stopped letting myself." He lights himself downstairs with a tallow candle. He has not carried a green lamp since.
5. **The Guard tower.** Eldgard to Nixon: "My son will come tonight, and he'll bring her. Let them through. Seal the core behind them." "And Nathaniel?" "He'll be with me, Captain."
6. **The plan.** A model of the Works in teacups and oranges; the Lost One eats the core. Tamara arrives with the lock ciphers, which her father has been stealing for two years and she has been reading over his shoulder. The fiancée and the lamp-girl meet. Every line is polite and every line is a knife. Nathaniel is the only one who does not notice.
7. **The outfall.** Fireworks over a blazing green city. The pipe is full of black water. The Lost One freezes it into a glass road and they walk inside the river.
8. **The gantries.** Nixon, on the high walk, gives the whistle they invented when Nathaniel was nine: *turn back.* Nathaniel hears it, smiles because it is Nix, and keeps going.
9. **The shaft.** The inner door resets mid-code. Tamara recodes it by ear with Alice holding her by the belt over a four-hundred-foot drop. Neither says thank you.
10. **The core.** We see her whole: nine feet of ice and green fire, hooks through shoulders and hips, her blood running in channels out into London. She is singing, and it is the Lantern Hymn with none of the words. "Little frost. You came." The Lost One grabs the chain and it burns her mittens to smoke. Morgana cries out, and across the city every lamp flares white.
11. **The mother.** To Alice: "You have my daughter's hand. Give it back." "She's my sister. Whatever came back in her, my sister's under it." Morgana looks at her with something like pity and does not correct her. Neither does the Lost One. To her child: "I bought you free. Do not spend it here. Go." To Nathaniel: "You came down these stairs once, little Harding. And your brother is not dead. He is cold."
12. **The three roads.** Eldgard steps out; he has been waiting. He gives him the Tithe. Nathaniel: "And if nobody pays?" "The chain breaks and the White keeps this city." Morgana, over him, to the boy: "There was a third thing your fathers never chose. The lock opens to what closed it. The wolf's ring, and your blood, given." Eldgard does not deny it. "And four million people wake up in December with no fire. I have done that arithmetic every night for forty years." "Jamie was to pay." "Yes." "So now it's me." "It will not be you. I swear it." "Then who?" Eldgard does not answer, and Nathaniel files the silence away.
13. **The door.** It seals with a hiss. Behind the glass is Nixon, lips moving on the oath, over and over, loud enough in his own head to drown out everything else. "Nix!" Nixon does not look away, and that is worse.
14. **The burst.** The Lost One, desperate, throws herself at the chain again. Morgana screams. Her blood flares through every channel at once, and on Lantern Night every lamp in London blazes white for one heartbeat and bursts, *pop-pop-pop-pop*, street after street. The chain itself is tearing. Eldgard, at the speaking tube, breaks his own trap: "Open it! Get that girl away from her!" Nixon throws the bolt. Alice reaches for Nathaniel. "I need to hear the rest." She leaves without him, which is the one thing she was paid not to do.
15. **Westminster Bridge.** Glass rains on the festival crowd. A crush. Cole's squad holds a barricade and people still die against the railings. Lambrick, on a coal cart: "Remember who kept you warm!"
16. **The core, after.** "She said Jamie isn't dead." Eldgard does not look at her. "She told me my sister was still singing." Then: "I won't lose two sons. Help me find another way." Nathaniel cannot tell what is a lie. He says yes.
17. **The gate, dawn.** Nixon is waiting. "You locked me in." "He's your father. He said you'd be safe with him." "Did you know what was down there?" "No." It is true, and it is not enough. Nathaniel walks past him.

**Tag. A Gin Lane roof, the city lit in patches.** The Lost One, hands burned: "I can't touch it. He can. Your Harding can open it." A beat. "That's why I let him find us." Alice takes the burned hand anyway.

### Episode 6 — "The Girl Who Isn't"

**28 NOVEMBER. NO SNOW.** Two lies break in one night.

*Logline:* Alice finds the letter Miriam never left her and learns what she has been protecting. Nixon tells Nathaniel who fired. Then the White comes through the crack, and the only way through the night is beside the person you just lost.

| Carries it | Want | Twist |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Alice | Her sister back | Gets the apology she always wanted, and that is the proof |
| The Lost One | To be let in | Is thrown out, and then chosen |
| Nixon | To put the lie down | Confesses, loses his friend, and is handed a father |
| Nathaniel | Another way | Loses Nixon and Byron in six hours |

**Cold open. Gin Lane, 2 November, morning.** Miriam's last day. Radiant, sick in a basin, a hand on her belly. Alice asleep across the bed. Miriam starts a letter: *Ali, about him, when we were small—* She scores it out until the paper tears. She writes three quick lines instead, loses her nerve, and sews it into her coat lining. Then she pulls on the red mittens.

**The beats**

1. **The cold city.** Half the lamps are stumps. Coal carts with LAMBRICK on the side, prices chalked up and up. Every night now there is frost on Blackwood Bridge and nowhere else, and this morning a lamplighter found prints in it that were not a dog's.
2. **The bench.** Father and son, finishing each other's equations, designing a lock that needs no blood. It is everything Nathaniel ever wanted. At the window, Alice has climbed the wall to tell the only person who would understand that her sister forgot her birthday. She sees his father's hand on his shoulder. She does not knock. Her breath fogs the pane; she wipes it clean with the glove that has his handkerchief in it, and goes. Byron, clearing cups afterward, quietly: "He's built that lock before, sir. In 'sixty-four, the winter Master Jamie was born. It doesn't work. He's keeping you at the bench."
3. **The canal at Little Venice.** They meet under a bridge, breath smoking. He tells her what the mother said: he can open it. She tells him what Gin Lane looks like without a fire. A glove comes off and his handkerchief falls into the frost. "You kept it." "Bounds keeps things." For once he does not wait to be reached for. *Tik. Tik.* Ice ticking off the bridge above them.
4. **The borrowed room.** Alice mends the coat and the needle catches paper. *I'm leaving with him. I'm sorry, Ali. Don't follow me.* The Lost One comes in with an armful of oranges, chattering. She has not used Alice's name in a week. Alice says nothing all evening, and we watch her not say it.
5. **The same room, one candle. The peak of the sisters' story.** "You were leaving." "Yes." And the fence comes down, because Alice is too angry to hold it up: "You always left. You left me under that bed. You promised, and then you counted fence posts while he—" Fifteen years, said out loud to the only person who was there.

    The Lost One kneels, the way you would kneel to a child under a bed. "I should have stood in front of you. Every time. I was so frightened of him that I let you be frightened alone, and I've hated myself every day since." Then the thing Miriam could never have said: "If he came through that door now, I'd stand in front of you. I'd let him freeze."

    It is the apology Alice has wanted her whole life, and it is perfect. That is how she knows. Miriam flinched. "Say my name." The mouth opens, and there is nothing there. "You're not her."

    "No." Alice asks the question she has been walking round for four weeks. "Did you kill her?" "The cold that came through with me did. I came where the blood was, and she was standing there. I didn't choose it. I didn't stop it. I needed a door." "Is she in there?" "No. She was gone before I opened her eyes." Then, gently, as a gift: "I love you. I don't know whose that is. I think it's mine."

    Alice looks at the thing that remembered her birthday and peeled her an orange and gave her the only apology she has ever been given, and understands that her real sister never loved her this well. "Get out."
6. **The Guard roof, afternoon.** Cole, two fingers short, holds out his diary. "Today, sir. Or I do it." So Nixon does. "I fired the shot. I didn't know it was him. Your father sent me to the gate. Jamie was alive when I left." Nathaniel hits him once. Nixon lets him. "The page." "Yes." "I asked you, and you looked at me." "Yes." Fifteen years end on a roof.
7. **Blackwood Bridge.** The Lost One, thrown out, goes to the only place she knows. Tonight the crack is wider. The fog splits like a pane, and wolves of the White step out of nothing, white-furred, blue-eyed, and lower their heads to her. Then they run past her into the city.
8. **Whitechapel, set piece.** Shot the way the pilot's raid was: black, a strobe frame, black. Every flash shows a wolf somewhere new and closer. Lamps freeze and burst, *tink, tink*. Inside the Pavilion music hall a comic singer keeps a hundred people roaring "The Man on the Flying Trapeze" to drown out the scratching at the doors. A panel bows inward. A child stops singing, and her mother starts her again.
9. **The stage door.** Nathaniel comes in off one roof and Nixon off the other. No words. Nixon counts the Guard's pair-drill aloud because it is the only thing they have left to say to each other, "three, four," and their gauntlets fire together, doubled, the way they did at nine and eleven. Perfectly in time, four hours after the friendship ended.
10. **Commercial Road.** A wolf takes Nathaniel off his feet. A green flash, then another: Byron, sixty-four, with his old sergeant's Lantern down from its case on the wall, hearing her again as clearly as he did on the gate. He puts himself between the boy and the animal. Dying: "Your mother killed the inspector. She does it for you. Don't let her." A breath. "You were always enough." In his fist is a blue ribbon.
11. **The street of wolves.** The Lost One walks into it and says one word in a language of wind. They stop, all but the biggest. She kills it with cold, and pays. Then she walks the rest back through the fog to the bridge, and they go back through the crack the way they came. A hundred people watch. By morning she is the Frost Witch. Alice reaches her through the emptied street. The girl looks at her politely, like a stranger. "I'm Alice. I'm your sister." "Who am I?" Alice has no answer she is allowed to give. "I don't know yet. You get to pick."

**Tag. Eldgard's study.** Nixon has come to hand in his lantern and be charged. Eldgard does not take it. He opens a drawer and puts Jamie's cufflinks on the desk between them. "Your mother was a lamp-girl at the Works called Marian Hargrave. I have watched you since the day you were born. You took a son from this house, Captain." He slides the cufflinks across. "You are also the only other one it has." For twenty-three years Nixon has wanted three words from this man. He gets two. "My son."

## Act III — The snow

### Episode 7 — "The Wedding"

**7 DECEMBER. NO SNOW.** One morning, one chapel, and the girl nobody is watching.

*Logline:* Nathaniel marries Tamara for the coal that will keep London alive when he puts out the light, and Alice watches from the gallery. While the vows run, the Vykra come for her sister. Nixon refuses an order at the gates. That night a coal baron repeats the oldest crime in London, and it snows.

| Carries it | Want | Twist |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nathaniel | A London that survives what he means to do | Married, and the cost is Alice's sister |
| Alice | To see him through it | Nods him through the vows while they take her |
| Tamara | Her own name on the paper | Gets it, and marries the wrong brother to do it |
| Nixon | To be a Harding | Will die for the house. Will not kill for it |
| Josephine | The frost girl gone and her son safe | Learns there is a spare, and that she has been outbid |

**Cold open. The core, sixteen years ago.** Nathaniel, five, follows a lullaby down a forbidden stair and finds a huge, gentle woman of ice who asks his name and sings it back to him. Eldgard snatches him up and swings a lantern slowly before his eyes. "You had a dream. You never heard anything." The boy, glassy: "I had a dream." It is why, of everyone in that house, he is the one who hears her.

**The beats**

1. **The music room.** Nathaniel, in mourning for a butler. Josephine at the piano. "He told me about Inspector Hawke." The music stops. "Then you know I love you."
2. **A Gin Lane roof.** The Lost One asks to be taught the counting game again. For the first time Alice tells her where it came from: eight posts and he's done. The Lost One counts with her, and at eight keeps going. "Nine." "There's no nine." "There is now. Nine is the one where we get up."
3. **Wren's attic.** Tamara lays a marriage settlement on a tea chest. She drafted it herself. On the wedding, every Lambrick depot inside the city passes into a trust in the bride's name; her father thinks he is buying a seat at the Works and has not read past page four. "Open your lock in December and Gin Lane is dead by Twelfth Night. Marry me first, and I open every depot the hour the lights go out." Nathaniel looks at Alice. "Marry her." "Bounds—" "I've seen a child frozen on my own doorstep. Marry her."
4. **The same attic, the night before.** Nobody has mentioned the morning. The Lost One, who has never seen a wedding, insists on practising one, and reads the service solemnly from a prayer book held upside down, with Alice standing in for the bride. They say every word to each other, slowly, and both of them mean it and both of them know. "You may now kiss the—" Alice: "That part isn't ours."
5. **Eldgard's study, midnight.** Josephine has a knife at her husband's throat and thirty years of practice behind it. "You'll walk down that stair yourself." He does not move. "A forced hand feeds nothing. Do you think I've never wished it could?" Then he tells her about the lamp-girl's son. She takes the knife away slowly. She is not angry about the woman. She is doing the arithmetic, and it comes out the way his did. "Does the boy know what he's agreed to?" "He thinks it's a gift."
6. **The groom's dressing room.** Josephine straightens his tie and hands him Grise's receipt, in a clerk's hand: *The second Harding son. Agent: Alice Bounds.* It is the first time he has ever seen her first name, and it is on a bill. "Whatever she's felt, I bought it." She wants his eyes on the altar and the lamp-girl's eyes on him. Grise already knows the hour.
7. **Wren's stair.** "Were you paid?" "At first." She starts to say the rest and he is already gone. The Lost One, from the attic door: "Go. Watch him. If you don't you'll hate yourself, and then you'll hate me." "Mittens on. Door barred." "Yes, Alice." She has the name back. She has had to learn it twice.
8. **The vestry.** Tamara, in white, finds Nixon standing over a ring box with Jamie's cufflinks at his wrists. She shuts the door. "Say one word and I'll walk out of this church with you." He tells her what he is and what he has promised to do when it snows. "I can't give you something that's already spoken for." She opens the box, closes it, and puts it in his hand. "Then hand it to me properly."
9. **The chapel.** Nixon steps up behind the groom he has not spoken to since the roof. Nathaniel, not turning: "Stand where I can see you." At the back of the gallery, where a lamp-girl would stand, is Alice in a borrowed hat. In her pew, Josephine turns, finds her, and smiles at her for the first time ever.
10. **The attic.** Grise's girls come over the roofs; Grise taught Alice every bolt-hole she has. Wren gets in the way and is put on the floor. The Lost One has her mittens off and the cold standing up in her like a held breath. She could kill all four. She has one thing of her own left, a name she learned twice, and the cold always takes the newest. She closes her hands and lets them take her.
11. **The vows.** Nathaniel looks up and finds Alice at the rail. She nods once, small, like a permission. "I will." He says it the way he said it in the attic, and at the rail her lips move with his. Nixon hands over the ring, properly. "Congratulations, miss." "Thank you, Captain." Lambrick signs the settlement in the porch without reading it, and leaves before the breakfast.
12. **The gates, set piece.** A crowd: candles for the Frost Witch who stopped the wolves, and coal-men with clubs who want her burned, and under both of them people who are simply cold. Stones. Eldgard, on the steps: "Captain. Clear them." "They're Londoners, sir." "Fire." Nixon looks at the man who called him son nine days ago. "Lanterns, stand down." Eldgard takes the lantern off his collar himself. "Keep the flame, Captain." "I am." Forty Lanterns pull off their own and drop them in the dry gutter. Sergeant Voss does not. She holds what is left of the line, and does not fire either. Eldgard, low, as Nixon passes: "You'll still come when it snows." "I gave you my word. I didn't give you my men."
13. **The old nursery.** The newlyweds and a wedding breakfast nobody touches. "We said forty years of politeness." "We managed four minutes." She tells him what Nixon told her in the vestry: whose son he is, and what he means to do. Nathaniel sits with the fact that he has two brothers, and that one killed the other, and that his father has spent a month keeping him at a bench.
14. **The attic.** Alice finds the door off its hinges, Wren bleeding against the wall, and two red mittens on the floor. Her knees go. She was watching a wedding.
15. **The laundry.** Josephine paid for a passage on the morning tide, alive, to anywhere. Grise counts a second purse onto the table, twice the size. "You taught me this yourself, Josie. Everything's for sale."

**Tag. Pimlico, Lambrick's retort house.** The girl in cold-iron shackles that smoke where they touch her. Lambrick has thought about this since Ajax told him what he saw glowing in a cellar. He opens her wrist over a lamp. *Fwhumph.* Green. He laughs like a man who has just been proved right about God. Under Tyburn, Morgana lifts her head. Four hundred years ago she agreed to stay so that this would never be done to her child again. The chain screams. Over London, out of a clear sky, it begins to snow. Card: **SNOW.**

### Episode 8 — "The Snow Confession"

**SNOW.** Dusk to eleven.

*Logline:* A mother is tearing her own chain apart and there are hours left. To get the girl back, Eldgard has to fight beside the son he lied to and the son he asked to die, and Alice has to take an army from the woman who sold her sister. At Pimlico, Eldgard takes a blow meant for Nathaniel and spends his last breath on everything he did.

| Carries it | Want | Twist |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Alice | An army | Wins it on a count her sister invented |
| Nathaniel | The girl, the ring, his brother | Gets the first two |
| Eldgard | The chain whole until midnight | Tells the truth instead |
| Tamara | Her father out of that building | He cannot put it down |
| Nixon | To keep his word | Slips away to keep it |

**Cold open. The Serpentine, twenty years ago.** Eldgard teaches five-year-old Jamie to skate, both of them laughing as the boy falls and falls. Then, kneeling on the ice: "One day the city will need you to be very brave." "I'll be brave, Papa." Eldgard's face as he hears it. On the bank, Josephine holds the scarf and thinks she is watching a father play with his son.

**The beats**

1. **The core.** Links are snapping, *tnk, tnk*, one every few minutes. Eldgard counts them alone. He knows what it means before the runner tells him where the girl is. He takes down his own gauntlet for the first time in twenty years.
2. **The glasshouse, snow coming through the broken panes.** Nathaniel says it first. "You nodded, and I said it, and while we were saying it they took her." "She sent me up there." It makes him cruel for exactly one line: "My mother's receipt had your name on it. That's where I learned it. On a bill." She does not flinch, which is how he knows it went in. Then she gives him the rest of the sentence from the stair. "At first I was paid. Then you flew across a foundry into a coal bin for an audience of one, and looked at me like I was the first person who'd ever watched. After that I'd have done it for nothing. I did do it for nothing. Grise cut me loose three weeks ago and your mother's money stopped the night I left you in that cellar. I kept coming." Snow ticks on the glass. "I'm married." "I know. I nodded." Eldgard is standing in the doorway with snow on his shoulders. "Lambrick has her in irons at Pimlico. If she's still in them at midnight there will be no lock left to argue over. I need her free as much as you do."
3. **Wren's door.** Wren, bandaged. "You knew. The first night." "I knew you couldn't hear it yet. Five weeks you had a sister. I'd give them to you again." "What else did you hear?" Wren tells her about the second heart, the one that never slowed. Alice has no time to decide what that means. Wren touches the arm she set when Alice was nine: "Nobody stood in front of you then, either. Go on."
4. **The laundry.** "I sold her because she's worth more than you." Alice calls a challenge by Vykra law. Knives among steaming sheets, *fwip, fwip*, shadows through linen. Grise fights on eight; every Vykra does. Alice counts one post, two post, through eight, and moves on nine. She spares the old woman. The girls pick up their knives. "Where?" "Pimlico."
5. **A bell foundry, Whitechapel.** Forty disgraced Lanterns cooking sausages on gauntlets set low. Nathaniel walks in. "You shot my brother." "Yes." "You're my brother." A long beat. "Yes." "Then you're family. God help you." Nixon says nothing about what he has promised. Cole, watching him, knows anyway.
6. **Outside Pimlico.** The whole alliance in the snow for the first and only time: Alice's Vykra, Nixon's forty, Voss's loyal company behind Eldgard, Tamara with a plan of her father's works drawn from memory. Cole asks if married ladies are allowed to fight. She hands him a knife. Voss and Nixon look at each other across the line. "Sergeant." "Captain." It is not forgiveness. It will do until morning.
7. **The yard, set piece.** Coal-men at the walls, Lanterns over them, Vykra up the drains, and then the wolves shaping themselves out of the drifts and going for everyone. Flash-bursts turn the snow green, and Nathaniel can hear her in every one. He fires anyway.
8. **The retort house.** Six lamps burning on the girl's blood, and she is barely standing. Alice gets to the shackles. Cold iron does nothing to a Gin Lane girl; she has the pins out in four seconds, counting. Tamara, to her father, across the room: "Come out. It's over." He has an armful of the lamps. "This is for you. All of it. It was always for you." "Then leave it." He looks at the green in his arms, and at her. He cannot put it down. He never could. She walks out. Behind her, one of the lamps in his arms goes over into an open gas main.
9. **The yard.** *WHOOOM.* Fire along the river. Ajax comes out of it with his coat alight and the Black Gauntlet up, and drives Nathaniel into the ground. "You should've pulled me up, Harding." The fist rises, and Eldgard steps under it. His ribs go. Nixon is there a second late: quench-blade into the gauntlet's core, the piston freezes, and the second cut is for Ajax. He dies mid-laugh. His apple rolls away through the snow.
10. **The Snow Confession.** Nathaniel has his father across his knees. Nixon kneels at the other side. Alice sits behind Nathaniel with her forehead between his shoulder blades and her sister breathing against her arm.

    "What did you do after Nixon left?" "Nothing." A breath. "The round went through his shoulder. He'd have lived. His palm was on the iron and the lock had begun. I could have pulled him off. If I had, the chain would have gone that night. I told myself I was choosing. I wasn't. I stood there. He looked at me the whole time, and I stood there, and when the lights came back on I knew to the second why."

    "The coffin." "Slag. The lock leaves nothing. It left nothing of Otty. I was eight. My father made me watch from the top of the stair so I'd know what we were for, and I have been afraid of that stair every day since. I raised a boy to walk down it so I would never have to. And when he wouldn't—" He turns his head. "I went looking for another one. I told you that you owed this house a son, and I called you mine in the same breath, because I knew what you'd pay for the word. It was true. That's the worst of it. It was true and I used it."

    To Nathaniel: "I never looked at you. You thought it was because you were the spare. You were the one I got to keep. I couldn't look at you without seeing what the other one cost."

    He works the wolf ring off his finger. "It opens with what closed it. I have known how to end this for forty years and I liked being warm. Don't be me." Nathaniel's free hand goes behind him without looking and finds Alice's.

    At the edge of the firelight, Josephine has been standing in the snow with thirty-year-old knives on, for long enough to hear *I stood there.* She does not come closer. He sees her, and that is the last thing he sees. Snow settles on his open eyes and does not melt.

**Tag.** Nathaniel looks up. The place where Nixon was kneeling is empty. So is the place where his mother stood. Cole, bleeding, holding out a pair of cufflinks: "He said to give you these back, sir. He said he's sorry about your brother. Both of them." Across the river a bell starts counting. Eleven.

### Episode 9 — "The Tithe"

**SNOW.** Eleven to dawn.

*Logline:* One brother has gone to pay, and their mother has gone to make sure he does. Nathaniel does what no Harding has done in four hundred years, and it takes an undercity girl's hand to do it. Then London has to live through the night.

| Carries it | Want | Twist |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nathaniel | Nobody on that lock again | Becomes the man who put out London |
| Alice | Her sister | Lets her go, and so keeps her |
| The Lost One | Her mother free, and a sister | Gets both, and pays with the White in her |
| Nixon | To mean something | Refused as a payment. Chosen as a captain |
| Josephine | One son alive | Leaves him, for the other |

**Cold open. The pit, 1489, an hour before the pilot.** Alistair at the new chain, the child in his net. Morgana, fresh in her hooks: "Free me, and pay in light. Keep me, and pay in sons. Either way my child goes free tonight, and stays free, or there is no bargain." He slides the wolf ring onto his finger. "Sons." He cuts the net. In the doorway behind him a boy of nine is waiting with an unlit lantern, hoping to be given something to do.

**The beats**

1. **Tyburn Road.** They run it through wolves. Cole plants himself at a barricade of overturned trams with the last full cartridge belt in the company. "Go on, sir. I've got the road."
2. **The core stair.** Josephine is on it with her knives out. "He chose it. Let him have it." "He chose it because Father sold it to him." "I don't care why." Alice goes at her, Vykra against Vykra, the girl and the woman who bought her. Josephine is better. She has Alice on the steps with a blade at her eye and says, quite reasonably: "Jamie touched that lock and it ate him. You will not take my last one down there." Nathaniel holds up the ring. "Jamie had a blade. I have this." "You have your father's word for that." It is a fair point, and nobody in the room can answer it.
3. **The lock.** Nixon is standing at it with his sleeve rolled. Tamara is between him and the iron and he will not lay hands on her to move her. "Let me be something on purpose." Nathaniel: "He told you that you owed him a son. You don't pay that by giving him what he wanted." Nixon wavers, and it is the prisoner who finishes it. Morgana, hoarse, to the boy with his hand out: "A girl sang on that stair fifty winters ago. I did not want her. I have never wanted one of you. It is the iron that is hungry, and the men who made it." Nixon looks at his own hand. He has been offering his life to a lock.
4. **The ring.** Four hundred years of frost have sealed the socket. The ring will not seat. Nathaniel's hands slip on it. Alice is beside him before he asks, and the Hairpin is already whining up past where he told her never to run it, *eeeeeee*. "If this works, every light in London goes out. They'll hate me for a hundred years." "Us," she says, and puts the core against the iron. The heat comes back through the plate into her palm and she holds it there, the smell of her own glove burning, until the ice lets go. He cuts his hand, seats the ring, and lays his palm on the lock. It begins, the way it began with Jamie. Josephine makes a sound. Alice puts her burned hand flat over his and keeps it there. The lock turns. Above them a bell strikes twelve.
5. **London.** The chain falls. The green dies street by street, the same wave as the pilot, and this time nothing comes back. A tram stops in the snow. A hospital ward goes black. A mother on Gin Lane lights a tallow candle with shaking hands. Under the Works, Nathaniel listens, and for the first time since he was five nothing is crying in the walls.
6. **The core.** Morgana rises as her wounds close. She says his name the way she sang it once. "Nathaniel. A Harding opened it." She does not thank him. Behind her the White stands open like a door, and what is in it comes out. She goes up through the roof into the storm. The door will shut at dawn. Until then it is winter.
7. **The night.** Tamara opens every Lambrick depot on her own signature, and there is a fire on every corner by two. The Vykra hold Gin Lane inside a ring of burning coal, with Alice at the gap and her sister wrapped in coats behind her, getting colder. On Westminster Bridge the crystal charges fade shot by shot, each flash dimmer than the last, the last of her going out of the world. By four the Guard's gauntlets are dead weight. Nathaniel straps on the Tallow, the ugly spring-wound thing with nothing stolen in it. It is weak and it is loud and it works. Nixon counts. They fire together. Voss brings her company onto the bridge beside the mutineers and takes the left of the line without a word. Josephine fights at her son's back until the hour before dawn, and then she is not there.
8. **Tyburn Road.** Cole holds it to his last cartridge, seventeen and two fingers short. A girl in a Vykra glove comes up the road carrying another girl who woke by the fire and asked for her mother. He knows them. He does not ask. He holds the road until they are past. Nixon finds him at first grey light, sitting against a tram as if resting, diary open in his lap. The last line, in pencil: *Held the road. Nobody past who shouldn't be.*
9. **Tyburn hill.** Morgana over London, the wolves in a ring, four hundred years of grievance and an hour in which to spend it. Alice comes up the hill carrying her sister. Nathaniel comes up the other side after his mother. They see each other across the snow, alive, and there is no time for any of it.
10. **In front.** The Lost One sets herself down between her mother and the city, in front of Alice, in the place Miriam never once stood. Alice sees it and her breath goes. The girl sings the hymn back in the old tongue, the way it was before it had words. The wolves stop circling. "They bled me for four hundred years." "One of them let you go."
11. **Letting go.** "Then come home, little frost." The girl looks at Alice. Alice, who has spent the season holding on: "Go, if you want to. I'll be all right." She means it. She has done this once before, with a nod, in a chapel.
12. **The last thing.** "I've spent everything else of hers. I kept this. On the bridge she was still breathing. She asked me for something. 'Go back to Ali. Stand in front of her. I never did.' I said yes. Then I wanted to." Miriam's last thought was her sister, and her last act was to find someone braver to keep her promise. Alice laughs and cries at once. The girl stays. Morgana, who tore her own chain apart for this child, lets her stay, which is harder. She lays a hand flat on her daughter's chest and draws the White out of her like a splinter. What is left is a girl, shivering for the first time.
13. **The door.** It is closing with the dawn. Nathaniel is looking through it. Somewhere in there is a man standing very still, and he has the ring that opens things, and he has been told since he was a child that being useful is the same as being loved. He takes a step. Alice is in front of him with her fist in his coat, in the place nobody ever stood for her. Her first word to him since the lock: "Don't."
14. **Josephine.** She steps past them both. To Morgana: "My son is in there." "He is kept. He will not know you." "I'll know him." Nathaniel catches her wrist. She has controlled every door in his life, and now she asks him for something, the only time all season: "They'll say I died of fever in Switzerland." "No. They'll say your name." His hand opens. "Go." She kisses his forehead. To Alice: "I paid for a fortnight. You stayed for the rest. Keep him out of the Works." "It's the only thing you ever bought that worked." Josephine almost laughs, and that is how she goes. The wolves follow. The door shuts. The snow stops in the middle of falling.
15. **Dawn.** Nathaniel is on his knees where his mother was. Alice does not touch him. She kneels where he can see her until the sound he is making passes. Then she takes the handkerchief out of her glove and tears it in two. *Rrrip.* Half round the palm he cut. He takes the other half and binds the hand she burned. Neither of them is any good at it. She has nothing to say to a man who has just said "Go" to his mother, so she gives him the most private thing she owns. "One post. Two post." He knows it; he heard it in a corridor five weeks ago and never asked. At eight she stops. He keeps going. "Nine." Nobody told him about nine. And then, for the first time: "Alice." He is saying it when nobody has paid for it. He kisses her, or she kisses him. They will disagree about which for the rest of their lives.
16. **The hill, full light.** London comes up Tyburn hill for the man who put out the lamps: cold, frightened, some of them carrying rope. Nixon and what is left of the Guard are there. They do not stand between him and the crowd. They stand beside him. Nathaniel does not ask for anything. He starts to speak, and the first thing he says is a name.

**The Record.** His voice runs under the season's last images. It starts flat, like a ledger, and somewhere it stops being for the crowd. No score until the hymn comes in, tune only.

## What Season 2 inherits

- **A London with no light.** Coal is power now, and a nineteen-year-old owns the coal.
- **A Harding child carried by a mortal girl who used to be the White's daughter.** Both worlds have a claim.
- **Josephine in the snow** beside a son who cannot answer her, and everyone the lock ever took standing where it left them. The season's question becomes: what do you owe the dead when you know exactly where they are?
- **Three people in love and one marriage.** Tamara is Lady Harding, and her hold on the depots rests on staying so. Nixon is her husband's brother. Alice has shaken hands on it.
- **Nixon Hargrave**, captain of a Guard with no lanterns, acknowledged by one dead man and one living brother.
