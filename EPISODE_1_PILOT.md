# THE GREEN GALLOWS
## Episode 1, "The Exhibition": the editor's cut

**49 DAYS TO FIRST SNOW.** London, 2 November 1889.

---

## What this cut is built on

Five rules, each taken from one of the showrunners you named. Every change in the edit log at the end traces back to one of them.

1. **Everything is caused (Gilligan).** Nothing in the hour happens by luck. Felix isn't on Blackwood Bridge by chance: he has been tracking the Narrows job since dusk. Every object that turns up early pays off before the credits: the purse, the orange, the locket, the watch, the match, the count of eight.
2. **Every lead makes a choice that costs them (Gilligan).** Miriam skims the gold, and it kills her. Jamie goes down to the lock, and it kills him. Nathaniel stays at the party when his brother walks out, and he'll carry that all season. Alice does what she's told for once, locks the door, and loses her sister. The pilot is four choices going off at once.
3. **The world explains itself through action (DiMartino and Konietzko).** Nobody stops to explain what gallowglass is. We watch it get made (1489), sold (Jamie's speech), fail (the eastern provinces), and die (the blackout). Every lead says what they want aloud before the end of Act One, and there's a joke in every scene that isn't a funeral.
4. **Let the picture carry it (Tartakovsky).** The cold open, the break-in, the poisoning, the blackout and the river are told without dialogue. Words only come in when the picture can't do the job.
5. **One house's party, one shock to end on (Benioff).** The Exhibition does what the Winterfell feast does: every house is in one room, and every alliance, grudge and secret can be read across it. The hour ends on the one image nobody can come back from: a dead girl says her sister's name.

---

## TEASER

### 1. Tyburn, 1489. Night, no snow, but frost.

*No dialogue.*

A hundred empty gallows frames on a frozen field, and one old thief turning on his rope. *Creak. Creak.* Below the ground is a pit lined with iron, and something vast is breathing behind a lantern's glare. We never see her face. We see a hand of blue ice the size of a cartwheel, an iron hook through the palm, and we hear her singing a wordless lullaby, low and patient.

ALISTAIR HARDING kneels at the wrist. He's in his forties, a threadbare scholar's gown over a nobleman's shirt, a wolf-crest ring on his finger, and a satchel of brass instruments nobody ever paid for. He's a failed natural philosopher on the one night he stops failing. He opens the ice with a brass lancet. Her blood runs green into a lantern of his own design. *Fwhumph.* It's the first gallowglass light.

His son EDRIC, nine, holds it up for him, solemn and proud to be useful.

Behind them a tiny girl made of frost slips out of the pit. Alistair sees her, and steps aside. He has what he wanted. She runs into the white dark, and the wolves run after her. At the treeline a young woman in Harding colours, nineteen, a cousin, has seen all of it. She backs into the trees before anyone sees her face.

The singing doesn't stop. We push into the green flame of Edric's lantern until it fills the frame—

**MATCH CUT TO:** the same green flame in a street lamp, four hundred years later. A barrel organ on the corner grinds out the Lantern Hymn, and under it, for anyone who has just heard the lullaby, it's the same tune.

**TITLE CARD: 49 DAYS TO FIRST SNOW.**

## ACT ONE

### 2. The Narrows, a merchant's house. Dusk, rain.

*Almost no dialogue until Ajax.*

Rain hammers the slates. *Tak-tak-tak-tak.* One figure crosses the roofline: MIRIAM BOUNDS, 24. She's tall and long-limbed, her dark hair braided tight under a wool cap. She wears a charcoal coat with Vykra red stitched inside the collar, soft boots worn thin at the toe, and a knife flat against her spine. She has the calm face of a woman who has already counted the exits.

She goes in through the skylight. *Thud.* Then she goes down the stairs like water down a drain: an elbow, *krk*, a knee, a throat, a body folded over a banister. *Thump. Thump. Thump.* Nobody gets a shout out. She snags a thread of her red collar on the skylight frame and doesn't notice.

In the study a man waits in the dark with his back to the safe, eating an apple. *Crnch.* This is AJAX FINK, 35: good teeth, a bad smile, a camel coat too fine for the room, and his right arm cased to the elbow in black iron, all pistons and rivets.

> **AJAX:** Grise sends one girl now? I'm insulted.

The gauntlet takes the knife out of her hand, *KLANG*, and pins her to the safe by the throat. *Hssssss.* Her boots leave the floor.

> **AJAX:** Pretty. Shame.

A shape comes out of the dark behind him: ALICE BOUNDS, 20. She's small and wiry, her hair chopped short with a knife, in a borrowed coat two sizes too big, her knuckles wrapped in rag. She has wolf's eyes. Her blade opens Ajax from cheekbone to jaw. *Shhk.*

> **AJAX:** *Aaagh*—!

He drops Miriam and backs to the window, blood running through his fingers, and looks at the girl who did it, properly, for the first time.

> **AJAX:** I'll remember you.

*KSHHH.* He's through the glass and gone.

> **MIRIAM:** *(dragging in air)* What are you doing here?
> **ALICE:** Helping. He almost *had* you.
> **MIRIAM:** I had it under control.
> **ALICE:** Your feet weren't touching the floor.
> **MIRIAM:** *Ughh.*

The safe. *Click… click… clunk.* Grise's strongbox goes into the satchel. A purse of gold stamped with the Lambrick crest goes into Miriam's coat. Alice sees which pocket. We see Alice see it. She says nothing.

> **MIRIAM:** *(taking her by the ear)* Walk.
> **ALICE:** Ow, *easy*—

### 3. The Narrows, an hour later.

Constables' lanterns, guards being carried out, the rain thinning. A man steps over a body as if he's stepped over a great many: INSPECTOR FELIX HAWKE, 40. He's unshaven, in a wet brown overcoat whose buttons don't match, a bowler hat dripping, and a cold pipe he never lights. Under it all is the patience of a man who's been passed over for promotion three times and has stopped caring who notices.

> **CONSTABLE:** Two of them, sir. Girls. A tall one and a small one.
> **FELIX:** Girls did this?
> **CONSTABLE:** The small one did *that.* *(the bloodied window)*

Felix lifts a thread of red wool off the skylight frame and rolls it between his fingers.

> **FELIX:** The tall one's careful. *(pockets it)* Careful people have somewhere to be.

### 4. Harding Works, the gauntlet shop.

NATHANIEL HARDING, 21, straps on his leap-gauntlet prototype. He's lanky and ink-stained, his sleeves rolled, wearing goggles he has forgotten are pushed up on his head. He has a face that hasn't yet learned to hide how much it wants things. He fires the booster. *Kshhhh-THOOM.* He crosses the foundry in one jump and lands in the coal bin.

BYRON, 64, the butler, picks soot out of his hair. He's silver-haired and straight-backed, an old soldier's moustache, his white gloves somehow still white.

> **BYRON:** Very graceful, sir. The coal agrees.
> **NATHANIEL:** He's going to see it tonight, Byron. In front of the Crown.
> **BYRON:** Then I'll brush you twice.

Byron has been the only audience for these for years. Nathaniel says what he wants out loud, so we know it: *"He'll have to look at it."*

### 5. Harding House, the study.

ELDGARD HARDING, 58, pours two drinks. He's broad and grey and very still, in a perfectly cut black coat, with the wolf-crest ring from the cold open on his hand: four hundred years of it on one finger. JAMIE HARDING, 25, stands across the desk. He's tall and immaculate in evening black, his cravat already crooked from pulling at it, with grey eyes that haven't slept in a week.

> **ELDGARD:** After tonight your preparation begins.
> **JAMIE:** I know what the Tithe is.

Eldgard pushes a glass across. Jamie leaves it where it is. On his way out he sets his pocket watch on his father's desk, wound and ticking, like a man who won't need to know the time where he's going. Eldgard doesn't notice. Neither of them says the word that matters.

### 6. The Shadwell laundry, after.

Steam pours from the vents. *Fssshhh.* MOTHER GRISE, 70, counts Miriam's take onto a scrubbed table. She's narrow as a coat hanger, grey hair scraped back, and her hands are as soft as a clerk's. *Clink, clink, clink.* She counts it again and doesn't say what she makes it.

In the yard a laundress slaps a wet strap onto the trestle and the buckle rings on the wood. Alice is across the yard before she knows she's moved. In the doorway, Miriam looks away.

Grise comes out and looks Alice over the way a horse buyer looks at teeth.

> **GRISE:** Ajax Fink, across the face. Nobody's touched that man in years. You have talent, girl. The Vykra would receive you.

Miriam is between them before Alice can open her mouth.

> **MIRIAM:** She's not interested, Grise.
> **GRISE:** I asked her.
> **MIRIAM:** And I'm answering. Keep your greasy, slimy hands off her.

Grise smiles as if she can wait. The sisters walk out into the fog.

*No dialogue.* Grise goes back inside, takes a pair of kid gloves from a drawer and holds them out to a girl working the mangle. The girl puts them on. Stitched inside the cuff is a knot.

## ACT TWO

### 7. Harding House, the long gallery: the Exhibition photograph.

Four hundred years of Hardings in oil line the walls, and every portrait is the same picture: the eldest son holds up a brass lantern and his father's hand rests on his shoulder. Younger sons stand at the edges, half out of frame. Under them a PHOTOGRAPHER fusses beneath a black cloth, his flash-tray heaped with powdered gallowglass, "the new process."

JOSEPHINE HARDING runs the room. She's 50, in black silk the colour of a closed piano, grey-streaked hair coiled tight as rope, a jet brooch she has never explained, and kid gloves indoors, always. She stands like a woman who has sat with her back to a wall her whole life, and she moves furniture and family the same way, with two fingers.

> **JOSEPHINE:** James, at your father's left. Nathaniel, behind.
> **NATHANIEL:** Behind.
> **JOSEPHINE:** You're taller. It's composition, not a verdict.
> **JAMIE:** It's always a little bit of a verdict.
> **ELDGARD:** *(reading a card)* The Chancellor's sending three men tonight. Last year he sent flowers.
> **JOSEPHINE:** Then we give them something to write home about.
> **ELDGARD:** We give them light.
> **JAMIE:** We give them the speech.
> **ELDGARD:** *You* give them the speech.
> **JAMIE:** Alistair the misunderstood genius. Laughed at by the Crown. Iron into light. I could say it in my sleep.
> **JOSEPHINE:** Say it less like a joke.
> **JAMIE:** It isn't a joke, Mother. A joke has a point.
> **ELDGARD:** Essex was dark four hours on Tuesday.
> **JOSEPHINE:** Then tonight nobody says Essex.
> **JAMIE:** Essex.
> **ELDGARD:** James.
> **JAMIE:** Essex, Essex—
> **NATHANIEL:** *(trying not to laugh)* He's nervous.
> **JAMIE:** I'm *thorough.*

Josephine hands Jamie the brass lantern from its stand, the one every heir on the walls is holding.

> **JOSEPHINE:** Up.
> **JAMIE:** Does it come with instructions?
> **ELDGARD:** Hold it up.

Eldgard's hand settles on Jamie's shoulder, exactly where every painted father's hand rests behind them. Nathaniel, behind, looks for somewhere to put his hands and finds nowhere. Josephine straightens Jamie's collar, and her glove catches a fine silver chain at his throat.

> **JOSEPHINE:** What's this?
> **JAMIE:** A chain.
> **JOSEPHINE:** I can see it's a chain.
> **JAMIE:** Then we agree on something.

She lets it go. She doesn't forget it.

> **PHOTOGRAPHER:** If the family could hold very still. The new powder wants a long exposure.
> **JOSEPHINE:** How long?
> **PHOTOGRAPHER:** A count of eight, madam.

*Fssshhhhh.* The powder catches and burns green, and the gallery goes the colour of deep water. Four Hardings hold four smiles.

*One.* Nobody blinks. *Two.* Eldgard's smile doesn't reach anything. *Three.* Josephine's gloved hand tightens on Jamie's sleeve. *Four.* In the stillness, faint, somewhere in the bones of the house, Nathaniel hears a woman crying. *Five.* His eyes move. Nobody else's do. *Six.* The lantern in Jamie's hand trembles, very slightly, and Jamie holds it higher. *Seven.* The crying is under the floor now, under their feet. *Eight.* It stops.

> **PHOTOGRAPHER:** *(emerging pink from the cloth)* Lovely. Very natural.

Eldgard is already walking. *"Nathaniel, your toy—" "It isn't a toy, it's a—" "After."* Josephine is already walking too. Jamie puts the lantern back on its stand like something that burns.

### 8. INTERCUT: Nathaniel's dressing room / the Bounds room on Gin Lane. Evening.

Two older siblings get ready to leave and don't say so. We cut on the rhythm of their hands.

**HARDING HOUSE.** Nathaniel is at the mirror, losing a fight with a cravat. The gauntlet schematic lies folded on the dresser like a love letter. Jamie comes in, takes the cravat out of his hands and ties it for him, the way he has since Nathaniel was six.

**GIN LANE.** One room above a chandler's, warm and cheap and theirs: a cot, a cracked mirror, a kettle ticking on the stove, *tk-tk-tk*. Through the window, children skip rope in the lane: *"Green light, green light, who's in the cellar tonight?"* Miriam does her hair at the mirror, slowly, with more care than she gives anything. Alice is on the cot, pressing a rag to a split knuckle.

> **JAMIE:** You're showing him tonight.
> **NATHANIEL:** If he asks.
> **JAMIE:** He won't ask. Show him anyway, in front of everyone. He can't say no in front of everyone.

> **MIRIAM:** Whatever Grise offers you, you say no.
> **ALICE:** She said I had talent.
> **MIRIAM:** She says that to every knife she wants to own. This life brings nothing but misery, Ali. Once you're in, you don't come out.
> **ALICE:** You're in.
> **MIRIAM:** *(to the mirror)* That's why I'm asking.

She pins her hair, takes it down, and pins it again.

> **JAMIE:** Nat. If you could leave, would you?
> **NATHANIEL:** Leave what? The work's here.

> **ALICE:** That's three times.
> **MIRIAM:** Twice.
> **ALICE:** Who is he?
> **MIRIAM:** Nobody.
> **ALICE:** Nobody gets his hair done three times.
> **MIRIAM:** *(a small smile)* It isn't real until it's mine.

Jamie looks at his brother a long moment: at the schematic, at the boy who has spent his whole life trying to be useful to their father. Whatever he came to say, he doesn't say it. He pulls him into a hug that goes on a beat too long.

> **NATHANIEL:** What's that for?
> **JAMIE:** Your cravat was crooked.

> **ALICE:** *(to her hands)* Do you know what today is?

Miriam stops.

> **MIRIAM:** …Oh. Ali.

She digs in her coat and tosses her an orange, stolen off a cart on the way home. It's the only present there is.

> **MIRIAM:** Happy birthday. I'll make it up to you. Tomorrow. I swear it.
> **ALICE:** Tomorrow.

For one unguarded second we see it on Alice's face. It isn't anger. It's yearning. She wants what Miriam has: someone to get ready for.

Both older siblings reach their doors on the same beat.

> **JAMIE:** *(without turning)* The work isn't worth you. Remember I said it.
> **MIRIAM:** *(without turning)* Lock the door.

Two doors close. *Clk. Clk.* Neither younger sibling knows they've just been said goodbye to.

### 9. The steamship office, Wapping. Night.

*No dialogue.*

A clerk stamps three tickets for Southampton, paid for in Lambrick gold. At the next desk sits the girl from the mangle, gloved and smiling. She raises a glass to the bride-to-be and pours her one. Miriam never drinks with strangers. Tonight she's happy enough to smile and drink.

Across the street, under the awning of a closed chemist's, Felix Hawke watches a charcoal coat with red stitched inside the collar come out of the office. He knocks out his cold pipe and follows.

### 10. A coach on the Blackwood Bridge stair.

Rain drums the roof. Miriam climbs in, and Jamie takes her face in both hands before she can sit. We know what's in her glass; she doesn't. The scene plays like a love scene, and the audience watches it as a death.

> **JAMIE:** Tonight's the night everything changes for us.

He presses a velvet box into her palm. Inside are two silver hearts set with halves of one piece of green glass, a Harding heirloom.

> **JAMIE:** Wherever one is, the other finds it.

He fastens one round her neck and one round his own. *Click. Click.* It's the chain Josephine's glove caught. She shows him what's in her red mitten: three tickets.

> **JAMIE:** Three?
> **MIRIAM:** I'm not going without her.
> **JAMIE:** You were meant to wait for me.
> **MIRIAM:** I don't wait.

He kisses her, then steps out into the rain toward Harding House, lit green on the hill. He has one last thing to do. She has till midnight.

## ACT THREE

### 11. Harding House, the ballroom: the Exhibition.

Two hundred guests under a thousand green chandeliers, and the Crown's men among them. The light has been failing across the eastern provinces, a street at a time and an hour at a time, and the papers have started saying *the end of the green*. Tonight the Hardings answer the rumour with a party. Under the music: *Essex dark on Tuesday. Kent on Thursday.*

LORD HECTOR LAMBRICK, 55, the coal baron, arrives. He's heavy and pink, with soot in the creases of his knuckles that no valet has ever got out, a diamond pin like a wet eye, and the friendliest smile in London. With him is his daughter TAMARA, 19: sharp-chinned and quick-eyed, in grey silk two seasons out of fashion on purpose, with a cipher wheel hidden inside her fan. Their fathers have promised her to Nathaniel in a private letter that nobody will announce tonight: coal and light, an alliance.

> **TAMARA:** I'm told we're to be very polite to each other.
> **NATHANIEL:** For how long?
> **TAMARA:** Forty years, I think.
> **NATHANIEL:** I can manage polite.
> **TAMARA:** *(reading his soot-blackened cuff)* Can you manage clean?

Lambrick finds Eldgard by a chandelier and admires it like a man pricing a horse.

> **LAMBRICK:** Lovely light, Harding. Essex had none of it Tuesday.
> **ELDGARD:** A fault in the line.
> **LAMBRICK:** My grandfather kept a lawn at Lambrick, the greenest in the county. He used to say grass only stays green while somebody keeps paying to water it. *(lifts his glass to the green)* It won't stay green for ever, Eldgard. Nothing does. Ask your Alistair.

Eldgard smiles for the room and doesn't answer.

### 12. The ballroom: the speech.

Eldgard takes the gallery stair, and the room goes quiet. He promises new lines to the eastern provinces, a new Works, light for another hundred years.

> **ELDGARD:** My son and heir. James.

Jamie steps up and says what he's supposed to say, the story every Harding child learns before the alphabet:

> **JAMIE:** Four hundred years ago, our ancestor Alistair Harding, a natural philosopher the Crown laughed at, found that the iron under Tyburn could be grown into light. He gave London the first lantern. Every lamp in this room is his gift, and our promise.

The audience saw the cold open; the room didn't. The applause is the first lie of the series, and we're the only ones who know it. Jamie smiles into it like a man swallowing glass. Only Nathaniel notices that his brother doesn't look at their father once.

Then, on his brother's advice, Nathaniel steps forward in front of everyone with the gauntlet strapped to his arm.

> **ELDGARD:** *(smiling for the room)* Not tonight. Toys after supper.

Laughter. On Nathaniel's face we watch his brother's advice turn into a humiliation.

From the gallery, Josephine watches her elder son, whose hand keeps going to his throat.

> **JOSEPHINE:** That boy is planning something.
> **BYRON:** Then he takes after you, madam.

One look between them says they share thirty years of history and agreed never to mention it.

Across the floor, Jamie slips out through a service door. Nathaniel sees him go and takes a step after him. His father's hand closes on his arm.

> **ELDGARD:** The Chancellor's men, Nathaniel.

Nathaniel stays. It's the choice he'll pay for all season: he stayed to be useful.

### 13. The Bounds room, Gin Lane.

*No dialogue.*

Alice has her coat on and her hand on the bolt. She could follow Miriam. She did this afternoon, and she saved her life. The orange sits on the sill. She looks at it for a long time. Then she takes her coat off, slides the bolt home and sits down to wait. For once in her life she does as she's told.

### 14. The Works core, deep below.

Jamie goes down with a key cut from Byron's old Guard key. We glimpse the chained thing only as moving green light, and we hear a woman weeping: the lullaby from the cold open, broken. He saws at the iron lock with a blade of crystal until it sticks fast, then presses his bleeding palm to it. The chain shudders.

In the shudder, a small girl made of frost comes up out of the dark below the chain, and the first warm thing she touches is the locket at his throat. She rips it loose, with his blood still on it. It flares green and burns out in her fist, and she's gone, pulled across the city toward its twin.

## ACT FOUR: the blackout

*Played as one cross-cut sequence. The cuts get shorter and shorter until there's almost no gap between them.*

### 15. London, the wave.

*No dialogue.* Street by street the green dies across the city, a wave of dark rolling out from Tyburn: a tram stalls on its rails, a pub sign goes out, a hospital window goes black, a child on Gin Lane looks up at the lamp she was skipping under.

### 16. The ballroom, in the dark.

Every chandelier gutters out. Two hundred guests shout for lamps. Nathaniel stands very still in the black, because he can hear a woman crying inside the walls. Nobody else can. He turns to say so, and his father is already gone.

### 17. The Bounds room, in the dark.

The green dies in the window. Alice strikes a match. *Fssst.* She watches the stair through the crack in the door, waiting for footsteps that don't come. The match burns down to her fingers and she doesn't flinch. *Fff.*

### 18. The Works.

The alarm sounds: a saboteur in the core. A Lantern Guard squad comes down the gantries, and with them a Wick, COLE PRUETT, 17, all elbows, his uniform too big for him, trying very hard not to be sick. Flash-bursts strobe the dark, *fzzt, fzzt*, freezing it into snapshots: pipes, steam, a masked figure at the lock. "Step away!" The figure turns with something glinting in his hand. A lantern rifle fires out of the strobe. *KRAK.* We never see whose. The figure drops.

### 19. Blackwood Bridge, midnight.

Miriam waits at the rail in her red wool mittens, three tickets in one of them. Jamie doesn't come. Her lips are blue and her hands won't stop shaking. The taste on her tongue is crushed glass and violets, and the frost on her lips isn't the night's. Every Vykra girl knows that taste. She understands whose glass it was, and why.

At the foot of the bridge stair Felix comes out of the fog, one step too late. Her legs go at the rail and she goes over. *SPLSSSH.*

> **FELIX:** *No*—

He drops his coat and goes in after her.

### 20. The core.

The shooter pulls off the mask. It's Jamie, alive and bleeding. ELDGARD steps out of the dark.

> **ELDGARD:** Go. Wait at the gate. Speak to no one.

The shooter goes. On the gantry above, forgotten, Cole watches the man on the floor lift his head and say something to his father. Eldgard kneels beside his son, and his hand moves toward the lock. *Cut away.*

### 21. Under the Thames.

*No dialogue.*

Black water, and cold. Miriam sinks, her hair spreading like ink, the locket at her throat glowing faint green. A streak of green cuts down through the water toward her: the frost girl, the burned-out twin in her fist. The two hearts find each other, *tink*, and lock. Miriam, drowning, looks into the small frost face, and her lips move on something we can't hear. The girl nods, and pours into her eyes and mouth.

Felix's hand closes on her collar and hauls her up.

### 22. The riverbank.

Felix kneels in the mud with two fingers at her throat. Rain falls on her open eyes.

> **FELIX:** *(quietly)* Nothing.

He closes her eyes. At her throat the two hearts lie locked together, gone dull.

## TAG

### 23. Harding House, grey dawn, then the stables.

The lights are back, every one. Eldgard tells Josephine and Nathaniel there was an accident in the core. Josephine looks at her husband a beat too long. Nathaniel hears the weeping again, and this time it's in the floor. He makes it as far as the stables before he breaks.

Byron finds him there and sits down in the straw beside him.

> **NATHANIEL:** I saw him go. I stayed for the Chancellor's men.

Byron says nothing, which is what he has always been best at, and puts his white-gloved hand on the back of the boy's neck.

### 24. The city morgue, dawn.

The green lamps sputter. *Bzzt… bzzt.* Alice comes through the doors at a dead run, coat open, breath tearing out of her. Felix, still damp, draws back the sheet.

Alice doesn't scream. She sinks to her knees, takes her sister's cold hand and presses it to her forehead.

> **ALICE:** You said tomorrow. You *promised* tomorrow.

Her shoulders shake without a sound. Felix looks away.

Under her breath the frost on her sister's lips melts. *Drip. Drip.* The dead woman's eyes open and move to Alice.

> **MIRIAM'S BODY:** Ali.

**CUT TO BLACK.**

## The edit log: what changed, and why

| # | What changed | Before | After | Why (the rule) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Cold open is wordless and ends on a match cut** | 1489, then a hard cut to 1889 | The green flame in Edric's lantern becomes a London street lamp, and the lullaby becomes the Lantern Hymn on a barrel organ | Lets the picture do it. The season's central secret (the city's light is her blood, the city's hymn is her lullaby) is planted in one cut, without a word. |
| 2 | **Felix is tracking the job, not passing by** | Felix happened to be on the far bank | He picks up the Narrows trail, pockets a red thread, and follows the charcoal coat from the steamship office to the bridge | Nothing by luck. Felix arrives because of something Miriam did, and the thread pays off at the bridge. |
| 3 | **The two goodbyes are intercut** | Separate scenes for the brothers and the sisters | One cross-cut scene: the cravat against the hair pinned three times, ending on "The work isn't worth you" and "Lock the door" on the same beat | Shows the pilot's structure in one scene: two houses, two older siblings, the same goodbye. Editing does the work dialogue would otherwise have to. |
| 4 | **Grise's gloves** | The poisoner appears only at the office | A silent beat at the laundry: Grise hands the knotted gloves to the girl at the mangle | Suspense: the audience knows about the poison before Miriam does, so the coach scene plays as a death and not a romance. |
| 5 | **Order: poison, then coach** | Kept, but now with the tension spelled out | The coach scene follows directly after the glass | Same principle. Put the danger on screen, then make the audience watch the love scene knowing it. |
| 6 | **"Three?" "I'm not going without her."** | Three tickets shown, never explained | Jamie asks; Miriam answers | Puts her want on screen in the pilot. It's also the first payoff of "coming back for Alice," with the full weight saved for Ep 7. |
| 7 | **Nathaniel stays** | Only Nathaniel sees Jamie leave | He takes a step after him; Eldgard stops him with "The Chancellor's men" | Every lead chooses and pays. His guilt ("I stayed for the Chancellor's men") becomes his engine for the season. |
| 8 | **Alice locks the door** | Alice waits at home | Coat on, hand on the bolt, the orange on the sill; she takes the coat off and obeys | Her tragedy: she disobeyed and saved her sister at dusk, obeyed at night, and lost her. It's the root of her lie that love means holding on. |
| 9 | **The blackout is one sequence** | Separate numbered scenes | Six threads cross-cut with shrinking gaps (the wave, ballroom, match, raid, bridge, core) into the river | A wordless action climax. Every plan in the episode goes off at once. |
