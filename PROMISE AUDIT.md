# The Green Gallows: Promise Ledger Audit

Audited: `FULL NEW SEASON.md` after the clox pass (Oct 14, 2026).

**Numbering.** I use the document's own numbering. **5.16** means Episode 5, scene 16. **CO** is a cold open. **Rec.** is the Record montage (10.20). **Fin.** is the final scene (10.21). "pre" means a character knew before the series begins.

**Verdict.** This season keeps its contract. The pilot promises a city warmed by someone in the dark, a family that spends its children, and two sisters split by the only thing strong enough to split them, and the finale pays all three on screen, at a cost. The ledger is unusually honest: most of its 107 rows hold up when checked against the scenes.

The problems are concentrated in three places:

1. **The engine is a mystery when it should be a bomb.** Every episode opens on *49 DAYS TO FIRST SNOW*, but until 5.16 the audience doesn't know what first snow costs or whom. For four episodes the clock ticks with no stakes attached. Hitchcock would give the audience the bomb in Ep 2 and let Eldgard groom his son on top of it.
2. **Three finale mechanics are explained only at the moment they're used:**
   - the wolf-crest ring opening the lock;
   - Morgana drawing the White out of her daughter;
   - the overload Alice runs on her gauntlet.

   A fourth, the Vykra challenge law, has the same problem at 9.6. Each needs one small demonstration nobody comments on, placed episodes earlier.
3. **Two lines contradict their own payoffs.** At 5.16 Eldgard says the White "takes one of us," but the finale runs on Morgana's rule that a Harding must be *given* and that she won't *take* one. And the finale never says why Nathaniel must open the lock rather than let midnight pass.

Every fix below is a plant inside an existing scene. Nothing new is invented.

### (a) Suspense missed

- **Nathaniel's fate, Ep 2–4.** This is the big one. The audience learns at 5.16, the same moment he does, that the clock is counting down to him, so 3.2 ("his first real job") and 4.10 ("I'll take you into the work") play as warm father–son beats. If the audience knows in Ep 2, both become the most frightening scenes in the first half: a father grooming the son he's going to spend. Tell them at **2.9**, in a scene that's about something else (→ #1).
- **The early snow.** At the Ep 8 tag it's pure surprise. Plant a few flakes in the core when Morgana screams at **5.15**. Then the audience spends 8.20, the Lost One offering herself, dreading the scream they know is coming (→ #5).
- **The ring's power.** Once the audience has seen it work (→ #2), 8.14 ("they leave the wolf-crest ring on his hand") stops being trivia and becomes a bomb: the marshals just left the key on the prisoner.

### (b) Surprises that earn it (keep them hidden)

All of these survive a rewatch:
- **"You're not her"** (7.3): the clue is the apology being too perfect.
- **The coffin of slag** (7.14): Byron's shaking hands (2.3), and the coffin dead still on the steps (2.7).
- **Eldgard on the lock** (9.14): the cut-away (1.16), the watch (2.13), "I finished it" (7.15).
- **Nixon's round wasn't fatal**: "still talking" in Cole's diary (2.4), and "Yes" (7.7).
- **Morgana refuses the door** (10.2): 8.19 predicts it.
- **Josephine goes into the White**: "You would" (8.19), and "a woman who has already decided something" (10.11).
- **The heartbeat** (Rec.): Wren's drowned boy (2.2), 2.16, 7.CO, 9.5.

Fair play throughout.

### (c) Dramatic irony left on the table

- **3.2 and 4.10**, once #1 is in: no rewrite needed. The scenes already play it, if the audience knows.
- **3.2 and 3.5: Cole and Nathaniel.** Cole saw the shot and the living man on the floor. Nathaniel fits the Wicks' new rigs. One beat: Nathaniel buckles Cole's booster himself, and Cole can't look at him. It costs nothing and loads Cole's diary for four episodes.
- **8.14: the ring.** Plays only after #2.
- **Nathaniel never knows about the child.** It's deliberately held for Season 2. Keep it.

### (d) Redundant reveals

- **9.15 against 10.CO.** Eldgard says "Free her, and pay in light. Alistair chose sons" eight minutes before the Ep 10 cold open dramatizes Morgana saying it to Alistair. The bargain lands twice and the cold open loses its reveal. Cut the words from 9.15 and keep "There was always a way out," so Ep 10 opens on the bargain fresh (→ #12).
- Nothing else repeats without adding something. "He is cold" recurs five times, but each time carries new evidence or a new argument. Keep it.

### #1: Give the audience the bomb in Ep 2

- **Location in Script:** 2.9 (Shadwell laundry), with ripples into 2.14, 3.2, 4.10 and 5.16.
- **Current Friction:** The clock card counts down for four episodes toward something the audience can't name. 3.2 and 4.10 play as warmth.
- **The Fix:** In 2.9, Josephine pays Grise double. Replace "His father will use him" with: "His father has one son left to spend, and the snow is coming. Keep him out of the Works." Grise doesn't ask what that means, and neither does the scene. Then 2.14 runs as written: Eldgard counts the frayed links ("Forty-seven"), now under a card that says *47 DAYS*.
- **The Mechanical Ripple Effect:**
  - 3.2: Eldgard handing Nathaniel "his first real job" becomes grooming in front of a bomb.
  - 4.10: "I'll take you into the work" becomes the scariest line in Ep 4.
  - 5.16: the reveal becomes Nathaniel's discovery, not the audience's, and the twist that stays a twist is the light being a woman.
  - Every clock card from Ep 2 on now ticks for a person.

### #2: Show the ring work before anyone explains it

- **Location in Script:** 5.16 (the core), with ripples into 2.14, 8.14, 9.2, 9.15 and 10.5.

- **The Fix:** At 5.16, when Eldgard steps out of the dark, the chain is still straining from Nathaniel's touch. Eldgard lays his ringed hand flat on the lock. Nobody looks at the ring. Optional echo at 2.14: he counts the frayed links with that same hand.
- **The Mechanical Ripple Effect:**
  - 8.14: "They leave the wolf-crest ring on his hand" now lands as dread: the marshals just left the key on the prisoner.
  - 9.2 and 9.8: "I came for your ring" doesn't need explaining.
  - 10.5: inevitable.
  - 10.CO: Alistair sliding the ring on confirms what we already saw.
- **Effort / Impact:** Low / **High**

### #3: Make the price "given, never taken," and say the midnight logic once

  - 10.16: "It has to be given" becomes recognition, not a new rule.
  - 10.17: Josephine's walk becomes the rule kept, in full view.
  - 10.5: Nathaniel's opening of the lock becomes a race against a willing man.

### #4: Morgana's gift, planted through the glass, and moved to her exit

- **Location in Script:** 9.10 (the core) and 10.16–17.
- **Current Friction:** The power that makes the Lost One mortal and restarts the heartbeat is invented at the moment it's used, in the most crowded scene of the season.
- **The Fix:**
  - At 9.10, through the cracking Heartglass, Morgana presses her palm to the glass over her daughter's chest. The crystal on the girl's skin pulls back a finger's width, like frost off a warm pane. The Lost One says, surprised, "Ali's birthday is the second of November," a memory back for one breath. Morgana takes her hand away, because every bit of warmth she gives makes the trade hum louder. Nobody explains it.
  - At 10.16, remove the drawing-out. The scene becomes the ask, the staying and the price.
  - In 10.17, after Josephine takes Morgana's hand, Morgana stops beside her daughter on the way into the white. She lays her hand flat on the girl's chest and draws the White out of her "the way you'd draw a splinter," and goes.
- **The Mechanical Ripple Effect:**
  - The gift becomes Morgana's goodbye, given once the debt is paid, rather than a bargain chip spent before it.
  - 10.16 breathes.
  - The heartbeat in Rec. becomes the consequence of something the audience has seen work.
  - The birthday line at 9.10 rhymes with 1.2, 3.3 and 6.4.

## One question that changes a fix

**Do you want the audience ahead of Nathaniel on the Tithe from Ep 2 (#1)?** It trades a midseason surprise (that Nathaniel is the next to be spent) for three episodes of suspense. My preferred answer is yes. The midseason still has its true twist, the light is a woman, and the Tithe's mechanism and Nathaniel's own discovery still land at 5.16. If you'd rather keep the surprise, the fallback is the frayed-links count at 2.14 alone, which gives the clock a body without naming whose.

## The Top Five

1. **Josephine tells Grise at 2.9 that "his father has one son left to spend, and the snow is coming,"** so every clock card from Ep 2 ticks for Nathaniel.
2. **At 5.16 Eldgard's ringed hand settles the chain like a hand on a bell,** so the ring opening the lock at 10.5 is inevitable instead of explained.
3. **At 5.16 the price becomes "given, never taken," and at 10.4 Lambrick says the midnight logic out loud,** so the finale runs on rules the audience already holds.
4. **At 9.10 Morgana draws the cold a finger's width out of her daughter through the glass, and her full gift moves to her exit,** so the Lost One's mortality is earned and 10.16 breathes.
5. **At 5.15 snow falls in the core from no sky when Morgana screams,** so the early snow is the bomb going off and the Ep 8 trade plays as suspense.

## The Season's Contract

The pilot promises that London's light is paid for by someone in the dark, that one family keeps spending its children to keep it lit, and that two sisters will be split by the only thing strong enough to do it. The finale pays all three on screen and at full price: the light goes out by choice, the last Harding spent is the one who chose the name, and the sisters are split and rejoined by a stranger keeping a dead woman's promise. It keeps the contract, but three of its finale mechanics are explained at the point of use: the ring, the White-drawing and the price. Plant them where the audience can see them, and the whole finale clicks into place.
